You have two parents, four grandparents and eight great-grandparents. Go back ten generations and your family tree already has 1,024 places for ancestors. Each of them is someone who lived, loved, worked and passed life on, or you would not be here. Genealogy is the attempt to get to know at least some of these people. And its history is almost as old as writing.

## The longest family tree in the world

Guinness World Records names Confucius's family as the one whose line can be traced back furthest. The philosopher's ancestor Kung Chia is documented in the 8th century BC, and the recorded lineage spans 86 generations. For thousands of years, knowing your ancestors was the business of ruling houses, priests and the powerful: it proved a right to land, a title or power.

## The day ordinary people's ancestors went on paper

For most of us, everything changed in the 16th century. In 1563 the Council of Trent ordered parishes to record baptisms and marriages. Fifty-one years later the *Roman Ritual* added deaths. In the Polish lands, synods introduced the duty, and the historian Marian Butkiewicz writes that by the early 17th century most Polish parishes kept registers.

It was a revolution, though nobody thought of it that way. For the first time, the name of a peasant, a craftsman or a maid went into the book just like the name of the lord of the manor. For more than three centuries, Butkiewicz writes, the parish office did the job of today's civil registry. These are the books genealogists read today.

## Napoleon and the civil registers

The next turning point came with Napoleon. The constitution given to the Duchy of Warsaw in 1807 made the Napoleonic Code its civil law. With it came civil registration, and because there were not enough officials, much of it was entrusted to parish priests. That is why many 19th-century civil records were written by priests acting as officers of the state.

## Maths that cannot add up

Back to counting. Twenty generations back, your tree has more than a million places. Thirty generations back, more than a billion. There were not that many people alive then.

The answer is simple and beautiful: the same people appear in your tree many times, in different branches. The geneticists Peter Ralph and Graham Coop write that any two people in Europe share more ancestors than the continent's population at the time. Go back about a thousand years and both have as ancestors all the Europeans of that time who left descendants, each of them many times over.

## We are all cousins

In 2004 the researchers Douglas Rohde, Steve Olson and Joseph Chang built a model of the history of all humanity. The result surprised even them: the most recent common ancestor of everyone alive today may have lived just a few thousand years ago, about three thousand years ago in the more realistic versions of the model. "While we may not all be 'brothers,' the models suggest we are all hundredth cousins or so," Chang said.

Next time you meet a stranger, think about it: somewhere far back in the past, your family trees meet.

## Ancestors and DNA

There is also a fact that surprises many fans of genetic tests. An ancestor in your tree and an ancestor you carry DNA from are not the same thing. In every generation the genes are shuffled again, and some lines simply disappear. Ralph and Coop write that people whose common ancestor lived more than eight generations ago usually have not inherited any genetic material in common from that ancestor.

That is why a DNA test does not replace documents. The furthest you can reach is with both: genes that point to relatives, and records that tell you who your ancestors were.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many ancestors do I have ten generations back?

Your tree has 1,024 places. In practice there are often fewer different people, because distant relatives married each other and the same person can fill several places.

### Since when have parish registers been kept in Poland?

The Council of Trent ordered baptisms and marriages to be recorded in 1563, and by the early 17th century most Polish parishes kept registers. Whether a parish's books survive depends on its history, so in many places the oldest surviving books are later.

### Will a DNA test show all my ancestors?

No. After a few generations you inherit no DNA at all from many of your ancestors. A test helps you find living relatives, but a family tree is built from documents.

### Are all people related to each other?

According to the model by Rohde, Olson and Chang, everyone alive today shares ancestors from just a few thousand years ago. In that sense, yes: each of us is a distant cousin of everyone else.
