## The short answer

Very little surname-meaning evidence is cleared for text reuse in a paid product. "Open access" here usually
means free to read, not free to reuse.

Of 100 papers on Polish surnames, classified by the licence in their Crossref metadata:

| Crossref licence | Papers | What Name Atlas may do |
|---|---:|---|
| CC BY or CC BY-SA (commercial reuse allowed) | 12 | Reuse text with attribution; BY-SA adaptations must be shared alike |
| Non-commercial (any NC) | 12 | No text reuse |
| No derivatives (ND, not NC) | 6 | Quote only; no adapted text |
| No licence in metadata | 70 | Treat as all rights reserved |

For every paper, whatever its licence: a fact can be restated in our own words with a citation, and a short
quotation is possible within Polish statutory quotation rights. The licence only decides whether text may be
copied or adapted beyond that.

By topic, the 12 commercially reusable papers are: one comparative etymology paper (Polish vs Lithuanian
surnames); five regional or historical surname studies (Wysokie Mazowieckie, Daleszyce, Reszel, the Poletyło
family, the Lithuanian minority); one on the phonology of German-origin surnames; two older papers on `-kiewicz`
surnames and on women's surname forms; one on given names; and two forest-history papers that are mainly about
place names. There is no general etymological coverage anywhere in the reusable set.

## Per-paper check procedure (before any text reuse)

1. Read the Crossref licence field, including version and start date.
2. Read the article's landing page (the `DC.Rights` tag and the visible licence block). It often differs from
   Crossref.
3. Read the licence statement inside the PDF, if there is one.
4. Check the journal policy's start date against the paper's year and volume.
5. Save each page with URL, fetch time and SHA-256.
6. Allow text reuse only if every source agrees on CC BY, CC BY-SA or CC0. Otherwise store only the claim in
   our own words, with its citation.
