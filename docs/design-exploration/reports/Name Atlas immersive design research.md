# Let the evidence draw the name

The research points to one direction that is both new and safe: **evidence-gated emergence**, in which nothing on screen grows, sharpens or settles unless something real caused it (a keystroke, a genuine lookup step, a dated register count, a source arriving) and the surname's own letters and diacritics are the only generative material. Award juries since 2022 have rewarded exactly this pattern of a process made visible, and heritage subjects have won at the very top, yet the research found no award-recognised immersive surname experience, while the incumbents share one generic template (heat-map, one-line meaning, upsell) and routinely attach religion, ancestry and crests to a surname. That gap turns Name Atlas's trust rules from a brake into raw material: six confidence statuses, named kinds of absence, competing hypotheses kept sharp and side by side, and PESEL's separate men's and women's lists form a visual language that competitors cannot copy without abandoning their business model, which is exactly the "proprietary effect" 2026 trend reports prescribe against AI-driven sameness. Polish visual culture supplies the accent through method rather than motif (Opałka's counting, Abakanowicz's crowds in which no two figures are identical, Rybczyński's loops that never merge, Strzemiński's afterimages, a written doctrine for Polish diacritics), while folk ornament, heraldry, sepia and trauma imagery fail on taste and on the rules at once. People will share the result when every screenshot carries its own list, date and status, and when the shareable art is a portrait of the letters, never of the family. None of this needs WebGPU: a server-rendered answer, CSS-native motion, a static SVG "poster" computed from the surname and a WebGL 2 layer budgeted at about 60 KB gzipped all run in 2026 mobile browsers, with one seed driving page, animation and share image alike. The open risk is human rather than technical, because none of these encodings, metaphors or share formats has yet been tested with Polish or diaspora users.

## Surname incumbents overclaim while heritage craft wins top awards

Every surname product examined serves the same page: a count and rank, a red-to-yellow heat-map, a one-line meaning and an upsell. The overclaiming is systematic. Forebears reports **268,732 Nowaks in Poland**. It mixes dictionary etymologies with user submissions, which it solicits with "a Chance To Win a $60 Genealogy DNA Test", and infers religion and politics from the name: "predominantly Catholic (43%) in Ukraine", "1.87% more likely to be registered with the Democratic Party" ([Forebears](https://forebears.io/surnames/nowak)). Ancestry asks "What did your Nowak ancestors do for a living?" beside an "Ethnicity Percentage Map" ([Ancestry](https://www.ancestry.com/name-origin?surname=nowak)). House of Names sells crest downloads while stating flatly that "the surname Nowak was first found in Bohemia and Moravia" ([House of Names](https://www.houseofnames.com/nowak-family-crest)).

The Polish sites are thinner rather than better. nazwiska-polskie.pl gives **about 170,000** Nowaks with no methodology and no data date ([nazwiska-polskie.pl](https://nazwiska-polskie.pl/Nowak)), and moikrewni.pl now redirects to MyHeritage ([moikrewni.pl](https://www.moikrewni.pl/)). FamilySearch's server-rendered page tells crawlers and readers without JavaScript "We found 0 records for the Nowak last name", which is absence shown as zero in its purest form ([FamilySearch](https://www.familysearch.org/en/surname?surname=nowak)). None of these sites offers a dated official count, diacritic-exact identity, a dignified "no evidence" state, competing origins side by side, or the primary record behind a historical claim. Even Geneteka, which indexes **68,075,297 records**, needs an "exact search" checkbox because its default matching merges spellings ([Geneteka](https://geneteka.genealodzy.pl/)).

At the craft end of the web, heritage already wins top awards. Getty's Persepolis Reimagined took FWA of the Year and People's Choice 2022, plus Awwwards Developer Site of the Year ([FWA](https://thefwa.com/news/fwa-of-the-year-2022-peoples-choice-award-winners-announced); [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/persepolis-reimagined)). Getty's Frank Gehry project won two Webbys in 2024 ([Webby](https://winners.webbyawards.com/2024/websites-and-mobile-sites/features-design/best-visual-design-aesthetic/279895/sculpting-harmony)), and the National Gallery's Imaginarium won the 2026 Cultural Institutions Webby ([Webby](https://winners.webbyawards.com/2026/websites-and-mobile-sites/general-desktop-mobile-sites/cultural-institutions/380455/national-gallery-imaginarium)).

The closest model for how Name Atlas handles certainty is Google's Language Explorer. It won the 2026 Webby for Responsible AI while stating inside its own interface that "all attempts to represent languages geographically are problematic" ([Language Explorer](https://sites.research.google/languages/language-explorer/); [Webby](https://winners.webbyawards.com/2026/websites-and-mobile-sites/responsible-technology/responsible-ai/364609/language-explorer)). The Polish reference point is the Warsaw studio Huncwot, whose *Where is Poland?* tells the story of the partitions in nine chapters built from archival material held by the National Museum in Warsaw, the Library of Congress and Smithsonian Folkways ([Where is Poland?](https://whereispoland.com/en)).

By contrast, no award-recognised immersive surname or genealogy experience from 2022–2026 turned up. None of the Information is Beautiful "People, Language & Identity" winners of 2022–2024 is about surnames ([IIB 2022](https://www.informationisbeautifulawards.com/news/593-information-is-beautiful-awards-2022-the-winners); [IIB 2023](https://www.informationisbeautifulawards.com/news/636-information-is-beautiful-awards-2023-the-winners); [IIB 2024](https://informationisbeautifulawards.com/news/680-announcing-the-2024-winners)). This finding needs the product's own caveat: the searches were cut short when a shared search budget ran out. The niche is unclaimed as far as the research could see, which is not the same as provably empty.

The award web cannot simply be copied, because it has its own sameness and its own blind spot. Figma's 2026 list already counts 3D, immersive elements and dark mode as mainstream ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/web-design-trends/)). The 2025 Site of the Year, Lando Norris, uses #D2FF00 on #111112, and Hatom leans on #C1FF12 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lando-norris); [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/hatom)): acid green on near-black is hardening into a period style. Webflow warns that "visual homogeneity becomes a real risk" and calls for proprietary effects that nobody can "replicate with a prompt" ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/web-design-trends-2026)). Recent Information is Beautiful winners turned toward physical craft instead: data quilting, beads and "tangible fragments" ([IIB 2024](https://informationisbeautifulawards.com/news/680-announcing-the-2024-winners)).

Juries also score motion far above accessibility. KPR, Site of the Year 2022, earned 9.6 for animations and **6.0 for accessibility** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/kpr)). ZURB names "motion treated as decoration" and interactions that only work with a mouse cursor as failures ([ZURB Radar](https://radar.zurb.com/article/your-award-winning-site-might-be-locking-people-out)). For this research, the shipped code of 19 award sites was text-searched for reduced-motion handling, and only six contained it. Several shipped 3–6.5 MB of uncompressed HTML, CSS and JavaScript before any 3D asset loaded, led by [Lando Norris](https://landonorris.com/) and the [David Whyte Experience](https://davidwhyte.com/experience/).

Name Atlas should borrow the award web's craft vocabulary (growth, assembly, staged reveal, the held gesture) and leave its look behind: neon on near-black, blobs and globes, hijacked scrolling, reveals that need a mouse cursor, and 0–100% preloaders. The brief has already ruled out a warm-paper editorial atlas, so the obvious "heritage" alternative is closed as well. The direction has to come from the data itself.

## Emergence must be caused by something true

The award winners that feel alive share a mechanism rather than a style: they make a process visible and let form come out of it. Igloo Inc, Awwwards Site of the Year 2024, grows each project's ice with "a custom algorithm that mimicked the growth of ice crystals" and assembles particles into a different shape per link. It was also tested "to ensure smooth navigation on low-end devices" ([Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html)). Hatom shows a griffin's transformation in five stages, each unlocked by click-and-hold ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/hatom)). In the David Whyte Experience, "a long press on any painting reveals a video depicting the actual location that inspired it" ([Immersive Garden](https://immersive-g.com/projects/david-whyte-experience)).

Data art reaches the same conclusion from the other side. Ben Fry's *All Streets* draws 26 million road segments and nothing else, yet mountains and empty land "emerge" from the gaps ([Ben Fry](https://www.benfry.com/allstreets/)). Martin Wattenberg's NameVoyager answered each typed letter with the combined popularity of every name starting with those letters, and drew "millions of visits" ([bewitched.com](https://www.bewitched.com/namevoyager.html)). Emergence with a real cause reads as discovery. A particle cloud that morphs into a logo reads as a template.

Name Atlas has five real processes, and each can own one visual event. Tying every emergent moment to one of them is the core of the proposed direction. Each also has a failure mode that would break a product rule.

| Real event | Emergent visual | Must never imply |
|---|---|---|
| The user types | A field of published surnames narrows letter by letter, showing a true count at each keystroke. It then splits at a diacritic (Ł ≠ L) into two populations that never re-merge. | That Luczak is a misspelling of Łuczak, or that the two share an origin |
| The input resolves | The canonical form crystallises. Ambiguous input ("Walesa") prompts "Wałęsa?" instead of autocorrecting. | Silent correction or merging of forms |
| The lookup runs | The loading screen lists the real steps: diacritics normalised, PESEL snapshot of 2026-01-20 read, sources checked. | Fake progress, artificial delay, a 0–100% counter |
| Evidence arrives | Marks settle and sharpen only where a claim has a source. An unsupported claim stays a reserved, labelled place. | That a gap is zero, or that a guess is evidence |
| The count lands | Exactly N marks for each register list, each field labelled and dated | A merged total, a trend, or a map of "where your family lives" |

The first row answers Mitchell Whitelaw's criticism that a search box is "ungenerous: it withholds information, and demands a query" ([DHQ](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/9/1/000205/000205.html)). The page can open on the living field of published surnames, and the typed name emerges from it instead of hiding behind a bare box. The loading row rests on the "labour illusion": when a site shows its effort, people "prefer websites with longer waits to those that return instantaneous results—even when those results are identical" ([Buell & Norton](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v57y2011i9p1564-1579.html)). The same mechanism becomes deception the moment a step is invented or a delay is artificial. The ritual is therefore allowed only when it shows operations that are really happening.

The discovery itself should follow what Segel and Heer call the "martini glass" structure: a short stem the author controls, opening into a bowl the reader explores freely. They single out two under-used devices, "stimulating default views" and "tacit tutorials" (teaching an interaction by letting it happen) ([Segel & Heer](https://idl.cs.washington.edu/files/2010-Narrative-InfoVis.pdf)). Nielsen's rule that a page must "clearly communicate your value proposition within 10 seconds" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)) makes the first screen the complete factual answer, readable without scrolling. That means the canonical surname with its diacritics, each list's count with its date, and a status on every claim. About five short chapters follow on ordinary vertical scroll: rarity, letters, meaning where there is evidence, one historical dimension, and sources and sharing. The page then opens into variants, method and related names.

The evidence favours scrolling. The New York Times graphics desk concluded that "readers just want to scroll", and that asking for anything else means "something spectacular has to happen" ([Tse](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/archietse/malofiej-2016/master/tse-malofiej-2016-slides.pdf)). McKenna and colleagues found that animated transitions scored significantly higher on engagement than static views, with no significant difference between tap-through steps and scrolling ([McKenna et al.](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~miriah/publications/narrative-flow.pdf)). Webflow's 2026 report describes a shift from scroll as cinema to scroll as navigation, with a "TL;DR" summary first ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/web-design-trends-2026)). Spotify Wrapped shows that tap-through works at enormous scale for content about oneself, so a story mode earns a place, but only for previewing and exporting 9:16 share cards.

Typing is both the only required ritual and the tacit tutorial, because the signature draws itself from the letters as they are typed. Press-and-hold to "develop" a claim's evidence, after the David Whyte long press, stays optional with tap and keyboard alternatives, and no ritual ever gates a fact. The chapters end with a final summary, another device Segel and Heer found under-used, which doubles as the default share card.

Emergence should be an event that finishes, not a permanent background effect. The New York Times's 2016 election needle "trembled toward a likely Trump presidency" and became a byword for anxious, distrusted prediction ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/?p=76433)). Name Atlas's claims are not live forecasts. Once a composition settles it should rest, and with reduced motion switched on it should arrive already settled. Amber Case's calm-technology principle that technology "should work even when it fails" ([calmtech.com](https://calmtech.com/)) is the same instinct in product form. Among award sites in constant motion, an interface that moves only when evidence moves is itself distinctive.

## Exactly N marks per register list, across five orders of magnitude

The count is the product's anchor fact, and the register's own structure should decide its form. The official dataset lists the current surnames of living people in the PESEL register, **excluding surnames that occur only once and excluding the deceased** ([dane.gov.pl](https://dane.gov.pl/pl/dataset/1681)). An analysis of the 2026-01-20 files found the figures below, shown as the two separate populations the register publishes.

| List (2026-01-20) | Distinct surnames | Entries | Minimum count | NOWAK |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Men's list ([file](https://api.dane.gov.pl/resources/1148808,nazwiska-meskie-stan-na-2026-01-20/file)) | 408,182 | 21,197,537 | 2 | 98,387 |
| Women's list ([file](https://api.dane.gov.pl/resources/1148811,nazwiska-zenskie-stan-na-2026-01-20/file)) | 420,082 | 21,560,302 | 2 | 99,738 |

Each list drops its own single-bearer surnames, so a figure summed across the two would be quietly wrong as well as forbidden. For example, a surname carried by five men and one woman appears only on the men's list, and a total would silently leave out the woman. The count therefore takes the form of two separate fields. Each has its own label, its own date and its own note that single bearers are not published. A visible seam divides them, and they are never merged into one crowd. Gendered forms such as Kowalski and Kowalska sit on different lists, so the pairing is part of the structure rather than decoration.

One label needs checking before launch. The analysis could not confirm whether "living persons in PESEL" includes people abroad or foreigners holding PESEL numbers. Until that is verified, "on the PESEL register" is safer wording than "living in Poland".

The distribution has an extremely long tail, which changes what a typical page looks like. On the men's list the median surname has just **5 bearers**, and **65.7% of distinct surnames have between 2 and 9**, even though together they cover only 4.7% of people. At the other end, **140 surnames with 10,000 or more bearers** cover 12.8% of people, and about two-thirds of entries belong to surnames with 100 to 9,999 bearers. The women's figures are nearly identical ([PESEL dataset 1681](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/1681,nazwiska-osob-zyjacych-wystepujace-w-rejestrze-pesel)). A typical surname page will therefore show a single-digit count on each list, even though most individual people carry a more common name, and one display must span five orders of magnitude, from 2 to about 100,000.

Isotype's first rule says a larger quantity is shown by more same-sized pictograms, not a bigger one ([Isotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotype_%28picture_language%29)). That supports one mark per person up to a few thousand. An illustrative count of 4,318 fills a solid 34 × 127 block at about 10.5 px spacing on a 390 px-wide phone, roughly 1.6 screens tall. Above that, each mark takes a stated value printed on screen ("1 mark = 100 people"). Rarity is read off a logarithmic ruler (10, 100, 1k, 10k, 100k) that starts at 2, because a linear scale would make most names invisible. An uneven last row is an honesty device: it proves the number was not rounded. Small counts are not a lesser experience, since five marks can feel intimate rather than meagre. But any metaphor that needs mass, such as a letterform built from exactly N dots, needs a small-count version or it fails on the median page.

The evidence favours abstract marks over people-shaped ones. Icon arrays remove the bias that makes "1 in 200" feel bigger than "5 in 1000" ([Pighin et al.](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/89289)). A large-sample study found that data graphics drawn as people ("anthropographics") had "at best, a small effect" on how much money people gave, compared with a bar chart ([Morais et al.](https://hal-lara.archives-ouvertes.fr/HCI-SORBONNE/hal-03132731v1)). Human figures would also imply age, gender or ethnicity, which the product must never infer.

Better models are Abakanowicz's crowd as "a mysterious assemblage of variants of certain prototype" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Abakanowicz)) and Moritz Stefaner's "same, same, but different" clusters ([Multiplicity](https://truth-and-beauty.net/projects/multiplicity)). In Name Atlas each mark would be a slightly varied copy of one glyph, placed by a fixed random seed, with an on-screen note that "order means nothing". Order matters because it creates meaning, which is why Maya Lin rejected an alphabetical wall after "a tally of how many Smiths had died" ([NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2000/11/02/making-the-memorial/)).

Three devices make the number felt without inflating it. The first is a scale descent from a whole list down to this surname's marks, in the manner of Neal Agarwal's *The Deep Sea* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Agarwal)). The second is an optional "count them" mode after Opałka, whose lifelong count of painted numbers ended at 5,607,249 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Opa%C5%82ka)). The third is a zoom into one anonymous mark, following 2026 research on visualisations that move smoothly from statistics to individuals ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31026)), captioned "one of N people on this list with this surname; we know nothing else about them". There should be no highlighted "you are here" mark, because diaspora users may not be on the register and the product makes no claims about the user. Any ratio or rank shown must itself be a validated claim for the same list and date, never arithmetic done by the page.

Most data-art metaphors survive only in an honest form. The test is simple: anything that implies links between people, movement across space or continuity through time breaks a rule, because one dated snapshot plus scattered dated records contain none of those things.

| Metaphor | Honest use | What breaks if misused |
|---|---|---|
| Strata or core sample | One layer per century, present only where a dated record exists, with gaps labelled "no record found" | Solid fill between records implies continuous presence |
| Braids | Competing origin hypotheses as parallel threads that never knot | Threads between bearers imply kinship |
| Constellations | Spelling variants placed near each other, with a line only where a source states the link; network lines are design choices, not facts ([Albrecht](https://kimalbrecht.com/item/the_network_behind_the_cosmic_web_2016/)) | Invented lines read as facts |
| Growth systems | Animating the search itself: a branch per source queried, a bloom where evidence is found, withering for "searched, nothing found" | Suggests the population "grew" |
| Streams and ridgelines | Only across several dated register releases | A centuries-long stream invents a series of counts that does not exist |
| Maps | Where a surname is unusually common relative to local population, as in incumbents' "most characteristic places" ([nazwiska-polskie.pl](https://nazwiska-polskie.pl/Nowak)), drawn as soft density with an on-screen caveat | A red-to-yellow heat-map as the main image; bordered regions read as territory |
| Evidence barcode | One stripe per century, filled where a record was found and hatched where nobody searched ([warming stripes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warming_stripes)) | Stripes that stand for people rather than records |
| Family tree | None; lineage visuals only work on documented genealogy ([Royal Constellations](https://royalconstellations.visualcinnamon.com/)) | Implies shared descent among unrelated bearers |

## Six statuses and four kinds of absence become the visual language

Name Atlas's six statuses are not one line and should not be drawn as one. VERIFIED, LIKELY, POSSIBLE and UNCONFIRMED form a four-step ladder of support. CONFLICTING is a different kind of problem, one of *consistency* ("5 say M; 2 say T"), and INSUFFICIENT_DATA is one of *completeness*, in MacEachren's classification of uncertainty ([MacEachren et al.](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)). A single blur scale would tell users that a disagreement is merely a little less certain than an unconfirmed claim.

The scale deliberately has no percentages, and that is a legitimate choice rather than a compromise. The IPCC's confidence language is explicitly not probabilistic, and its guidance demands "a traceable account" and careful explanation of why low-confidence findings are presented at all ([IPCC](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf)).

MacEachren's experiments tested which visual properties people read as "less certain" on an ordered scale. They rated **fuzziness, position and lightness "good"**, arrangement, size and transparency "acceptable", and **hue, saturation, orientation and shape "unacceptable"**, and each property worked in one direction only: fuzzier, lighter, further from the centre or more disordered means less certain ([MacEachren et al.](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)). Only three steps were validated, and people judge sketchy rendering very differently from one another ([Wood et al.](https://ieeevis.org/year/2012/paper/infovis/sketchy-rendering-information-visualization)), so a four-step ladder needs several visual properties at once, plus the status word itself.

The wording matters as much as the mark. Lay readers pull verbal terms toward the middle, reading "very likely" as roughly 70% rather than above 90% ([Budescu et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcli/v4y2014i6d10.1038_nclimate2194.html)). Randomised trials found that people treat evidence with no quality label as high quality ([Schneider et al.](https://doaj.org/article/10dc6f83311943838557e06ff4be64a9)). Plain-language confidence statements still move trust in the right direction ([Padilla et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.579267/full)). Across 5,780 participants, admitting uncertainty cost only a little trust, mostly when it was expressed in words ([van der Bles et al.](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/the-effects-of-communicating-uncertainty-on-public-trust-in-facts/)).

The proposed encoding uses a "vessel" as the status mark:

| Status | Mark | Edge and ink | Position | Words always shown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VERIFIED | Vessel with 4 of 4 segments filled | Sharp edge, darkest ink, no texture | On the baseline | Status, source and dataset date |
| LIKELY | 3 of 4 filled | Sharp, one step lighter | On the grid, first ring out | Status and basis ("2 independent records agree") |
| POSSIBLE | 2 of 4 filled | Dashed outline, or a soft halo on the mark but never on the text | Slightly displaced | Status and "one of N explanations" |
| UNCONFIRMED | 1 of 4 filled | Pencil-sketch outline in light grey | Visibly loosened from the grid | Status, what is missing, "Have a record?" |
| CONFLICTING | Split vessel with a seam; each half keeps its own fill | Sharp on both sides | Equal size on a shared baseline | "Sources disagree", who says what, "not resolved" |
| INSUFFICIENT_DATA | Empty vessel of the same size, with a dotted rim | Background tone, no texture, never red | Exactly where the answer would sit | Reason, what was checked and when, what would change it |

Three precedents support the encoding. The filled segments borrow GRADE's ⊕⊕◯◯ convention, which shows the current level and the maximum at once and so counters the drift of words toward the middle ([GRADE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8564158/table/table4-23969873211024143)). Users preferred dashed outlines over blur ([Boukhelifa et al.](https://www.aviz.fr/Research/UncertaintySketchy)). Georg Nees's *Schotter*, whose squares become steadily more scattered down the sheet, matches "poorer arrangement means less certain" almost exactly, and it still works in greyscale ([V&A](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O221321/schotter-print-nees-georg/)).

The ladder stays still by default, and every claim carries its status, VERIFIED included. A miniature of the full scale sits beside each status so it is read relative to the whole. Colour is kept for brand and category, never for confidence, and red never appears on absence. One caution follows from the brief: drawn as paper texture, the "pencil" and "background" language slides straight back toward the excluded warm-paper look. Line quality, not simulated material, should carry it.

CONFLICTING calls for sharpness, not haze, because the disagreement lies between claims rather than inside one vague claim. The honest form is a compact matrix based on Heuer's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses. Hypotheses are the columns, key evidence forms the rows, and each cell is marked supports, contradicts or silent ([CIA](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf)). The matrix also carries the "other, not yet identified" possibility that van der Bles and colleagues recommend acknowledging ([van der Bles et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549952)). It should read as an explanation of the status rather than a verdict, since analysts trained in the method did not reliably reduce confirmation bias ([Dhami et al.](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/69049/)).

Whether sources are independent belongs on screen too. Two dictionaries repeating one 19th-century author are one voice, not two, and a "shared origin" connector stops repetition from passing as agreement. Three precedents supply the story line. Wikidata's rule is that ranks "are not a way of asserting your view for a disputed value" ([Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Ranking)). In the Upshot experiment, four reputable pollsters turned one poll into four different results ([FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/2016/09/21/statistics-lesson-on-polling/)). FiveThirtyEight's 2020 forecast opened with several possible maps rather than one ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2020/07/this-is-how-fivethirtyeight-is-trying-to-build-the-right-amount-of-uncertainty-into-its-2020-election-data-analysis/)). The shared line is: here is how reasonable readings of the same records lead to different answers. An illustrated origin, such as a smith's hammer for Kowalski, would visually settle what the evidence has not.

Absence needs at least four named marks, because agencies that publish counts already keep these cases apart. Statistics Poland uses a dash for "did not occur", a zero for a value below half a unit, and a dot for missing or confidential data ([GUS](https://olsztyn.stat.gov.pl/files/gfx/olsztyn/en/defaultstronaopisowa/761/1/2/2024_objasnienia_znakow_umownych.pdf)). The UK Government Analysis Function prefers bracketed words such as [c] confidential, [x] not available and [z] not applicable, partly because screen readers stumble over bare symbols ([GAF](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/symbols-in-tables-definitions-and-help/)). In Name Atlas, **suppressed by the source** appears as a sealed vessel with the words "not on the published list, which omits surnames carried by a single person". **Not in our sources yet** appears as an empty shelf. **Not applicable** is plain text. A **true zero** is a dash, used only where the data's coverage supports it, as the architecture already requires.

Two memorials give the two main absences distinct emotional forms. Rachel Whiteread's Vienna library, with its book spines turned inward, stands for what exists but is sealed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judenplatz_Holocaust_Memorial)). Micha Ullman's empty shelves in Berlin, sized for the 20,000 books burned there, stand for what was never collected or did not survive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Empty_Library)).

Polish institutions already treat "not yet known" as something to place rather than hide. The Uprising wall at Izba Pamięci keeps **empty plaques for names still to be identified** among more than 62,000 ([Muzeum Warszawy](https://muzeumwarszawy.pl/dla-mediow/izba-pamieci_komunikat_styczen_2022)). Polona made a casket holding the ashes of manuscripts destroyed after the 1944 Uprising its two-millionth object ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polona)). Research agrees: clearly highlighted gaps keep readers' trust in the data while silently broken lines lower it ([Song & Szafir](https://cmci.colorado.edu/visualab/MissingData)), and empty values that surface as "0" mislead ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/my-empty-cells-get-displayed-as-zeros-in-tooltips)).

Each absence should say what was checked and when. It should count as "negative evidence" only where a record would reasonably be expected to exist ([Evidence Explained](https://evidenceexplained.com/node/1516)). Undocumented centuries should appear as a faint, labelled continuation, the way Periscopic extended lives cut short into "stolen years" ([FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/?p=28506)). An unfinished-looking form can invite contributions, since sketchy renderings made people more willing to annotate ([Wood et al.](https://ieeevis.org/year/2012/paper/infovis/sketchy-rendering-information-visualization)). That works only if every contribution enters the Source → Record → Evidence chain and is checked before it appears, as Arolsen Archives does by having three volunteers process each document ([Arolsen](https://arolsen-archives.org/en/get-involved/everynamecounts/)).

Sourcing must be structural, not decorative. An audit of AI search engines found only **51.5% of generated sentences fully supported by their citations**, and only **74.5% of citations actually supporting their sentence** ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848v2)), so numbered citation chips no longer signal trust on their own. A Name Atlas citation should open onto the record excerpt itself (transcription, archive, call number, date) within one tap, as gwern.net's nested popups do without losing the reader's place ([gwern.net](https://gwern.net/design)).

Museum provenance punctuation offers a ready grammar: a semicolon for a direct transfer and a full stop for a gap ([Krannert Art Museum](https://kam.illinois.edu/resource/aam-resource-how-read-provenance-narrative)). In Name Atlas that becomes a solid joint for a documented link and an open joint for an inferred one along the evidence chain. Muzeum Sztuki's superscript branch names, ms¹ and ms², are a local precedent for small superscript source and status tokens ([Muzeum Sztuki](https://msl.org.pl/en)).

Three further rules keep the layers honest. First, source authority, evidence verification and claim confidence stay three visible layers rather than one "trust score". Second, rights stay separate from truth. Because "more obscured" reads as "less certain", licensing restrictions must never use fading, blur or transparency, and a record held at its source keeps its confidence status visible. Third, as confidence falls, displayed precision should fall with it: years become centuries and towns become regions. This is the principle behind "value-suppressing" uncertainty palettes ([Correll, Moritz & Heer](https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/value-suppressing-uncertainty-palettes)). Uncertain geography never gets a sharp border: NOAA's review calls the hurricane "cone of uncertainty" possibly "the most misinterpreted product", with the cone's border a suspected cause ([NOAA](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/general/lib/lib1/nhclib/Bibliographies/Cone%20of%20Uncertainty%20Literature%20Review_4_10_19%20%281%29%20%281%29.pdf)).

## Polish method, memorial restraint and diacritics as the signature

Polish modernism offers metaphors that fit "a name shared by many people across time" more exactly than any imported motif, and all of them can be drawn from data alone. Roman Opałka painted consecutive numbers from 1965 until his death, speaking each one aloud and making each new canvas about 1% whiter ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Opa%C5%82ka)). That is a model for a count as a dated trace, in which fading can mark a data snapshot's age but must never suggest people disappearing. Zbigniew Rybczyński's Oscar-winning *Tango* loops 36 characters through a single room without any of them ever interacting ([Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/work/tango-zbigniew-rybczynski); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango_%281981_film%29)), an exact picture of Łuczak and Luczak coexisting without merging and of competing hypotheses sharing one frame. Władysław Strzemiński's *Afterimages* aimed "to grasp human memory (history) through the eye" ([Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/work/suns-afterimage-wladyslaw-strzeminski)), which suggests a typed name leaving a soft afterimage that becomes the result's background. His Unism sought "the greatest possible union of forms with the plane of the picture" ([MoMA](https://post.moma.org/?p=1964)), an argument against a hero photo with text laid over it.

The same generation supplies proportion, texture and framing. Katarzyna Kobro's 8:5 rhythm, "open to infinity" ([Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/artist/katarzyna-kobro)), gives one ratio for grid, type scale and motion timing, and a reason to let fields run off the edge of the screen. Henryk Berlewi built images from rhythmic lines, circles and squares in black, white and red ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Berlewi)), a method for turning letters into texture provided red stays rare enough never to read as the national flag. Oskar Hansen's Open Form treats architecture as a "perceptive background" that its users complete ([Metalocus](https://www.metalocus.es/en/news/oskar-hansen-and-open-form)), which describes a quiet frame that the surname finishes. Wojciech Fangor's soft-edged paintings ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Fangor)) offer a native vocabulary of halo and sharpness, to be applied to marks and never to words.

The Polish School of Posters contributes a discipline rather than a look: "succinct metaphors", lettering fused with image, and "pictorial economy" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_School_of_Posters)). It worked for audiences whom censorship had taught "to perfectly read the metaphors hidden in a poster" ([HMCT](https://hmctartcenter.org/articles/censorship-creativity-and-the-polish-school-of-posters)). A Polish audience can be expected to reward restraint and wit over explanatory illustration, whereas imitating 1960s brush lettering reads as souvenir design.

Folk culture works the same way: borrow procedures, never motifs. Kurpie paper-cuts are cut in one colour from a sheet folded once ([CREES](https://crees.ku.edu/wycinanki)), which suggests a share card that mirrors the surname's letter outlines on a single axis. Łowicz paper-cuts stack separately cut sheets, a model for time as layered planes. WWAA's Polish pavilion at Expo 2010 turned paper-cut logic into CNC-cut plywood and drew more than 8 million visitors ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2010/06/03/polish-pavilion-for-shanghai-expo-2010-by-wwaa-architects/)). Wanda Telakowska's Institute of Industrial Design treated folk art as a method of working with makers rather than a stock of ornaments ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Telakowska)). Regional patterns carry a rule risk as well as a kitsch risk: a Kurpie or Kashubian pattern beside a surname implies that the name belongs to a region or an ethnic group.

The strongest identity material is Polish spelling itself. **27.5% of register entries have a surname with at least one Polish diacritic.** The most common are ł (in 9.4% of men's-list entries) and ń (8.1%, largely from the ‑ński ending). The register stores surnames in capitals, so Ł, Ś and Ż are its native forms ([PESEL dataset 1681](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/1681,nazwiska-osob-zyjacych-wystepujace-w-rejestrze-pesel)).

Adam Twardoch's type-design guidance is precise. The Polish kreska (the accent in ś, ć, ń) "runs steeper and is shifted more to the right" than a Western acute ([Twardoch](https://www.twardoch.com/download/polishhowto/kreska.html)). The ogonek (the hook in ą, ę) is not an accent but "much more a character element", and it never extends past the right edge of its letter ([Twardoch](https://www.twardoch.com/download/polishhowto/ogonek.html)). An audit of 76 Google Fonts families found that all of them cover the 18 Polish letters, but **only five ship Polish-specific kreska shapes** through the OpenType `locl` feature: Playfair Display, Vollkorn, Cormorant Garamond, Archivo and Chivo ([google/fonts](https://github.com/google/fonts)).

A credible pairing, set with `lang="pl"`, puts **Półtawski Nowy**, a digitised 1928 typeface drawn specifically for Polish ([Google Fonts](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/poltawskinowy/DESCRIPTION.en_us.html)), on the surname and historical records. **Archivo**, which has Polish kreska shapes and a width axis for constructivist compression, carries the interface, and a monospace such as **IBM Plex Mono** carries counts, dates and source identifiers.

The slanted stroke of Ł and the kreska can become the two brand marks: the first as divider, cursor and progress line, the second as focus and active state. A stem-and-suffix split can show a surname's structure without claiming a meaning. The endings ‑ski, ‑cki and ‑dzki cover 29.3% of men's-list entries, and the ending "does not in itself denote" nobility ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_name)).

Polish institutions already work this way. POLIN's square mark was extracted from the museum building's windows ([Behance](https://www.behance.net/gallery/80263141/Polin-Museum)), and the National Museum in Warsaw derives its ornament from its own ligature ([MNW](https://www.mnw.art.pl/gfx/muzeumnarodowe/en/defaultstronaopisowa/103/3/1/mnw_ci_manual.pdf)). The closest precedent for per-name marks is the Lithuanian studio DADADA's system for the Warsaw Ghetto Museum, which generates signets from historical names ([dzieje.pl](https://dzieje.pl/node/115135)). Because that system belongs to a memorial, Name Atlas must not look like a memorial register.

Memorial practice teaches restraint and draws one hard boundary. Maya Lin wanted a wall that "allows the names to become the object" ([NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2000/11/02/making-the-memorial/)), an argument for the surname as the typographic hero. Nowy Sącz read all 12,000 names on its new monument aloud at the unveiling, as part of a project called "People, not numbers" ([Notes from Poland](https://notesfrompoland.com/2022/08/30/polish-town-unveils-monument-naming-its-12000-jews-killed-in-holocaust/)). That argues for pronunciation as a moment of presence, which is the one good pattern Behind the Name already offers, with /ˈnɔ.vak/ ([Behind the Name](https://surnames.behindthename.com/name/nowak)). A pronunciation is itself a claim, though, and needs a status.

Other memorials set limits. Munich bans the Stolpersteine name plaques from public ground because people would step on the names ([Jüdische Allgemeine](https://www.juedische-allgemeine.de/politik/stolpergefahr-in-muenchen)), so no interaction should trample, scatter or pop a name. The 9/11 memorial placed names side by side in response to about 1,200 requests from loved ones, not by guesswork ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/september-11-memorial)). Names in Name Atlas should therefore sit together only through evidenced relations (spelling variant, gender form, related name in another language), with the reason written on the link. Ellis Island's $300 inscriptions show how quickly presence becomes a product ([Ellis Island](https://ellisisland.org/wall-of-honor-overview/)).

The hard boundary is inferring identity from a surname. In Poland, lists that "expose" public figures' supposed "real Jewish surnames" are an established antisemitic practice ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonica.net)). Dictionary labels such as "Polish Rusyn (from Poland) Sorbian and Jewish (eastern Ashkenazic)" read as statements about the bearers when shown without explanation ([Ancestry](https://www.ancestry.com/name-origin?surname=nowak)). Copy must speak of languages and word roots, never of groups of people, and must never answer "is my name Jewish, German or noble?" with a label.

Script is no clue to identity either. Parish records in the Kingdom of Poland moved from Latin to Polish around 1808, and to Russian in Cyrillic from about 1868 ([Stephen Danko](https://stephendanko.com/blog/2732)): that is a fact about administration, not ancestry. Heraldry fails twice, because Polish coats of arms belonged to clans shared by "sometimes hundreds" of unrelated families with different surnames ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_heraldry)). Finally, three sources together rule out sepia, synthetic faces and trauma imagery as atmosphere: USHMM's guidance against simulation and romanticising ([USHMM](https://www.ushmm.org/en/teach/fundamentals/guidelines-for-teaching-the-holocaust)), Svetlana Boym's warning that restorative nostalgia passes itself off as "truth and tradition" ([Boym](https://base.milano.it/en/extracts-from-an-adoptation-and-elaboration-of-the-future-of-nostalgia-by-svetlana-boym-2001-as-published-on-monumenttotransformation-org/)), and the backlash against animated ancestor photos ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/myheritage-creepy-deepfake-tool)).

## Share a portrait of the letters, never a verdict on the family

Lasting dynamic identities pair one fixed element with a variable layer driven by a stated input. The MIT Media Lab's 2011 identity generated 40,000 variations, one per person ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/mit-media-labs-new-logo-has-40000-variations)). By 2014 Pentagram had rebuilt it around one 7×7 grid with glyphs for 23 research groups ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/mit-media-lab)), a sign that unlimited variation needs a much stronger fixed element. Visit Nordkyn's weather-driven logo is "part logo, part useful information" ([Neue](https://neue.no/work/visit-nordkyn/)), and that is the warning: people read data into a mark that changes.

Name Atlas's signature should therefore be drawn from the letters alone. Meaning, origin, region and heraldry are excluded as inputs, and so, less obviously, are the count and rarity. Register data changes with each release, and a signature that changed with it would repeat the AncestryDNA episode, in which an update drastically changed customers' ethnicity estimates ([ClickOrlando](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/2019/04/30/ancestrycom-under-fire-after-update-changes-the-ethnicity-of-users)).

Three properties make a generated mark feel personal rather than random. **Determinism** means the same seed produces the same image forever. **A readable mapping** matters because musical cryptograms such as B-A-C-H and Shostakovich's D-S-C-H have carried meaning for centuries through a known key ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_cryptogram)), whereas GitHub's hash-generated identicons only tell users apart ([GitHub](https://github.blog/2013-08-14-identicons/)). **A curated range of outputs** matters because, as Tyler Hobbs puts it, in well-made long-form generative work "bad results are extremely rare" by design, which took him about two months of quality checking on *Fidenza* ([Hobbs](https://tylerxhobbs.com/essays/2021/the-rise-of-long-form-generative-art)). People also pick the letters of their own name more often than other letters (0.30 against 0.20, and 0.46 for initials), a finding known as the "name-letter effect" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name-letter_effect)). That suggests letter-derived form will feel owned, although no study has tested this for art.

The signature therefore has two layers. A structural layer maps each letter, each Polish letter pair (cz, rz, sz and others) and each diacritic type (kreska, ogonek, overdot and the stroke of ł) to a consistent mark, so related names such as Kowalski and Kowalska visibly share a stem. An ornamental layer, seeded from a hash, adds texture, noise and a choice among curated palettes, so that every surname is unique. The seed is a hash of the algorithm version plus the canonical surname in Unicode NFC form. Unicode guarantees that equivalent strings are identical only after this normalisation ([UAX #15](https://unicode.org/reports/tr15/)). Including the version keeps old shared links showing the same image. Before launch, every surname on both lists should be rendered and checked for broken or ugly outputs, including the shortest, longest, most diacritic-heavy and hyphenated names.

The signature carries its own caption: "Drawn only from the letters of Łuczak. It says nothing about your family's history." It never carries "rare trait" labels, which users would confuse with a rare surname or a special family. An optional musical-cryptogram rendering of the letters, started by a tap, is an honest sound layer with centuries of precedent.

Personal-data artefacts spread at enormous scale when they compare and reveal something about the person. Spotify Wrapped 2025 reached **more than 200 million engaged users in 24 hours** and was shared **more than 500 million times, a figure that counts screenshots and downloads** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day)). It had adopted the social-media stories format in 2019 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify_Wrapped)). Duolingo found that putting learners' percentile ranking on the share card raised share rates, that a second "learner style" card "significantly boosted" them, and that the top 10% of learners produced more than half of all shares ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/year-in-review-behind-the-scenes)). People give up money to talk about themselves, and sharing that disclosure is more rewarding than keeping it private ([Tamir & Mitchell](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3361411/)).

Rarity is therefore both Name Atlas's most natural thing to share and its main dignity hazard. If rare means winner, the Nowaks get a lesser card, so every surname needs an equally proud, factual card with no status language. Honest second reasons to share are the letter signature, the diacritics a surname carries, and a comparison of two surnames. The comparison must be started by the user, never automatically, because it can reveal maiden names.

Screenshots are a main sharing channel, so every chapter screen must be complete on its own, with surname, fact, list, date and status in frame. A cropped image then stays true for its date. The card should carry its explanation with it: the portrait on one side, and list, date and sources on the other, in the spirit of Lupi and Posavec's hand-drawn *Dear Data* correspondence ([Dear Data](http://www.dear-data.com/)).

Engagement techniques stay legitimate only while they are tied to the truth, and European regulators are actively testing that line. The European Commission's 2023 sweep found manipulative practices on **148 of 399** online shops, **42 of them with fake countdown timers** ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_23_418/IP_23_418_EN.pdf)). A 2024 global sweep found at least one manipulative design pattern on 75.7% of 642 sites and apps ([ICPEN](https://icpen.org/news/1360)), and Poland's consumer authority UOKiK took part ([wiadomoscikosmetyczne.pl](https://www.wiadomoscikosmetyczne.pl/rynek-kosmetyczny/uokik-wzial-udzial-w-globalnym-przegladzie-internetu-pod-katem-dark-patterns-2514434)). Article 25 of the Digital Services Act bars deceptive interface design on online platforms ([DSA Art. 25](https://prighter.com/resources/laws/dsa/articles/article-25)). A Digital Fairness Act aimed at manipulative and addictive design is expected before the end of 2026 ([PrivacyLaws](https://www.privacylaws.com/news/eu-proposal-on-digital-fairness-act-expected-by-the-end-of-2026/)), although no proposal had been published as of 30 September 2026.

Loewenstein describes curiosity as the pull of an "information gap" ([Loewenstein](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/)). Opening such a gap is ethical only when the gap is real and is closed on the same page, for free. The "IKEA effect" (valuing what you built yourself) disappears when people fail to finish ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IKEA_effect)), so any ritual must be optional and completable.

A premium offer should appear only where deeper evidence actually exists. It should say what it contains and what it does not (no ancestors, no crest), show the full price, and ask once. There should be no unlocking by sharing, no badges for sharing, and no streaks or "come back tomorrow" hooks. Results should be available without an account: the 23andMe data breach and bankruptcy remain the trust lesson for any product close to personal identity ([Yahoo/AP](https://www.yahoo.com/news/genetic-testing-company-23andme-files-145618325.html)).

## A server-rendered answer carries a living layer under 60 KB

The 2026 web platform makes this direction buildable without exotic technology. WebGPU is on by default in Safari 26 on iOS and macOS, and in Chrome for Android 121 and later on Android 12 devices with Qualcomm or ARM graphics. Firefox for Android still hides it behind a flag ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers); [gpuweb](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status)). caniuse puts full WebGPU support at 85.72% of users, against **96.44% for WebGL 2** ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/webgpu); [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/webgl2)).

Three.js's WebGPU renderer falls back to WebGL 2 automatically ([three.js](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three@0.186.1/src/renderers/webgpu/WebGPURenderer.js)). But its WebGPU build measured about **296 KB gzipped against about 190 KB** for the WebGL build, both measured without removing unused code, while OGL's entire minimal WebGL library is **29 KB** ([three.js build](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three@0.186.1/build/); [OGL](https://github.com/oframe/ogl)). Even FWA's 2025 winner, Bruno Simon's WebGPU portfolio, ships a WebGL fallback and turns off water blur and depth of field on phones ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)).

A field of 5,000 to 50,000 marks is well within what WebGL 2 draws efficiently. A 2014 test on a Nexus 5 managed about 17,500 objects at 30 frames per second in WebGL, against 360 in plain Canvas 2D ([W3C archive](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-whatwg-archive/2014Aug/0038.html)). No current benchmark on mid-range phones exists, so testing on real devices is essential.

The architecture is progressive enhancement in five layers. Each layer is a complete experience on its own, and each adds to the one below.

| Layer | Content | Technique | Fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 Facts | Surname heading, per-list counts and dates, statuses, citations, tables | Server-rendered React components, semantic HTML, `next/font` with both Latin font subsets | This is the fallback |
| L1 Native motion | Transitions between pages, reveals on scroll, brief type animations | React `<ViewTransition>`; CSS scroll-driven animation inside an `@supports` check; `@property` | Instant page swap; content is never hidden at the start |
| L2 Poster | This surname's signature and count fields as a static SVG image | One shared module that turns the seed into geometry using whole-number arithmetic only | Also serves as the reduced-motion, no-JavaScript and low-end-phone view |
| L3 Live layer | Marks assembling and settling, built from the same geometry | WebGL 2 through OGL or custom code, loaded after the page first appears, optionally in a background worker | Stay on L2 |
| L4 Optional extras | Sound, vibration on Android, richer type animation | Web Audio after a tap; `navigator.vibrate` where supported | Silently absent |

The order matters because the heading should be what Google treats as the page's main content when it measures loading speed (Largest Contentful Paint, or LCP). A `<canvas>` never counts, and elements with zero opacity are ignored ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/lcp)). Text hidden until an animation reveals it therefore slows the measured load and shows search engines a blank page. Google still recommends server-rendered or pre-rendered pages because "not all bots can run JavaScript" ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics)).

The motion layers can lean on the platform rather than on heavy libraries. Same-page View Transitions have worked in all major browsers since October 2025, and the Next.js 16 App Router supports React's `<ViewTransition>` with no configuration ([webstatus.dev](https://webstatus.dev/features/view-transitions); [Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/view-transitions)). CSS scroll-driven animations work in Chrome-based browsers and Safari 26 but not in stable Firefox, so they belong inside an `@supports` check with content visible by default ([webstatus.dev](https://webstatus.dev/features/scroll-driven-animations)). Motion, which is MIT-licensed and has a built-in setting that respects reduced-motion preferences, is the natural default for interface animation ([Motion](https://motion.dev/docs/react-accessibility)). GSAP is now free, but under a proprietary Webflow licence that Webflow can change "at any time" and terminate for non-compliance ([GSAP](https://gsap.com/standard-license/)), so it must be recorded as proprietary if chosen.

Several popular tools fit poorly. Lenis smooths mouse-wheel scrolling but leaves touch scrolling native by default ([Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)), so it adds little on phones. Rive's animation runtimes weigh roughly 350–885 KB gzipped ([jsDelivr](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@rive-app/canvas@2.43.1/)) and belong only on dedicated pages, and animated SVG turbulence filters remain slow in Safari's engine ([WebKit Bugzilla](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=263848)). Sound must start from a tap, because browsers suspend audio started without a user gesture ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices)). Vibration is an Android-only extra, because Safari on iOS has no Vibration API ([webstatus.dev](https://webstatus.dev/features/vibration)).

Making the art reproducible is where engineering and trust meet. One shared module should take the canonical surname from the validated data and normalise it to Unicode NFC. "Łódź" is four UTF-16 code units in NFC but six in NFD, so an un-normalised name would yield a different seed. The module then hashes the name with xmur3 or cyrb53 and uses the hash to seed a random-number generator such as mulberry32 or sfc32 ([bryc](https://github.com/bryc/code/blob/master/jshash/PRNGs.md)). It builds geometry with whole-number arithmetic only, because JavaScript's `Math` functions "have a precision that's implementation-dependent" and can differ between browsers ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/Math)). The same geometry then feeds the server-rendered poster, the live layer and the share image, so the card people post matches what they saw.

Next.js generates share images through Satori, which supports only flexbox layout, rejects WOFF2 fonts and caps the bundle at 500 KB ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/functions/image-response); [Satori](https://github.com/vercel/satori)). Share images therefore need a static TTF font that includes the Polish letters, plus pre-computed SVG paths. Takumi is the fallback if grid layout becomes necessary ([Takumi](https://github.com/kane50613/takumi)). Every number on a card must be text from the validated data, never a sum, ratio or rank calculated while drawing it.

Fonts need Polish-specific care. Google Fonts splits the Polish letters across two subsets: `latin` holds ó and Ó, and `latin-ext` holds ą, ć, ę, ł, ń, ś, ź and ż ([Google Fonts](https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Inter:wght@100..900)). Both must load. `next/font` hosts fonts itself and builds size-matched fallbacks ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/font)). However, Safari supports only `size-adjust`, not the ascent and descent overrides that stop text jumping when the web font arrives ([MDN BCD](https://unpkg.com/@mdn/browser-compat-data/data.json)). The display face therefore needs reserved line heights, or `display: optional`, to keep layout stable on iPhones.

Accessibility sets hard floors. Background motion that runs for more than five seconds alongside content needs a pause control at the most basic conformance level ([W3C 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)), and nothing may flash more than three times a second ([W3C 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)). Motion triggered by interaction should be possible to switch off ([W3C 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)), which here means reduced motion shows the finished composition, never an empty or loading one. Screen readers cannot see inside a canvas ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/canvas)), so every fact lives in the HTML and the canvas is hidden from assistive technology. ARIA forbids giving accessible names to paragraph and span elements ([WAI-ARIA 1.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/wai-aria-1.2/)), so letter-by-letter animation must keep the whole word intact in a heading, and reveal masks must leave room for the kreska above Ś and the ogonek below Ę.

The proposed budgets, to be validated on real devices, leave headroom under Google's "good" thresholds of 2.5 s, 200 ms and 0.1 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). For the 75th-percentile mobile user they are **2.0 s for Largest Contentful Paint, 150 ms for Interaction to Next Paint and 0.05 for Cumulative Layout Shift**. The live layer should be at most 60 KB gzipped and kept out of the initial JavaScript, rendering resolution should be capped at 1.5× on low- and mid-range phones, and animation should be designed for a floor of 30 frames per second, because iOS Low Power Mode deliberately limits animation to that rate ([WebKit Bugzilla](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=215745)). Graphics-tier detection libraries are now rough hints at best, since detect-gpu's benchmark data stopped updating in December 2025 ([detect-gpu](https://github.com/pmndrs/detect-gpu)). A live frame-rate monitor should decide the quality level instead.

## Three concept directions pass every constraint

The principles leave room for distinct concepts, and three directions emerge from the research. Each is built around a different hero moment. All share the same fixed elements: the letters-only signature, the status vessels, the named absence marks, and screens complete enough to share.

| Direction | Hero moment | Polish anchor | Precedents | Honesty guard | Technical emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crystallisation | Typing narrows the field of published surnames, splits it at a diacritic, then settles exactly N marks per list | Abakanowicz's variants; Strzemiński's afterimage; Kobro's 8:5 | Igloo's grown ice; NameVoyager; Isotype | Real counts at every keystroke; lists never merge; a small-count version | L3 animated marks over an L2 poster |
| Core sample | A vertical descent from a whole list to this name, then down through dated layers of records with labelled gaps | Opałka's count as time; Łowicz layering; the 1843 quartermaster's map with its legend of 54 standard signs ([PAN Library](https://platforma.bk.pan.pl/en/bib_records/195119)) | *The Deep Sea*; History Flow ([hint.fm](http://hint.fm/projects/historyflow/)); the chapters of *Where is Poland?*; Periscopic's faded continuation | No fill between records; gaps named and dated | Ordinary scroll, CSS scroll-driven reveals, SVG layers |
| One room, many loops | Spelling variants, gendered pairs and competing hypotheses share one frame as sharp loops that never touch | Rybczyński's *Tango*; Kurpie single-fold symmetry; the poster school's single metaphor | FiveThirtyEight's multiple maps; the competing-hypotheses matrix; KPR's "One world, two factions" ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/kpr)), as metaphor only | Motion only on request, with equal time for each; no threads between bearers | `<ViewTransition>`, Motion and SVG |

Each direction suits a different case. Crystallisation serves visitors arriving from search best, because its hero moment is the answer itself. Core sample suits surnames with dated records and gives the richest scrolling story. One room serves the diaspora case, where the diacritic split and spelling variants carry the emotional weight. The three combine naturally: crystallisation as the opening, the core as the history chapter, and the room for variants and disagreement. But Webflow's argument for proprietary effects rewards one signature move used everywhere, so one direction should lead.

The research leaves concrete tests to run before choosing. The first is whether users can put the four ladder steps in order at phone sizes, which MacEachren flagged as an open question. The next two are whether the split vessel reads as disagreement rather than "half-certain", and whether the empty vessel reads as unknown rather than zero or an error. The remaining three are how Polish readers interpret Polish wording for the six statuses, how Polish and diaspora adults respond to abstract rather than literal heritage visuals (no source addressed this), and whether rare and common surnames are shared at different rates. Three blockers sit outside design. Any premium offer needs a legal review under the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive and Polish law. Someone must confirm exactly who the PESEL "living persons" population includes. And particle counts need benchmarking on real phones.

## Conclusion

The research changes where Name Atlas's originality comes from. Rendering technology contributes little that is essential. The distinctive forms come from the product's refusals: to add up what the register keeps apart, to fill silence, to fold Łuczak into Luczak, to crown one origin. Each refusal produces a visual form that incumbents cannot adopt without dismantling their heat-map-and-upsell model. The practical consequence is to build three boundaries into the design system's basic components rather than into written guidelines. The first is a signature generator whose only input is the canonical surname. The second is a count component that accepts one list and one date and has no way to show a total. The third is a rights marker that shares no visual property with confidence. Guidelines erode under deadline pressure; components that cannot express a violation do not.

The long tail also changes what a hero moment means. Most pages will show a handful of marks, an absence that needs explaining, or a split between a diacritic spelling and its plain-ASCII shadow. The direction therefore succeeds or fails on those quiet states rather than on the 98,387 Nowaks of the men's list. For diaspora users whose families lost a diacritic, the moment Luczak splits from Łuczak is the natural climax of the experience and deserves the most care. The next research question is whether Polish and diaspora users actually read vessels, gaps and splits as intended. It is worth answering rather than retreating to templates, because every alternative on the market already breaks the product's rules.
