Research notes for the Name Atlas immersive design exploration. Compiled 2026-09-30.

## 1. Competitor and adjacent surname pages: what they show, how they look, where they are generic or overclaim

### Takeaway
Surname pages all use the same template: a count and rank, a heat-map, a one-line meaning, and an upsell to records, DNA tests or crests. They regularly overclaim:
- they attach religion, politics, income and "ethnicity" to a surname
- they talk about the reader's "ancestors"
- they mix user-submitted etymologies with dictionary ones
- they publish counts without dates or methodology

The Polish sites are thinner: undated counts and maps, no provenance, joke comment threads. One former Polish surname-map site now redirects to MyHeritage.

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)
- **The look is generic and interchangeable.** Every product relies on a red-to-yellow heat-map, sortable tables, a "meaning" paragraph and an upsell. Nobody has an original visual idea about what a surname *is*. House of Names adds crest imagery; the others add stock family-history cues.
- **Overclaiming falls into seven types:**
  1. **Identity inference:** religion, political party and salary by surname (Forebears); an "Ethnicity Percentage Map" (Ancestry).
  2. **Lineage framing:** "your Nowak ancestors" (Ancestry). This treats every census Nowak as the user's family.
  3. **Heraldic commerce:** crests sold per surname (House of Names).
  4. **Unsourced origin narratives:** "first found in Bohemia and Moravia" for the most common surname in *Poland*.
  5. **User content blended with dictionary content**, plus prize contests for meanings (Forebears).
  6. **Undated, unexplained counts** that contradict each other (268,732 vs ~170,000).
  7. **Absence shown as zero:** FamilySearch's "0 records".
- **The white space Name Atlas can own.** No competitor does any of these:
  - a dated official count
  - diacritic-exact identity: Łuczak ≠ Luczak (Geneteka even needs an "exact search" box because its default matching merges forms)
  - a visible, dignified treatment of "no evidence"
  - competing origin hypotheses shown side by side
  - the primary evidence itself (a scan) behind a historical claim
- **Good patterns worth keeping:**
  - Behind the Name's **pronunciation**, which matters for diaspora users.
  - Relative geography: nazwiska-polskie's **"most characteristic" places** (20×/25× over-representation) and UCL's "unusually prevalent" maps. Both say more than raw heat-maps, which mostly mirror population density.
  - Geneteka's plain table **with act numbers**. Genealogists trust it because provenance is visible, not because it looks good.
- **Consolidation risk.** A Polish surname-map brand (moikrewni.pl) now redirects to a US/Israeli DNA-and-records upsell funnel. That leaves an opening for an independent Polish product whose editorial stance is clear.
- Polish dictionaries such as DAFN label a surname by community ("Polish Rusyn… Sorbian and Jewish (eastern Ashkenazic)"). Shown to Polish users without framing, those labels would read as statements about who the bearers *are*. See §4.

### Gaps
- MyHeritage last-name pages and Geneanet's surname pages could not be retrieved, so their layout and claims are unverified.
- No real screenshots. Visual style is inferred from text and markup only.
- PESEL dataset metadata is unverified in this session: publication date, whether rare surnames below some threshold are left out, licence and update cadence.
- I did not check whether Forebears or MyHeritage merge diacritic forms (Łuczak/Luczak).
- Other Polish etymology sites and IJP PAN resources were not surveyed because the search budget ran out.

## 2. Digital archive and heritage experiences that feel alive

### Takeaway
Archives feel alive when they are "generous":
- they show the collection before asking for a query
- they let people move through it by similarity, colour, time or chance
- they make the scale visible
- they invite people to take part: indexing, bringing family objects, remixing

The Polish examples add something most international ones lack: memory work where **names and absence are the content**. Examples include Warsaw's walls of names with deliberately blank plaques, Polona's digitised casket of ashes, and Virtual Shtetl rebuilding history one locality at a time.

### Cited Findings (FACT)

**Theory**

- ◆ **Mitchell Whitelaw, "Generous Interfaces for Digital Cultural Collections"** ([DHQ 2015](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/9/1/000205/000205.html))
  - Search is "ungenerous: it withholds information, and demands a query."
  - Generous interfaces are "rich, browsable interfaces that reveal the scale and complexity" of collections. They support "exploration as well as the focused enquiry where search excels" and follow "overview first, zoom and filter, then details-on-demand".
  - "Successful browsing is not a 'task' but a pleasure." He also cites research that 21% of museum visitors want "casual browsing".
  - Case studies:
    - **Manly Images**: 7,000 photographs clustered by decade and title terms, in an animated mosaic that reveals its contents gradually.
    - **Australian Prints + Printmaking**: sortable artist tiles, a decade summary and collaboration networks.
    - **Discover the Queenslander**: a Mosaic view plus a Grid view, with colour palettes extracted automatically.
- ◆ **Johanna Drucker, "Humanities Approaches to Graphical Display"** ([DHQ 2011](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/5/1/000091/000091.html))
  - "Capta is 'taken' actively while data is assumed to be a 'given'."
  - "Humanistic inquiry acknowledges the situated, partial, and constitutive character of knowledge production."
  - Standard charts can "grotesquely distort[] the complexity — but also, the basic ambiguity" of what they show.
  - What she proposes instead:
    - boundaries that can be crossed, dotted or broken lines, and points of varying size
    - scales that are not evenly spaced
    - tone, colour and weight used to carry qualitative meaning
    - containers shaped by the data
    - maps warped by lived experience, and folded timelines

**Polish examples**

- **Polona** (National Library of Poland)
  - ◆ Launched in 2006.
  - ◆ The October 2017 redesign added "advanced search, press panel or private collections".
  - ◆ By 12 October 2017 it held more than 2 million objects (about 863,400 in the public domain), and about 2,000 items were added a day.
  - ◆ **The 2-millionth item was "a casket containing ashes of manuscripts destroyed after the 1944 Warsaw Uprising"**, from the Krasiński Library. Source for these points: [Wikipedia: Polona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polona).
  - ◇ The 2013 relaunch was "designed from scratch". It was responsive, had social features, and showed staff-picked highlights "in a tile-like board resembling Pinterest"; user numbers doubled afterwards ([Spider's Web 2013](https://spidersweb.pl/2013/06/nowa-polona-od-biblioteki-narodowej-to-nie-tylko-pinterest-dla-bibliotekarzy.html); [dzieje.pl](https://dzieje.pl/node/80603)).
  - ◇ A "panel prasy" lets users browse newspapers **by calendar**, and an institutions panel lets other cultural bodies publish on Polona's infrastructure ([dzieje.pl](https://dzieje.pl/node/80603)).
- ◇ **Virtual Shtetl / Wirtualny Sztetl** (POLIN Museum) ([POLIN 2014](https://polin.pl/en/news/2014/12/19/virtual-shtetl); [Salzburg Research case study](https://creativech-toolkit.salzburgresearch.at/case_study/virtual-shtetl-a-community-portal-for-jewish-heritage-in-poland/))
  - It documents local Jewish history "from small towns to large cities", using documents, maps, photographs and recordings.
  - About 1,900 localities are online.
  - Account holders can add text.
  - It describes itself as the world's largest database of Jewish local history.
- **Warsaw Rising Museum**
  - ◇ Its Wall of Remembrance (Mur Pamięci) is 156 m long. Reported name counts depend on the date: 6,000 in an older guide, 10,998 in a later report. New names are checked against documents and added **once a year before 1 August** ([Wprost](https://www.wprost.pl/tygodnik/79438/Przewodnik-po-Muzeum-Powstania-Warszawskiego.html); [Gość Warszawski](https://warszawa.gosc.pl/doc/1759307.100-tys-ofiar-odzyska-imiona)).
  - ◇ At the centre is a steel monument that runs through every floor. The Uprising's calendar is engraved on it and it gives out **the sound of a heartbeat**. With your ear against it you hear the fighting city: shots, songs, "Błyskawica" radio broadcasts ([culture.pl](https://culture.pl/pl/miejsce/muzeum-powstania-warszawskiego)).
  - ◆ The museum runs a contact point (an online form at 1944.pl, plus email, phone and visits) to collect testimony that can put names to the dead. The director: "Obiecuję, że bezimienne ofiary Powstania Warszawskiego przestaną być bezimienne" ("I promise that the nameless victims of the Warsaw Uprising will cease to be nameless") ([Gość Warszawski](https://warszawa.gosc.pl/doc/1759307.100-tys-ofiar-odzyska-imiona)).
- ◇ **Powstańcze Biogramy** (the museum's database of insurgent biographies) grew out of Marek Strok's decades-long collection of 50,000+ names and pseudonyms ([IPN award citation PDF](https://ipn.gov.pl/pl/aktualnosci/konkursy-i-nagrody/nagroda-kustosz-pamieci/2019/71866,Marek-Strok.pdf)).
- ◇ **Izba Pamięci** (a Museum of Warsaw branch at the Warsaw Insurgents' Cemetery) ([Muzeum Warszawy communiqué, Jan 2022](https://muzeumwarszawy.pl/dla-mediow/izba-pamieci_komunikat_styczen_2022); [Izba Pamięci PDF, Aug 2022](https://izbapamieci.muzeumwarszawy.pl/wp-content/uploads/sites/25/2022/08/Izba-Pamięci-IP-sierpien-2022.docx.pdf); [dzieje.pl](https://dzieje.pl/node/142475))
  - Its Wall of Memory carries **62,000+ brass plaques** with the names of those who died in the Uprising, civilians and soldiers alike.
  - It also has **empty plaques, kept for names still to be identified**. The gaps honour the unidentified victims in the meantime.
- ◆ **Museum of Polish History** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Polish_History))
  - It has been in its new building in the Warsaw Citadel since September 2023.
  - It says it will use multimedia to "immerse visitors in the sights, sounds and sensations typical for various eras".
  - Online work includes Google Cultural Institute exhibits ("Separated by History", Solidarity, "Jan Karski: Humanity's Hero") and the project **"Rodziny rozdzielone przez historię" (Families Separated by History)**.

**International examples**

- **Google Arts & Culture Lab**
  - ◆ **X Degrees of Separation** (Mario Klingemann): "lets you choose any two artworks and the computer using Machine Learning will find a visual pathway connecting them through a chain of similar artworks" ([Google blog, 15 Nov 2016](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/arts-culture/experimenting-crossroads-machine-learning-and-arts/)).
  - ◆ **t-SNE Map**: "hundreds of thousands of artworks … organized into one vast 3D landscape" by visual similarity (same post). ◇ The experiments site credits Cyril Diagne, Nicolas Barradeau and Simon Doury ([Experiments, t-SNE tag](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/experiments?tag=t-SNE)).
  - ◆ **Tags**: artworks machine-tagged by what they depict (same post).
  - ◆ **Curator Table** (Diagne & Doury) "applied the principle of laying out prints on a table when planning an exhibition". Works animate in real time in a shared 3D space and can be sorted by artist, style or object. It uses t-SNE and TensorFlow and works on desktop only ([Curator Table](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/curator-table)).
  - ◇ **Free Fall** (Diagne & Barradeau) lets you "explore artworks in one 3 dimensional space" ([Experiments listing](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/search?q=+doury)).
- ◇ **Cooper Hewitt collections site** ([Cooper Hewitt Labs, 2013](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2013/03/28/colorforms/); [Slate 2013](https://www.slate.com/blogs/the_eye/2013/11/19/smithsonian_cooper_hewitt_s_new_beta_website_lets_you_browse_the_online.html); [colour search](http://collection.cooperhewitt.org/objects/colors/))
  - It has a **"random"** button and **search by colour**: each object carries up to five representative colours picked by machine, and you choose from a grid of 117 CSS3 colours.
  - A colour search mixes departments together: drawings, textiles, furniture, posters, matchsafes.
- ◇ **Rijksmuseum Rijksstudio** ([Enfilade 2012](https://enfilade18thc.com/2012/11/01/rijks-studio-offers-125000-images-free-of-copyright/); [Techdirt 2013](https://www.techdirt.com/articles/20130529/19425723249/amsterdams-rijksmuseum-does-digital-archives-right-hi-res-downloads-suite-online-editing-tools.shtml); [Gigazine 2021](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210122-paintings-from-rijksmuseum-free))
  - It launched with 125,000 copyright-free images and openly encouraged remixing (stationery, T-shirts, tattoos).
  - It offers online image tools, and user-curated "studios" sit next to the museum's own selections.
  - It later grew to 700,000+ works under CC0.
- ◇ **Library of Congress Newspaper Navigator** ([LoC press release, 15 Sep 2020](https://www.loc.gov/item/prn-20-060/library-of-congress-launches-new-tool-to-search-historical-newspaper-images/2020-09-15/); [The Signal](https://blogs.loc.gov/thesignal/2020/09/newspaper-navigator-search-application-now-live/))
  - It holds 1.5 million+ images machine-extracted from 16 million Chronicling America pages (1789–1963).
  - You search by keyword, pick some images, then **search by visual similarity**.
  - Built by Ben Lee. It grew out of the 2017 crowdsourcing project "Beyond Words".
- ◆ **Arolsen Archives #everynamecounts** ([Arolsen](https://arolsen-archives.org/en/get-involved/everynamecounts/))
  - Its aim is to "erect a digital memorial".
  - Volunteers transcribe names through a "simple and intuitive" online form, taking "a few minutes" per document. **Each document is processed by three volunteers.**
  - Former director Floriane Azoulay: volunteers "feel an immediate connection to the person whose name they're capturing", and "The reasons for persecution are not history."
- ◇ **Arolsen #StolenMemory** ([Arolsen](https://arolsen-archives.org/en/learn-participate/initiatives-projects/stolenmemory/); [IPN](https://eng.ipn.gov.pl/en/news/9040,Opening-of-the-quotStolenMemoryquot-exhibition-and-handing-over-personal-effects.html))
  - Since 2016 it has been returning personal effects taken from concentration-camp prisoners. About 2,500 items are still held, and several hundred families have been found so far.
  - Most owners came from Poland, Germany and the former USSR, and the search **focuses on Poland**.
  - Items have been handed back in Warsaw, e.g. to the families of Stanisława Mordes and Anna Tomczyk, who were arrested during the Warsaw Uprising.
- ◇ **Europeana Migration** ([Europeana Pro](https://pro.europeana.eu/post/our-migration-collection-days-launch); [People's Collection Wales](https://peoplescollection.wales/blogs/europeana-migration-collection-days-6th-7th-july))
  - It ran during the 2018 European Year of Cultural Heritage.
  - At "collection days", people brought family objects — photos, letters, recipes, even a chair — told their stories, and had the objects digitised on the spot.
  - One event gathered 51 stories and 100 objects spanning 43 countries.

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)
- **"Aliveness" has four ingredients:**
  1. **Motion through relations:** similarity, time, colour, chance.
  2. **Scale made visible:** mosaics, tile boards, 3D fields.
  3. **Participation that is itself verified:** Arolsen's triple-keying, Warsaw's yearly verified additions.
  4. **Material authenticity:** the real scan or object, not an imitation of age.
- **Polish memory institutions treat naming as an open, public process that is checked before anything is added.** Examples: blank plaques waiting for names, a yearly ritual of adding names, a public contact point. This is a direct model for Name Atlas's dataset versioning and its INSUFFICIENT_DATA state: "not yet known" can be shown with dignity instead of hidden.
- **Polona's casket of ashes** shows a national archive presenting destruction and absence as an object in its own right. That legitimises designing "what is missing" as content.
- **Machine-learning similarity maps (t-SNE, Free Fall) create wonder, but their relations are opaque.** For Name Atlas, "neighbouring names" should rest on stated, evidence-based relations (spelling, etymology, grammatical gender), each with its reason shown. The Art Selfie controversy (§4) shows what opaque similarity can do to identity.
- Newspaper Navigator's image-first search and Polona's calendar-based newspaper browsing both put **the primary document itself** in the interface. That fits a product whose historical claims rest on digitised attestations.

## 3. Names, counting, crowds and absence as material in memorials and art

### Takeaway
Memorials and artists turn names and counts into presence in five ways:
1. an **order that carries meaning** (chronology, adjacency)
2. **scale made physical** (grave-sized panels, crowds of figures that each differ, numbers painted until the canvas is white)
3. a **standard compact unit, repeated** (a 10 cm stone, a 3×6 ft panel, a one-page testimony)
4. **the visitor's own reflection or voice** (polished granite, names read aloud, recorded heartbeats)
5. **precise, honest absence** (first names only, town names in place of people, blank plaques)

### Cited Findings (FACT)

**Polish and Polish-context memorials**

- ◇ **Treblinka** ([Muzeum Treblinka](https://muzeumtreblinka.eu/en/informacje/commemoration/); [History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/treblinka-memorial/))
  - 17,000 stones symbolising matzevot stand on 22,000 m² of concrete.
  - **216 stones carry the names of towns** from which Jews were deported. There were 130 at first; more were added in 1998, many from the former Białystok District, "areas which after the Second World War were not within the borders of Poland".
  - Designed by Adam Haupt and Franciszek Duszeńko; the museum also names Franciszek Strynkiewicz.
- ◇ **Bełżec** (memorial opened 2004) ([JTA 2004](https://www.jta.org/2004/06/03/lifestyle/belzec-memorial-opens); [Renewal Art](https://renewalart.pl/en/portfolio/german-extermination-camp-in-belzec/))
  - A path cuts down into the ground to **a wall bearing the first names of Jews**.
  - About 450,000–500,000 people were murdered there between March and December 1942. Only one or two survivors are known, which is why so few names are known.
- ◆ **Nowy Sącz** (monument unveiled 30 Aug 2022) ([Notes from Poland](https://notesfrompoland.com/2022/08/30/polish-town-unveils-monument-naming-its-12000-jews-killed-in-holocaust/); [TVN24](https://tvn24.pl/tvn24-news-in-english/polish-town-of-nowy-sacz-unveils-monument-to-12000-shoah-victims-6088775))
  - It bears **12,000 names** of the town's Jews who were killed.
  - Łukasz Połomski and 20 volunteers compiled the list from Yad Vashem, USHMM, IPN and ŻIH records. **All the names were read aloud** at the unveiling.
  - It was driven by Olympian Dariusz Popiela's project **"People, not numbers"** and crowdfunded with €4,950.
  - Popiela: "Each of us deserves a dignified memory. Which is why it is so important for us to list each victim individually by name."
  - Survivor Sara Melzer: "Here where I stand, I am not alone. I stand [with] 12,000 of my brothers and sisters."
- ◇ **Ośrodek "Brama Grodzka – Teatr NN", Lublin** ([dzieje.pl](https://dzieje.pl/node/114984); [Teatr NN library](https://biblioteka.teatrnn.pl//Content/45276/Brama_Pamieci.pdf); [Teatr NN laudation](https://biblioteka.teatrnn.pl/Content/42801/Laudacja_Nagroda_ks_Musiala.pdf))
  - "Listy do Getta" (Letters to the Ghetto, from 2001) became "Listy do Henia" (Letters to Henio, from 2005). It commemorates the roughly 43,000 Lublin Jews through one boy, Henio Żytomirski (born 1933, died at Majdanek 1942).
  - The **"Lampa Pamięci" (Memory Lamp)** has burned day and night since 2004 for the former Jewish district.
- ◇ **Warsaw names walls**: the Warsaw Rising Museum's Wall of Remembrance and the Izba Pamięci wall of 62,000+ plaques **with empty plaques kept for names not yet identified** (sources in §2).
- ◆ **Katyń Museum** (Warsaw Citadel): nominated in 2017 for the EU Prize for Contemporary Architecture; displays "objects, documents and personal effects" from the massacre sites ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy%C5%84_Museum)). Its design details are unverified (see Gaps).
- ◇ **USHMM Tower of Faces** ([Wikipedia: Yaffa Eliach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaffa_Eliach); [VCU thesis](https://scholarscompass.vcu.edu/etd/3296); [Yad Vashem: Eliach collection](https://collections.yadvashem.org/en/documents/10023565))
  - A three-storey section of the permanent exhibition, devoted to the Jewish community of Eishyshok (Eišiškės), murdered on 25–26 September 1941.
  - The photographs show **life**: bar mitzvahs, weddings, family groups. Yaffa Eliach gathered them from 100+ families; many were taken by her grandparents' photo studio (Yitzhak Uri and Alte Katz).
  - **Sources give different photo counts**: about 1,000 reproductions vs "1,500 photographs". Eliach collected about 6,000 photos in all.

**Polish artists**

- ◆ **Roman Opałka, "OPALKA 1965/1 – ∞"** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Opa%C5%82ka))
  - From 1965 he painted consecutive numbers, starting at 1, on canvases ("details") of 196 × 135 cm.
  - From 1968 the background was grey. From 1972 **each new canvas got 1% more white**, aiming at "white on white".
  - From 1968 he **spoke each number into a tape recorder** and **photographed himself** after each day's work.
  - Last number painted: **5,607,249**. He died on 6 August 2011.
  - Quotes: "All my work is a single thing, the description from number one to infinity. A single thing, a single life"; "My objective is to get up to the white on white and still be alive".
- ◆ **Magdalena Abakanowicz, "Agora"** (Grant Park, Chicago, installed 2006 on permanent loan from the Polish Ministry of Culture) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agora_(sculpture)))
  - **106** headless, armless, hollow iron figures, each 9 ft (2.7 m) tall and about 1,800 lb.
  - Left to rust into a bark-like texture, they mill about like a crowd that visitors walk through.
  - She described crowds as "brainless organisms acting on command, worshiping on command and hating on command."
- ◆ **Zbigniew Rybczyński, "Tango"** (1981, 8 min) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango_(1981_film)))
  - One room. A boy comes in for a ball and leaves; the action repeats "over and over, gradually accompanied by more and more repeating paths of different people through the room". It ends with an old woman walking out.
  - Made on an optical printer with "several hundred thousand exposures", working "sixteen hours a day over seven months".
  - Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film (55th ceremony, 1983).

**International memorials**

- ◆ **Vietnam Veterans Memorial, Maya Lin** ([NYRB, "Making the Memorial", 2000](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2000/11/02/making-the-memorial/))
  - She insisted on chronological order: **"a tally of how many Smiths had died made it clear that an alphabetical listing wouldn't be feasible."**
  - Chronology gives "a returning veteran the ability to find his or her own time frame on the wall."
  - "The wall dematerializes as a form and allows the names to become the object, a pure and reflective surface that would allow visitors the chance to see themselves with the names."
  - "The use of names was a way to bring back everything someone could remember about a person."
  - "I found myself searching out the name of a friend's father and touching it."
  - ◇ The first (1959) and last (1975) casualties meet at the vertex, so those who died together stay together ([Wilson Quarterly](https://www.wilsonquarterly.com/quarterly/undefined/monumental-time); [Berkeleyan 1995](https://newsarchive.berkeley.edu/news/berkeleyan/1995/0315/lin.html)).
- ◆ **9/11 Memorial names, "meaningful adjacencies"** ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/september-11-memorial))
  - **2,983 names** on bronze panels. About **1,200 adjacency requests** came from loved ones.
  - Names are nested: pool → group (a tower, a flight, first responders, the Pentagon, the 1993 bombing) → affiliation (e.g. Cantor Fitzgerald, 704 names; FDNY Ladder 3) → clusters of requested adjacencies.
  - Jer Thorp and Local Projects built a two-stage algorithm:
    - Stage 1 clusters the requests into "a pile of really irregularly shaped puzzle pieces", some with 70+ names.
    - Stage 2 is a space-filler that "takes the big pieces and finds a place for them, and then fills in the gaps with the small pieces".
  - Designers could adjust the layout by hand through a manual interface.
  - Jake Barton: "You get this incredibly powerful sense of undifferentiation. Based on the event, all these names are equal."
  - ◇ A blurb for Thorp's book says his layout satisfied **99.99%** of adjacencies, against about 93% for a rival analysts' team ([Living in Data listing](https://lehmanns.de/shop/technik/53838152-9780374189907-living-in-data); secondary source). Thorp's own blog post was unreachable (HTTP 526) and the 9/11 Memorial's names-arrangement page returned 404.
- ◇ **Yad Vashem Hall of Names** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_of_Names); [Yad Vashem](https://www.yadvashem.org/museum/holocaust-history-museum/hall-of-names); [Pages of Testimony](https://www.yadvashem.org/archive/hall-of-names/pages-of-testimony))
  - Two 10 m cones. The upper cone shows about 600 photographs and fragments of Pages of Testimony.
  - About 2.7 million names are recorded on **one-page testimonies** in 20+ languages, submitted by relatives and others.
  - More than 4.9 million victims' names were held as of 2024. The online Central Database launched in 2004.
- ◇ **Stolpersteine** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolperstein); [The Local](https://www.thelocal.de/20160503/20-years-since-the-first-stolpersteine-were-laid); [Jüdische Allgemeine](https://www.juedische-allgemeine.de/politik/stolpergefahr-in-muenchen); [Tablet](https://www.tabletmag.com/scroll/192696/plan-for-holocaust-memorial-stumbling-stones-takes-a-tumble))
  - Gunter Demnig began them in 1992. Each small brass-topped stone is set at the victim's last freely chosen address and gives a name, dates and fate.
  - The **100,000th** was laid in Nuremberg on 26 May 2023, for Johann Wild. The project is called "the world's largest decentralized memorial".
  - Its guiding idea, from the Talmud: "a person is only forgotten when his or her name is forgotten".
  - **Munich bans them on public ground** (decided 2004, upheld 2015). Charlotte Knobloch called them "dishonorable and impious" because people stand on the names.
- ◇ **AIDS Memorial Quilt** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAMES_Project_AIDS_Memorial_Quilt); [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/names-project-aids-memorial-quilt-now-online))
  - Each panel is **3 × 6 ft, "approximately the size of the average grave"**, and is made by loved ones.
  - 48,000+ panels and 94,000+ names, which is only about 20% of those lost. It weighs about 54 tons and covers 1.3 million sq ft; seeing every panel for one minute would take 33+ days.
  - The whole quilt is online and searchable.
- ◇ **Ellis Island American Immigrant Wall of Honor** ([ellisisland.org](https://ellisisland.org/wall-of-honor-overview/); [Duke Migration Memorials](https://migrationmemorials.trinity.duke.edu/items/american-immigrant-wall-honor-%C2%AE.html))
  - Unveiled in 1990. Names are etched on stainless-steel panels **paid for by donors**: $300 for a single line (up to 40 characters); "Leadership" inscriptions from $1,000.
  - It includes immigrants of all eras and ports of entry, not only people who came through Ellis Island.
  - The totals reported conflict: "nearly one million" names vs 500,000+ on the circular wall and 56,000 on the straight wall.

**International artists**

- ◇ **On Kawara, "One Million Years"** ([Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/reading-years-for-on-kawara); [Halle für Kunst](https://halle-fuer-kunst.at/en/exhibitions/on-kawara-reading-one-million-years); [Hyperallergic on Guggenheim readers](https://hyperallergic.com/guggenheim-seeks-volunteer-readers-for-on-kawara-retrospective))
  - Two sets of ten binders: 2,000 pages, 500 years per page. "Past" was made in 1970–71, "Future" in 1980–98.
  - Since 1993 it has been **read aloud live** by two readers, each session picking up where the last stopped.
  - For the Guggenheim's "On Kawara—Silence" (2015), volunteers read in the rotunda.
- ◇ **Christian Boltanski, "Les Archives du Cœur"** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Archives_du_C%C5%93ur); [Benesse Art Site](https://benesse-artsite.jp/en/news/20150226-365.html); [Domus 2010](https://domusweb.it/en/art/2010/06/24/christian-boltanski-a-sentimental-minimalist.html))
  - An archive of **recorded heartbeats**, permanently housed on Teshima island (Japan): about 80,000 now, 30,000 in 2010.
  - Travelling booths let visitors record their own heartbeat and add it.
- ◇ **Mimi Ọnụọha, "The Library of Missing Datasets"** (2016) ([artist site](https://mimionuoha.com/the-library-of-missing-datasets); [ZKM](https://zkm.de/en/node/5972))
  - A filing cabinet of **empty folders** labelled with datasets that do not exist.
  - "The word 'missing' is inherently normative. It implies both a lack and an ought." Missing data tends to track whoever is most vulnerable.

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)
- **Lin's "Smiths" line is the single most relevant sentence for Name Atlas.** A shared surname is not a shared identity. The more common the name, the more a list of its bearers erases individuals, so the design has to make "many unrelated people share this" readable.
- **9/11 adjacency: the order itself is meaning.** But those adjacencies were **requested and consented to by families**, not inferred. Name Atlas can place names next to each other only by *stated, evidence-based* relations (variant, gender form, cognate). Never by assumed kinship.
- **Opałka, Tango and Agora** show three ways to make a large number felt:
  - accumulation with gradual change (1% whiter per canvas)
  - layering in one shared space over time
  - a crowd in which every figure differs
- **Abakanowicz's own warning** about crowds acting "on command" means crowd imagery must avoid reading as a marching, uniform mass.
- **Polish memorials handle absence with unusual precision:**
  - town names instead of people (Treblinka)
  - first names only, because full names were lost (Bełżec)
  - blank plaques waiting for names (Izba Pamięci)
  - names read aloud to turn numbers back into people (Nowy Sącz, "People, not numbers")

  Each is an exact, honest way to show *how much is known*. That maps closely onto VERIFIED / INSUFFICIENT_DATA thinking.
- **Border shifts show up even in memorial lists** (Treblinka's 1998 additions came from areas outside post-war Poland). Any place labelling in Name Atlas needs period-correct and present-day names side by side.
- **Participation creates presence:** relatives filling in Pages of Testimony, panels sewn by loved ones, visitors recording heartbeats, volunteers reading years or names aloud. But **paid presence** (Ellis Island's $300 line) turns commemoration into a product. That is a model to avoid.

## 4. Ethics and sensitivity: evoking the past without exploiting trauma or implying identities

### Takeaway
The guidance agrees on the basics:
- make history individual, but don't invent
- never simulate suffering
- use graphic material sparingly
- be precise with language
- don't romanticise
- label anything synthetic

For a Polish surname product the sharpest risk is **inferring identity from a surname**. In Poland, lists that "expose" public figures' supposedly "real Jewish surnames" are an established antisemitic practice. After that come heraldic and ancestral overclaiming, trauma imagery used as atmosphere, and nostalgic kitsch that passes myth off as tradition.

### Cited Findings (FACT)

**Polish-specific**

- ◇ **Antisemitic "real surname" lists** ([Wikipedia: Polonica.net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonica.net))
  - The portal Polonica.net published a "Lista Żydów w zniewalanej Polsce" ("List of Jews in enslaved Poland"). It set supposedly "real Jewish surnames" beside the names of well-known Poles.
  - Examples: ex-president Aleksander Kwaśniewski shown as "Izaak Stoltzman"; Jacek Kuroń as "Icek Kordblum".
  - The list even included Henryk Batuta, a fictional person.
- ◆ **Polish heraldry is clan-based, not surname-based** ([Wikipedia: Polish heraldry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_heraldry))
  - "A number of unrelated families (sometimes hundreds of them), usually with a number of different family names, may use the same, undifferenced coat of arms."
  - There were only about 160 arms in the late Middle Ages (Piekosiński).
  - Nobles were referred to by both their family name and their arms/clan name.
- ◇ **Borders moved**: Treblinka's 1998 additions came from towns "not within the borders of Poland" after the war ([Muzeum Treblinka](https://muzeumtreblinka.eu/en/informacje/commemoration/)).
- ◇ **Arolsen's search for relatives focuses on Poland**, because many of the persecuted were Polish, including people arrested in the Warsaw Uprising and forced labourers ([Arolsen #StolenMemory](https://arolsen-archives.org/en/learn-participate/initiatives-projects/stolenmemory/)).

**International guidance and cautionary cases**

- ◆ **USHMM "Guidelines for Teaching About the Holocaust"** ([USHMM](https://www.ushmm.org/en/teach/fundamentals/guidelines-for-teaching-the-holocaust)), verbatim:
  - **"Translate Statistics Into People"**: "In any study of the Holocaust, the sheer number of victims challenges comprehension. Show that individual people—grandparents, parents, and children—are behind the statistics."
  - **"Avoid Simple Answers to Complex Questions"**: "Be wary of simplification. Seek instead to convey the nuances of this history."
  - **"Avoid Comparisons of Pain."**
  - **"Make Responsible Methodological Choices"**: "Simulating experiences from the Holocaust remains pedagogically unsound…"
  - **"Strive for Precision of Language."**
  - **"Avoid Romanticizing History"**: warns that "an overemphasis on heroic actions can result in an inaccurate and unbalanced account."
  - ◇ Graphic material "should be used judiciously", chosen so as to avoid "exploiting students' emotional vulnerability or being disrespectful to the victims" (search summary of the same page).
- ◇ **Landecker Digital Memory Lab** (University of Sussex; Prof. Victoria Grace Walden) published recommendation reports in 2024 on using VR, AR and computer games for Holocaust memory. The lab, founded in 2024, aims to build digital literacy across Holocaust museums, memorials and archives ([Digital Holocaust Memory: recommendations post](https://reframe.sussex.ac.uk/digitalholocaustmemory/2024/07/11/shaping-the-future-use-of-vr-ar-and-computer-games-in-holocaust-memory/); [project home](https://reframe.sussex.ac.uk/digitalholocaustmemory/)).
- ◇ **Coats of arms belong to people, not surnames.** The College of Arms is quoted as saying "There is no such thing as a 'coat of arms for a surname'". Selling a "family crest" by surname is widely called a scam in genealogy circles ([CAGenWeb](https://www.cagenweb.org/plumas/nosuchthing.htm); [Legacy Tree](https://www.legacytree.com/da/blog/family-coat-of-arms-uk-commonwealth)). The crest seller in §1 shows no disclaimer ([House of Names](https://www.houseofnames.com/nowak-family-crest)).
- ◇ **The Ellis Island name-change myth**: "no names were written down at Ellis Island, and thus no names were changed there". Names came from ship manifests, and changes happened later, usually at naturalisation. The myth was popularised by *The Godfather Part II* ([JewishGen](https://www.jewishgen.org/InfoFiles/ellismythnames.htm); [ALA DttP](https://journals.ala.org/index.php/dttp/article/view/6655)).
- ◇ **Ancestry's "Inseparable" ad** (April 2019) was pulled after criticism that it romanticised slavery by showing an interracial romance at a time when such unions were overwhelmingly coerced. Ancestry apologised ([TheGrio](https://thegrio.com/2019/04/19/ancestry-com-slavery-advertisement); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/ancestrycom-pulls-ad-accused-whitewashing-slavery)).
- ◇ **MyHeritage "Deep Nostalgia"** (2021, built on D-ID) animates faces in old photos. Reception was split: "Some people love … others find it creepy". Critics pointed out that the dead cannot consent. The tool does not generate speech, although MyHeritage made a talking-Lincoln promo ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/myheritage-creepy-deepfake-tool)).
- ◇ **Google Arts & Culture "Art Selfie"** (2018) matched faces to portraits. Users of colour got limited or stereotyped matches: servants, "exoticised" figures, sexualised novelties. Google said it was "limited by the images we have… Historical artworks often don't reflect the diversity of the world." ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/21/why-inclusion-in-the-google-arts-culture-selfie-feature-matters/); [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/arts/13819312/google-arts-and-culture-selfie-app-inherits-art-world-disparities)).
- ◇ **Names underfoot**: Munich's ban on Stolpersteine, on the grounds that people step on the names (see §3) ([Jüdische Allgemeine](https://www.juedische-allgemeine.de/politik/stolpergefahr-in-muenchen)).
- ◇ **Svetlana Boym's two nostalgias** ([extracts from *The Future of Nostalgia*](https://base.milano.it/en/extracts-from-an-adoptation-and-elaboration-of-the-future-of-nostalgia-by-svetlana-boym-2001-as-published-on-monumenttotransformation-org/); [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/time-travelling-apollo/201606/the-two-faces-nostalgia)):
  - Restorative nostalgia "does not think of itself as nostalgia, but rather as truth and tradition."
  - Reflective nostalgia dwells on "the irrevocability of the past and human finitude."
- ◆ **Surname-to-identity inference in today's products** (§1):
  - Forebears: "predominantly Catholic (43%) in Ukraine"; party registration; salary.
  - Ancestry: an "Ethnicity Percentage Map"; "your Nowak ancestors".
- ◆ **Graphics that look certain hide interpretation**: Drucker, §2 ([DHQ 2011](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/5/1/000091/000091.html)).

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)
- **Separate the word's origin from the bearers' identity.** "Derived from Polish *nowy* 'new'" is a claim about a word. "A Jewish / German / Ukrainian surname" reads as a claim about people. In Poland that framing has been used for antisemitic "outing" lists. Name Atlas copy must talk about languages and word roots, never about which groups bearers belong to. It should also never answer "is my name Jewish / German / noble?" style queries with a label.
- **"Translate statistics into people" has to be done without inventing people.** Name Atlas cannot tell stories about individuals (hard rule). It can still give numbers presence through abstract, individually varied marks, sound and scale — see Patterns. No faces, no biographies.
- **Surface victim databases with great care.** A surname match against Yad Vashem, Arolsen or Warsaw Uprising lists does not show family connection (Lin's Smiths). If the historical dimension ever touches 20th-century records, the language must be exact and must never suggest the user's relatives were victims or perpetrators.
- **Aim for reflective, not restorative, nostalgia.** Sepia, parchment and heraldry present myth as tradition. A reflective register is honest about loss and gaps and about time passing.
- **Evoke trauma only through restraint and abstraction**, as memorials do: heartbeat, light, blank space. Never through literal iconography (barbed wire, rails, smoke) or anything simulated.
- **Label synthetic media, or better, avoid it.** Animated ancestors and face-matching features have both drawn public backlash (Deep Nostalgia, Art Selfie).

- No source retrieved in this session for:
  - how the March 1968 campaign used surnames and "origin"
  - IPN-catalogue name-matching risks
  - guidance on terminology for German camps in occupied Poland

## Transferable patterns for a surname-discovery product
(All INTERPRETATION. Each bullet links to the evidence it draws on.)

1. **Show the field before the query.** Open on a living, browsable overview, such as all Polish surnames as a field of marks, and let the typed name *emerge* from it rather than sit behind a bare search box. Evidence: [Whitelaw on search as "ungenerous"](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/9/1/000205/000205.html); [Polona's tile board](https://spidersweb.pl/2013/06/nowa-polona-od-biblioteki-narodowej-to-nie-tylko-pinterest-dla-bibliotekarzy.html).
2. **Make the count a presence.** Draw the PESEL count as discrete marks that each look slightly different (one mark per person, or a stated scale), always with the dataset date. Evidence: [USHMM "Translate Statistics Into People"](https://www.ushmm.org/en/teach/fundamentals/guidelines-for-teaching-the-holocaust); [Abakanowicz's crowd of individual figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agora_(sculpture)); [the Quilt's grave-sized unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAMES_Project_AIDS_Memorial_Quilt).
3. **Reveal by accumulation, like a developing photograph.** Build the result in layers, and tie each stage to a real evidence step: canonical form → count → rarity → meaning → attestations. Evidence: [Tango's layered loops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango_(1981_film)); [Opałka's 1% lightening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Opa%C5%82ka); [Manly Images' progressive mosaic](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/9/1/000205/000205.html).
4. **The name is the object.** Make the canonical form, with its diacritics, the typographic hero, not a caption to an illustration. Evidence: [Maya Lin: "allows the names to become the object"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2000/11/02/making-the-memorial/).
5. **Diacritics are identity: show variants as neighbours, never merge them.** Łuczak and Luczak appear as distinct, adjacent entities, each with its own count. Evidence: [Geneteka needs an "exact search" box](https://geneteka.genealodzy.pl/index.php?op=gt&lang=pol&w=07mz&rid=B&search_lastname=nowak&search_name=&search_lastname2=&search_name2=&from_date=&to_date=); [Behind the Name keeps Novák, Novak and Nowak apart as cognates](https://surnames.behindthename.com/name/nowak).
6. **Meaningful adjacency, with the reason written on the link.** Place a surname among evidence-based neighbours — feminine/masculine forms, spelling variants, cognates, same-root families like Nowak / Nowakowski / Nowicki — and print why each link exists. Evidence: [9/11 "meaningful adjacencies" came from stated requests](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/september-11-memorial).
7. **Where a name is characteristic, not just where people live.** Map over-representation relative to the local population rather than raw density. Evidence: [UCL "named"](https://named.publicprofiler.org/); [nazwiska-polskie "najbardziej charakterystyczne" (20×/25×)](https://nazwiska-polskie.pl/Nowak).
8. **Absence as a designed, dignified state.** INSUFFICIENT_DATA gets a visible reserved space that explains what was searched and not found. It never collapses to "0" or disappears. Evidence: [Izba Pamięci's empty plaques](https://muzeumwarszawy.pl/dla-mediow/izba-pamieci_komunikat_styczen_2022); [Ọnụọha's empty folders](https://mimionuoha.com/the-library-of-missing-datasets); [Bełżec's first names](https://www.jta.org/2004/06/03/lifestyle/belzec-memorial-opens).
9. **Confidence as a visual property of the mark, not only a badge.** VERIFIED is crisp and solid; LIKELY and POSSIBLE have softer, permeable edges; CONFLICTING is two forms present at once; UNCONFIRMED is an outline. Evidence: [Drucker's permeable boundaries and weight/tone](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/5/1/000091/000091.html).
10. **Competing hypotheses shown as equals.** Use a layout that holds both origins side by side, with no winner implied by position or size, until evidence decides. Evidence: [Barton: "all these names are equal"](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/september-11-memorial); [USHMM "Avoid Simple Answers"](https://www.ushmm.org/en/teach/fundamentals/guidelines-for-teaching-the-holocaust).
11. **Real primary evidence, not imitation age.** Show the actual Polona scan crop with the attestation highlighted, dated and linked. Authentic material replaces decorative "old paper". Evidence: [Newspaper Navigator's image-first search](https://blogs.loc.gov/thesignal/2020/09/newspaper-navigator-search-application-now-live/); [Polona's press calendar](https://dzieje.pl/node/80603).
12. **Serendipity on rails.** Offer "random surname" and "walk to a neighbouring name" journeys, always along labelled evidence relations. Evidence: [Cooper Hewitt random and colour search](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2013/03/28/colorforms/); [X Degrees of Separation pathways](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/arts-culture/experimenting-crossroads-machine-learning-and-arts/).
13. **Time as one continuous axis.** Earliest attestation → present-day count on a single line, so users can "find their time" in the name's history. Evidence: [Lin's chronological wall](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2000/11/02/making-the-memorial/); [Opałka's lifelong count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Opa%C5%82ka).
14. **One standard unit, repeated.** Render every surname in the same compact format so results can be compared, collected and shared. Evidence: [Stolperstein's fixed format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolperstein); [Yad Vashem's one-page testimony](https://www.yadvashem.org/archive/hall-of-names/pages-of-testimony); [the Quilt panel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAMES_Project_AIDS_Memorial_Quilt).
15. **Share cards that carry their own provenance.** Build the canonical name, count and dataset date, confidence status and source line into the share image, so a screenshot cannot be stripped of its status. Evidence: [contrast with Forebears' undated figures](https://forebears.io/surnames/nowak).
16. **Voice and pronunciation as presence.** A restrained "hear the name" moment (IPA plus audio) gives presence without imagery, and helps diaspora users who never learned how to say it. Evidence: [Behind the Name /ˈnɔ.vak/](https://surnames.behindthename.com/name/nowak); [names read aloud in Nowy Sącz](https://notesfrompoland.com/2022/08/30/polish-town-unveils-monument-naming-its-12000-jews-killed-in-holocaust/); [On Kawara readings](https://hyperallergic.com/reading-years-for-on-kawara).
17. **Ambient rhythm only when it is driven by data.** A subtle pulse or density tied to real numbers can feel alive; decorative motion cannot. Evidence: [Warsaw Rising Museum heartbeat monolith](https://culture.pl/pl/miejsce/muzeum-powstania-warszawskiego); [Boltanski's heartbeat archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Archives_du_C%C5%93ur).
18. **Reflection without lineage.** Show the user their name among the N others who carry it ("you share this name with…"), and say explicitly that this does not mean family. Evidence: [Lin's reflective surface and the "Smiths"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2000/11/02/making-the-memorial/).
19. **Make versioning and verification visible.** Show the dataset date, when the next update is due, and a changelog. Show how each claim was checked: how many sources, and whether they agree. Evidence: [the Warsaw wall adds names yearly after verification](https://warszawa.gosc.pl/doc/1759307.100-tys-ofiar-odzyska-imiona); [Arolsen's triple-keying](https://arolsen-archives.org/en/get-involved/everynamecounts/).
20. **Overview → zoom → detail as the navigation grammar.** National field → one name → one attestation → the source, without losing the user's sense of where they are. Evidence: [Whitelaw](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/9/1/000205/000205.html).
21. **Participation without identity claims.** Invite users to *suggest a source or attestation* for review, with the review status visible, rather than to assert family facts. Evidence: [Europeana collection days](https://pro.europeana.eu/post/our-migration-collection-days-launch); [#everynamecounts](https://arolsen-archives.org/en/get-involved/everynamecounts/).
22. **Place names that are honest about borders.** Give historical places both period and present-day names and states, because borders moved. Evidence: [Treblinka's town stones and the 1998 additions](https://muzeumtreblinka.eu/en/informacje/commemoration/).

## Tropes and risks to avoid
(All INTERPRETATION, grounded in the cited evidence.)

1. **Sepia, parchment, torn edges, wax seals, faux-aged "old documents", typewriter or blackletter pastiche.** This is restorative nostalgia that presents myth as "truth and tradition" ([Boym](https://base.milano.it/en/extracts-from-an-adoptation-and-elaboration-of-the-future-of-nostalgia-by-svetlana-boym-2001-as-published-on-monumenttotransformation-org/)), and it clashes with the owner's rejection of the "warm paper" direction.
2. **Family crests, coats of arms or a "your herb" reveal.** Arms belong to individuals, and in Poland to clans shared across many unrelated surnames ([Polish heraldry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_heraldry); [CAGenWeb on the College of Arms](https://www.cagenweb.org/plumas/nosuchthing.htm); [House of Names](https://www.houseofnames.com/nowak-family-crest)).
3. **"Your ancestors" copy, family-tree visuals, "Where is the X family from?"** These treat every bearer as kin ([Ancestry](https://www.ancestry.com/name-origin?surname=nowak); [Lin's "Smiths"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2000/11/02/making-the-memorial/)).
4. **Identity inference from a surname:** religion percentages, party registration, income, "ethnicity maps", or labels like "Jewish / German / Ukrainian surname". In Poland this echoes antisemitic "real surname" lists ([Forebears](https://forebears.io/surnames/nowak); [Polonica.net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonica.net)).
5. **Invented narrative:** unsourced "first found in…" origins, the Ellis Island name-change myth, romanticised historical vignettes ([House of Names](https://www.houseofnames.com/nowak-family-crest); [JewishGen](https://www.jewishgen.org/InfoFiles/ellismythnames.htm); [Ancestry "Inseparable"](https://thegrio.com/2019/04/19/ancestry-com-slavery-advertisement)).
6. **Synthetic faces, voices or "portraits of your name"; animated ancestors; face-matching** ([Deep Nostalgia](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/myheritage-creepy-deepfake-tool); [Art Selfie bias](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/21/why-inclusion-in-the-google-arts-culture-selfie-feature-matters/)).
7. **Trauma iconography as atmosphere** (barbed wire, rails, smoke, ghetto walls, decorative candles), **simulation of any kind**, and **comparisons of suffering** ([USHMM](https://www.ushmm.org/en/teach/fundamentals/guidelines-for-teaching-the-holocaust)).
8. **Showing absence as zero.** "0 records" style states hide the gap between "not in our sources" and "does not exist" ([FamilySearch](https://www.familysearch.org/en/surname?surname=nowak); [Ọnụọha](https://mimionuoha.com/the-library-of-missing-datasets)).
9. **Undated, unsourced, falsely precise counts, or a hidden methodology** (268,732 vs ~170,000 for the same name; a "© 2026" footer with no method) ([Forebears](https://forebears.io/surnames/nowak); [nazwiska-polskie.pl](https://nazwiska-polskie.pl/Nowak)).
10. **Mixing user-submitted etymologies with dictionary ones, or running prize contests for meanings** ([Forebears' $60 DNA-test contest](https://forebears.io/surnames/nowak)).
11. **Upsells next to claims** (DNA kits, free trials, crest merchandise). They undercut the credibility of every claim nearby ([Ancestry](https://www.ancestry.com/name-origin?surname=nowak); [House of Names](https://www.houseofnames.com/nowak-family-crest)).
12. **Red-to-yellow "hotspot" heat-maps** that mostly show population density and suggest danger, and **maps with anachronistic borders** ([Forebears legend](https://forebears.io/surnames/nowak); [Treblinka border note](https://muzeumtreblinka.eu/en/informacje/commemoration/)).
13. **Selling presence:** paid "add your name" placements, or rarity leaderboards that turn names into status ([Ellis Island $300 inscriptions](https://ellisisland.org/wall-of-honor-overview/)).
14. **Names underfoot:** interactions that trample, scatter, shoot or "pop" names, especially where historical persons appear ([Munich Stolpersteine objection](https://www.juedische-allgemeine.de/politik/stolpergefahr-in-muenchen)).
15. **Crowds drawn as a marching, uniform mass, or national-romantic kitsch** (flags, eagles). Abakanowicz warned of crowds acting "on command" ([Agora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agora_(sculpture))).
16. **Unmoderated comment threads** that invite nationalist or joke content ([nazwiska-polskie "Ten kraj należy do nas!"](https://nazwiska-polskie.pl/Nowak)).
17. **Opaque machine-learning similarity presented as meaning.** Visual or letter similarity is not a relation; always label what a link is based on ([t-SNE Map](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/arts-culture/experimenting-crossroads-machine-learning-and-arts/); [Art Selfie](https://www.kqed.org/arts/13819312/google-arts-and-culture-selfie-app-inherits-art-world-disparities)).
18. **Surname matches in victim or other historical databases shown as if they concern the user's family.** A shared name is not a shared fate ([Yad Vashem names database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_of_Names); [Lin's "Smiths"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2000/11/02/making-the-memorial/)).
