# Polish and Central-European visual culture as source material for a modern, abstract, non-kitsch Name Atlas identity

Research date: 2026-09-30. How to read these notes:

- **Cited Findings = FACT.** Every bullet carries an inline link. Tags: **[fetched]** means the page content was read directly. **[snippet]** means the fact comes from the search engine's extract of that page, either because the page is JS-rendered (all Culture.pl article bodies were unreadable to both fetch tools) or because it was not fetched. **[original analysis]** means the researcher computed it from the cited primary files, and the method is stated.
- **Inferences = INTERPRETATION.** These are design readings, not facts. **KITSCH RISK** and **RULE RISK** flags mark places where a source could pull the product towards folk kitsch, heraldry, sepia nostalgia, nobility or ancestry claims, or ethnicity/religion inference, all of which Name Atlas forbids.
- The session's shared web-search quota ran out partway through. Items that could not be verified afterwards are listed under Gaps and were not guessed.

## 1. Polish avant-garde and modernism: which principles express "a name shared by many people across time"?

### Takeaway
The Polish avant-garde gives unusually direct, non-figurative metaphors for Name Atlas:
- counting as the trace of a life (Opałka);
- a crowd as "variants of a prototype" with no exact repetition (Abakanowicz);
- many lives looping in one room without merging (Rybczyński);
- vision as historically conditioned, with afterimages as memory (Strzemiński);
- rhythm calculated from 8:5 (Kobro);
- mechanical texture built from simple elements (Berlewi);
- the frame as a "perceptive background" completed by its users (Hansen);
- long-duration observation from a fixed viewpoint (Robakowski).

All of these can be rendered from data alone, with no folk, heraldic or sepia imagery.

### Cited Findings

#### Władysław Strzemiński: Unism, Theory of Vision, Afterimages, lettering
- He set out the theory of Unism in *Unism in Painting* (1928) — [Culture.pl artist page, snippet](https://culture.pl/en/artist/wladyslaw-strzeminski).
- MoMA's post describes Unism, developed with Katarzyna Kobro, as seeking "the greatest possible union of forms with the plane of the picture", putting organic coherence ahead of dynamic contrast and treating the painting as a material object. The post is by Meghan Forbes, 30 May 2018 — [MoMA post, fetched](https://post.moma.org/?p=1964).
- The manuscript of *Theory of Vision* was finished in 1947. The Communist authorities suppressed it until 1958, six years after his death — [MoMA post, fetched](https://post.moma.org/?p=1964).
- Two quotations from *Theory of Vision* — [MoMA post, fetched](https://post.moma.org/?p=1964):
  - "the faculty of seeing, visual consciousness, cannot be formed outside its relation to history, the specific development of the forces of production and the class struggle"
  - "Realism is not a Platonic metaphysical absolute but the historically evolving process of the development of the human cognitive faculty"
- Culture.pl defines an afterimage as "an internal picture that appears on the retina after a moment of gazing at an object reflecting light or at a source of light itself". It says the series "expressed the desire to grasp human memory (history) through the eye and to crystallise it in the spatial structure of the painting" — [Culture.pl, *Sun's Afterimage*, snippet](https://culture.pl/en/work/suns-afterimage-wladyslaw-strzeminski).
- His *Neoplastic Room* was installed in the Łódź museum in 1948 and removed in 1950 under socialist-realist pressure. Andrzej Wajda's last film, *Afterimage* (2016), is about him — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_Strzemi%C5%84ski).
- Lettering, from a 1932 sketch — [Typoteka, *Komunikat FA*, fetched](https://typoteka.pl/en/typeface/komunikat-fa):
  - Strzemiński held that "modern economic letter forms should be standardized and based on lines and arches" and that "readability is a matter of habit".
  - In 2004 the Fontarte studio turned the sketch into *Komunikat FA*: full character set including **Polish diacritics**, Regular/Bold/Black weights, built from circles, circular segments and straight lines.
- The multi-script POLIN Sans (2016–2024) takes Strzemiński's "Alphabet a.r." as its explicit source — [Typoteka, *POLIN Sans*, fetched](https://typoteka.pl/pl/typeface/polin-sans).

#### Katarzyna Kobro
- In *Space Compositions: Space–Time Rhythm and Its Calculations* (1931), Kobro and Strzemiński described the mathematics of open spatial compositions using an **8:5 ratio** — [EVL "Virtual Unism" paper, snippet](https://www.evl.uic.edu/virtualunism/VirtualUnismFinalPR10-12-05.doc).
- Her model of sculpture was "open to infinity through the rhythm based on the golden section and the ratio 8/5". The 5:8 ratio became an "inviolable principle" of her compositions, and for her the movement of the human body through space and time generates the form — [Culture.pl, snippet](https://culture.pl/en/artist/katarzyna-kobro); [EVL, snippet](https://www.evl.uic.edu/virtualunism/VirtualUnismFinalPR10-12-05.doc).

#### Henryk Berlewi: Mechano-Faktura
- *Mechano-Faktura* was published in March 1924 (worked on since 1922), with a preface by Aleksander Wat — [Wikipedia, snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Berlewi); [berlewi.com, snippet](https://berlewi.com/biography/). It:
  - rejects the illusion of space in favour of two-dimensionality;
  - reduces colour to **black, white and red**;
  - builds images "by mechanical means using rhythmic arrangements of lines and simple geometrical forms such as circles and squares".
- The first show was held at the Austro-Daimler car showroom in Warsaw. In summer 1924 Herwarth Walden showed the work at Der Sturm in Berlin — [Wikipedia, snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Berlewi).

#### Mieczysław Szczuka and Teresa Żarnower (the Blok group)
- Szczuka (1898–1927) worked in book illustration, stage design, photomontage and abstract film. He promoted utilitarianism, belonged to the Blok group, and died climbing Zamarła Turnia in 1927 — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mieczys%C5%82aw_Szczuka).
- Żarnower (1897–1949) — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teresa_%C5%BBarnower%C3%B3wna):
  - co-created the Blok group, "the first Polish constructivist artistic group", and co-edited *Blok* until 1926;
  - edited Szczuka's magazine *Dźwignia* from his death until July 1928;
  - completed the cover of Anatol Stern's *Europa* in 1929;
  - designed 1928 election posters;
  - made a photomontage cycle for *The Defense of Warsaw*.

#### The a.r. group and Muzeum Sztuki, Łódź
- Members were the artists Strzemiński, Kobro and Henryk Stażewski and the poets Julian Przyboś and Jan Brzękowski — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_Strzemi%C5%84ski).
- The International Collection of Modern Art opened on 15 February 1931 with 111 works donated by avant-garde artists (Léger, Ernst, Arp and Schwitters among them). It is described as the first museum in Europe, and the second in the world after MoMA, dedicated to modern art — [Wikipedia, snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Art,_%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA).

#### Wojciech Fangor
- He made more than 400 blurred, soft-edged, quasi-geometric paintings between 1958 and 1978, first in black and white, then in colour — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Fangor).
- *Study of Space* (1958), made with Stanisław Zamecznik, was a "painting environment" built on "positive illusory space" — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Fangor).
- In 1970 he became the first Polish artist to have a solo exhibition at the Guggenheim — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Fangor).
- He co-founded the Polish School of Posters and made about 100 posters between 1953 and 1961, using "handwritten text, framed though as a painting, in a three-colour palette scheme" — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Fangor).
- **Warsaw Metro Line 2** carries his lettering of the station names (Wikipedia dates the design to 2007) — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Fangor):
  - "strong, almost psychedelic, use of aggressive, suffused colours", with uppercase letters angled to suggest the train's movement — [Icon, snippet](https://www.iconeye.com/design/warsaw-metro);
  - Culture.pl calls the seven stations "the largest underground Op art gallery" — [Culture.pl, snippet](https://culture.pl/en/article/stations-as-canvas-painting-the-warsaw-metro).

#### Roman Opałka: *OPALKA 1965/1–∞*
Sources for this subsection: [DailyArt, snippet](https://www.dailyartmagazine.com/?p=132344); [Pinault Collection, snippet](https://lesoeuvres.pinaultcollection.com/en/node/3465).
- He conceived the project at Café Bristol in Warsaw: to paint the numbers from one to infinity, canvas after canvas, for the rest of his life.
- From 1972, when he reached 1,000,000, he added 1% more white to each new background, aiming to paint white on white ("blanc mérité") by 7,777,777.
- From 1968 he spoke each number aloud in Polish into a tape recorder while painting it.
- The work comprises 233 paintings. The last number he painted was **5,607,249**.

#### Magdalena Abakanowicz: crowds
- Her own words: "I feel overawed by quantity where counting no longer makes sense… A crowd of people… is a mysterious assemblage of variants of certain prototype. A riddle of nature's abhorrence of exact repetition." — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Abakanowicz).
- *Agora* (2004–2006) is 106 headless cast-iron figures, each about 9 ft tall, in Grant Park, Chicago — [City of Chicago, snippet](https://chicago.gov/city/en/depts/dca/supp_info/chicago_s_publicartagora.html); [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Abakanowicz).
- *Crowd I* (1986–87) has 50 figures. *Nierozpoznani* (*The Unrecognised Ones*, 2002) stands in Cytadela Park, Poznań — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Abakanowicz).

#### Zbigniew Rybczyński: *Tango* (1980)
- Thirty-six characters at different stages of life enter a single room and loop through it without ever interacting — [Culture.pl, snippet](https://culture.pl/en/work/tango-zbigniew-rybczynski); [Animafest, snippet](https://animafest.hr/en/2023/film/read/tango_2).
- The 8-minute film needed "several hundred thousand exposures on an optical printer and sixteen hours a day over seven months". It won the Oscar for Best Animated Short Film at the 55th Academy Awards — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango_%281981_film%29).
- It was the first Oscar in the history of Polish cinema — [Culture.pl, snippet](https://culture.pl/en/work/tango-zbigniew-rybczynski).

#### Oskar Hansen: Open Form
- Formulated in 1959 and presented at CIAM Otterlo — [Culture.pl, snippet](https://culture.pl/en/artist/oskar-nikolai-hansen).
- It opens architecture to its users and lets them co-create it. The architect's role is limited to a "perceptive background" — [Metalocus, snippet](https://www.metalocus.es/en/news/oskar-hansen-and-open-form).
- In the 1960s he extended it into the Linear Continuous System — [Metalocus, snippet](https://www.metalocus.es/en/news/oskar-hansen-and-open-form).

#### Józef Robakowski
- He co-founded the Workshop of Film Form, which opposed narrative cinema and analysed the film medium itself; he coordinated it 1970–77 — [e-flux, snippet](https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/146127/j-zef-robakowskinearer-further).
- *From My Window* (1978–1999) was filmed for more than 20 years from his Łódź kitchen window, looking over a square in an estate nicknamed "Manhattan", with his own off-screen commentary — [U-jazdowski, snippet](https://ko-lekcjawyborow.u-jazdowski.pl/en/kolekcja/z-mojego-okna-1978-1999-).

### Inferences
- **Abakanowicz → "variants of a prototype".**
  - The most exact visual metaphor for a surname's bearers: one prototype, many near-identical variants, never exact repetition.
  - Render it as a field of marks derived from the name, each seeded with micro-variation.
  - RULE RISK: do not use human figures. They read as "your ancestors" or as ethnic types, and Abakanowicz's headless figures carry trauma connotations.
- **Opałka → a number as a trace of time.** Present the PESEL count as a dated "detail" (number plus snapshot date), not as a gamified score. His progressive whitening suggests a way to encode the *age of a data snapshot*. Do not use it to encode rarity: fading would imply that people are disappearing.
- **Rybczyński → co-presence without merger.**
  - Many independent loops share one space and never interact. That is a visual model of Name Atlas's invariant that "Łuczak" and "Luczak" coexist and never merge.
  - It also models layered historical attestations shown in one frame.
- **Strzemiński → afterimage and historically conditioned vision.**
  - Afterimages suggest transitions and loading states as residues of the typed name.
  - *Theory of Vision* justifies drawing each era's evidence in the "visual consciousness" of its medium (engraving, letterpress, register, database). Abstract it as line quality, not as sepia pastiche.
  - Unism argues for one continuous, non-hierarchical plane of information.
- **Komunikat / a.r. alphabet → letters built from lines and arcs.** A constructed lettering system is a Polish, intellectual reference, already reused institutionally by POLIN Sans. Readability "as habit" limits it to wordmark and display use.
- **Kobro → 8:5 rhythm, "open to infinity".** A ready-made proportional system (1.6) for grid, type scale and motion timing. Compositions that bleed off-screen imply bearers beyond what is shown.
- **Berlewi → mechanical texture from simple elements.** A generative-texture method (letters turned into bars and dots).
  - KITSCH RISK: a dominant black/white/red palette slides towards national-flag symbolism. Keep red as a rare accent.
- **Szczuka/Żarnower → constructivist diagonals and photomontage.** Energetic typographic layouts.
  - RULE RISK: montaging real archival records next to a user's surname implies ancestry. Use only generic, clearly labelled archival texture.
- **Fangor → soft-edged halos.** Blur and halo are a native Polish op-art vocabulary. The Metro Line 2 lettering proves it works for lettering at public scale.
- **Hansen → Open Form.** A recessive UI frame that is "completed" by the user's name.
- **Robakowski → fixed viewpoint over decades.** Re-render the same composition for each annual PESEL snapshot.

### Gaps
- The figure count of *Nierozpoznani* (commonly cited as 112) was not verified.
- The dimensions of Opałka's canvases were garbled in the one extract found ("195 × 35 cm"), so they are omitted.
- Dating of the Komunikat experiment: one search extract gave 1930, Typoteka gives a 1932 sketch. The source of the 1930 date was not isolated.
- Strzemiński's functional-typography writings (e.g., "Druk funkcjonalny") and the exact construction of the a.r. alphabet were not verified.
- Culture.pl pages could only be read as search snippets. Their `api.culture.pl` backend redirected to a login.
- *Tango* is dated 1980 by Culture.pl and Animafest, but the Wikipedia article is titled "Tango (1981 film)". The production versus release year was not resolved.

## 2. Polish School of Posters: strategies and credible contemporary reinterpretations

### Takeaway
The school's transferable method is **one succinct metaphor, hand-lettering fused with the image, and "pictorial economy"**. It grew out of censorship (audiences learned to read hidden metaphors) and scarcity. What transfers to a digital product is that method, including a Polish audience's trained literacy in visual metaphor, not the painterly 1960s surface, which quickly turns into retro pastiche.

### Cited Findings
- **Movement-level strategy** — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_School_of_Posters):
  - "succinct metaphors" and allusions;
  - a "vibrantly coloured painterly approach" with "hand-lettered typography" that "emphasised the individuality of the images";
  - "pictorial economy", and a blurred line between designer and illustrator;
  - active from the early 1950s to the late 1980s, ending with the 1989 transition.
- **Institutions:** poster department at the Warsaw Academy of Fine Arts under Tomaszewski (1952); International Poster Biennale (from 1966); Poster Museum (1968) — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_School_of_Posters).
- **International breakthrough:** Polish posters took first place five times at the 1948 Vienna International Film Poster Exhibition — [Culture.pl, snippet](https://culture.pl/en/article/6-legends-of-the-polish-poster-school). Tomaszewski's awards list includes five gold medals at the 1948 International Poster Exhibition in Vienna — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Tomaszewski_%28poster_artist%29).
- **Censorship:** "Omnipresent censorship forced the audience to learn how to perfectly read the metaphors hidden in a poster" — [HMCT Art Center, snippet](https://hmctartcenter.org/articles/censorship-creativity-and-the-polish-school-of-posters).
- **Hand-lettering and scarcity** — [IVPDA, fetched](https://www.ivpda.com/content/1502/The-Polish-School-of-Posters--A-Remarkable-Period-in-Graphic-Design-History):
  - the posters were "the first to incorporate beautifully hand drawn type and imagery into one cohesive design";
  - shortages of paper, inks and official fonts "fuelled their innovative nature";
  - IVPDA reads Świerzy's 1974 "Tuxedo Bear" as a hidden anti-USSR metaphor (their interpretation).
- **Henryk Tomaszewski (1914–2005), the school's "father"** — [Wikipedia, fetched via API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Tomaszewski_%28poster_artist%29):
  - from 1947 designed film posters for the state distributor Centrala Wynajmu Filmów, with Tadeusz Trepkowski and Tadeusz Gronowski;
  - used "bold colors, abstract shapes, and filmmaking techniques to convey the film's mood rather than rely on portraits of the film's stars", with an animated, witty manner;
  - professor from 1952 to 1985; influenced "successive waves of designers across three decades".
- **Jan Lenica** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Lenica):
  - trained as an architect;
  - worked on Walerian Borowczyk's early animations and made cut-out stop-motion films (FIPRESCI award at Annecy 1963 for *Labirynt*);
  - lived in France 1963–86, then Berlin;
  - Gold Medal Prix Max Ernst, Warsaw Biennale 1966.
- **Roman Cieślewicz** — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Cie%C5%9Blewicz):
  - art editor of *Ty i Ja* 1959–62; moved to France in 1963;
  - art director for *Vogue* and *Elle* 1965–69; *Opus International* 1967–69;
  - *Kamikaze* "review of panic information" (1976; *Kamikaze 2* 1991).
- **Waldemar Świerzy** made more than 2,500 posters. His style moved from folk influences (1950s) to pop (1960s), portraiture (1970s) and television imagery (1980s) — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldemar_%C5%9Awierzy).
- **Jan Młodożeniec** studied under Tomaszewski from 1948 to 1955 — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_M%C5%82odo%C5%BCeniec).
- **Franciszek Starowieyski** was the first Polish artist to have a one-man show at MoMA New York (1986) — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciszek_Starowieyski).
- **Rosław Szaybo** was chief art director at CBS Records from 1972 to 1988 and designed more than 2,000 album covers (including Judas Priest's *British Steel*). He was mentored by Fangor and Tomaszewski — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ros%C5%82aw_Szaybo).
- **Poster Museum at Wilanów** — [Poster Museum, fetched](https://postermuseum.pl/en/about):
  - opened on 4 June 1968 at the 2nd International Poster Biennale; the cornerstone was laid in June 1966;
  - the world's oldest poster museum, now a division of the National Museum in Warsaw;
  - about 36,000 Polish and 25,000 foreign poster titles;
  - the Biennale moved there from Zachęta in 1993.
- **Contemporary continuity verified in this pass:**
  - Mateusz Machalski was named Designer of the Year 2019 at the Polish Graphic Awards and has designed more than a hundred identities — [Design & Paper, fetched](https://www.designandpaper.com/?p=113827).
  - Świerzy's *Jazz Greats* lithograph series was reissued from 2003 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldemar_%C5%9Awierzy).

### Inferences
- **Take the method, not the look.** Use one data-true metaphor per screen, set the surname itself as the "hand-lettered" element fused with the data image, and keep pictorial economy (one number, one image, one claim).
- **Metaphors must be structural, not narrative** (a crowd for a count, layers for time).
  - RULE RISK: illustrating an etymology (a smith's hammer for Kowalski, a mill for Młynarczyk) turns a hypothesis into a picture that reads as fact and breaks "never invent a story". Etymology stays typographic and carries its confidence status.
- **The audience's metaphor-literacy is an asset.** A Polish audience raised on this tradition will reward restraint, allusion and wit over explanatory illustration.
- **Cieślewicz's photomontage and Tomaszewski's reduction** point to archival material rendered as enlarged halftone or cut shapes: abstract texture rather than sepia photographs.
- **KITSCH RISK:** pastiche of brush-lettered 1960s film or circus ("Cyrk") posters reads as retro souvenir design. The school's legacy is also heavily commercialised as collectibles.

### Gaps
- No fetched source gave usable style analysis of Młodożeniec's lettering, Lenica's poster line (e.g., *Wozzeck*), Starowieyski's baroque calligraphy or Szaybo's photographic minimalism. Wikipedia pages were thin and Culture.pl's "6 legends" article was readable only as a snippet.
- Post-1989 poster designers who credibly reinterpret the school were **not verified** (the search quota ran out).
- The current status of the Warsaw Biennale was not established; the museum page mentions "organisational changes".

## 3. Folk and vernacular forms treated in a modern way

### Takeaway
The non-kitsch route is to borrow folk **procedures**: fold symmetry, figure/ground, layered sheets, strict colour canons, co-design with makers. Then change scale, material or technology, as WWAA's Expo 2010 pavilion, MOHO Design's laser-cut oversized cut-out and Wanda Telakowska's Institute of Industrial Design did. Copying motifs (roosters, flowers, leluja, highlander hearts, eggs) is the kitsch route.

### Cited Findings
- **Wycinanki (paper-cuts)** use repetitive motifs and symmetrical, rhythmic compositions — [CREES, University of Kansas, snippet](https://crees.ku.edu/wycinanki).
  - **Kurpie** cuts are a single colour, folded **once**. Main forms are the leluja (tree-like), stars, ribbons and zielki (potted-plant-like) — [CREES, snippet](https://crees.ku.edu/wycinanki).
  - **Łowicz** cuts use multiple bright colours, each cut on a separate sheet and glued in layers, with flowers, roosters and geometric motifs — [CREES, snippet](https://crees.ku.edu/wycinanki).
  - The Łowicz forms are named kodra (round), tasiemka (elongated) and gwiozda (snowflake-like) — [Explore and More, snippet](https://exploreandmore.org/?p=10258).
  - Culture.pl publishes a regional guide (Kurpie, Łowicz, Kashubian, architectural) — [Culture.pl, snippet](https://culture.pl/en/article/kurpie-lowicz-kashubian-architectural-a-brief-guide-to-paper-cutouts).
- **Polish Pavilion, Expo 2010 Shanghai (WWAA)** — [Dezeen, snippet](https://www.dezeen.com/2010/06/03/polish-pavilion-for-shanghai-expo-2010-by-wwaa-architects/); [WWAA, snippet](https://wwaa.pl/?p=790):
  - a perforated façade inspired by folk paper-cuts, made of CNC-cut plywood on a steel frame;
  - slanting planes suggest a folded sheet, and the patterns continue inside;
  - more than 8 million visitors; voted "Shape of the Year 2010" by bryla.pl;
  - competition won by Wojciech Kakowski, Marcin Mostafa and Natalia Paszkowska.
- **Zalipie** — [Mental Floss, snippet](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/80547/zalipie-polands-prettiest-painted-village); [Visit Małopolska, snippet](https://narowery.visitmalopolska.pl/en_GB/obiekt/-/poi/zagroda-felicji-curylowej-zalipie):
  - women painted flowers on their houses, originally to hide soot marks;
  - Felicja Curyłowa painted almost every surface of her cottage, which became a museum after her death in 1974;
  - the "Malowana Chata" (Painted Cottage) competition has been annual since 1965.
- **Kashubian embroidery** — [Pomorskie.travel, official regional portal, snippet](https://pomorskie.travel/en/-/haft-kaszubski):
  - taught by the Norbertine nuns of Żukowo in the 18th–19th centuries; later centres in Wdzydze (the Gulgowski family), Puck, Wejherowo, Tuchola, Słupsk and Bytów;
  - **seven colours** with fixed meanings: yellow (sun, fields, sand, amber); green (meadows, forests); brown (fertile soil); three blues (sea, lakes and rivers, sky); red (blood shed for the homeland);
  - the Żukowo school follows a strict colour canon for each element.
- **Pisanki** — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisanka_%28Polish%29):
  - the name comes from *pisać* ("to write", historically also "to paint");
  - techniques: wax-resist (pisanki); single-colour dyeing (kraszanki/byczki); scratching (drapanki/skrobanki); glued decoration (naklejanki); and bulrush pith with yarn (oklejanki/wyklejanki, Podlaskie).
- **Wanda Telakowska** — [Wikipedia, snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Telakowska); [Culture.pl, snippet](https://culture.pl/en/artist/wanda-telakowska):
  - founded the Institute of Industrial Design (IWP) in 1950;
  - brought folk artists, children and young people into "designing teams" (1940s–1970s) to adapt folk small-scale making to industrial production;
  - in 1978 arranged the transfer of the IWP collection to the National Museum in Warsaw.
- **2000s "folk inspirations" wave** — [Culture.pl, "A Foreigner's Guide to Polish Design", snippet](https://culture.pl/en/article/a-foreigners-guide-to-polish-design):
  - the wave was marked by exhibitions, publications and a festival; designers approached heritage "with irony and humour, with no excessive solemnity";
  - MOHO Design made a carpet that is "a large-scale folk cut out which blows traditional folk ornaments out of proportion", with laser-cut borders;
  - Katarzyna Herman-Janiec made *Pleciaki*, seats and baskets woven from rolled newspaper.
  - Dezeen covered a "Polska Folk" show at Tent London in 2009 ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2009/10/19/polska-folk-at-tent-london/)); the page returned 403, so its contents were not read.
- **"Romantic Brutalism" (Visteria Foundation, Milan Design Week, April 2025)** — [Designboom, fetched](https://www.designboom.com/design/romantic-brutalism-polish-craft-design-milan-design-week-visteria-foundation-04-08-2025/):
  - curated by Federica Sala;
  - frames Polish design through "brutalism, romanticism, folk, and art deco";
  - participants: Jan Ankiersztajn (hand-welded aluminium), GRÔPK (coil-built ceramics), Giewont Studio (fallen or diseased timber), Zofia Chylak (hand-knitted lace).

### Inferences
- **Kurpie single fold → a symmetry generator.** Mirroring the surname's letter outlines on one vertical fold, in one colour, yields an abstract "name cut-out" with clear figure/ground and zero floral content.
- **Łowicz layering → time as stacked sheets.** Each dataset or era is a separately "cut" layer, stacked with visible edges (z-order = chronology).
- **Pisanki techniques → reveal interactions.** Wax-resist (mask first, reveal later) and scratching (uncover white from colour) map onto progressive disclosure of evidence. Use them sparingly; they can turn toy-like.
- **Kashubian colour canon → palette discipline.** Each colour has exactly one meaning (for example, one per confidence status), documented as a canon.
  - KITSCH RISK: do not borrow the Kashubian palette literally together with its motifs.
- **Telakowska → folk as method (co-design, adapting craft logic to industry), not motif.** The strongest intellectual precedent for a non-kitsch "Polishness".
- **WWAA pavilion → change of scale and material.** The digital analogue is perforated or cut SVG planes with light and depth (parallax), not clip-art ornaments.
- **KITSCH RISK:** Cepelia souvenir aesthetics, Łowicz roosters and flowers, Zalipie postcard florals, highlander (Podhale) hearts, painted eggs, and red-and-white "folk-nation" combinations.
- **RULE RISK:** Kurpie, Kashubian, Góral and other regional patterns are regional identities. Showing a regional pattern next to a surname implies the name "belongs" to that region or ethnos. Never map folk styles onto surname geography.

### Gaps
- Podhale **parzenica** facts could not be verified (the Polish Wikipedia title fetched is a stream of the same name).
- The Zakopane Style (Stanisław Witkiewicz) was not fetched (the Wikipedia API was rate-limited).
- Contemporary *graphic* (not product) designers reinterpreting wycinanki were not verified.

## 4. Typography: Polish typefaces, diacritic design doctrine, foundries and open-source fonts

### Takeaway
Polish typography has three strengths:
- **A written diacritic doctrine** (Adam Twardoch): the kreska is steeper and shifted right compared with the acute; the ogonek is a structural tail that never exceeds the base glyph; the kropka matches the dot on i.
- **Letterforms designed for Polish** (Antykwa Półtawskiego).
- **A live revival and multi-script scene:** Capitalics and Machalski (Brygada 1918, Półtawski Nowy, POLIN Sans), and Fontarte's *Komunikat FA*.

**Original analysis:**
- All 76 audited Google Fonts families cover every Polish letter, but only **five ship Polish-specific kreska glyphs** through OpenType `locl`/PLK: Playfair Display, Vollkorn, Cormorant Garamond, Archivo and Chivo.
- Those substitutions survive in Google's served web subsets.
- Diacritics deserve to be the product's signature: **27.5% of PESEL register entries have a surname containing at least one Polish diacritic**, led by **ł** and **ń**.

### Cited Findings

#### Twardoch's "Polish Diacritics: how to?" (© 1999, "working draft")

From the introduction — [intro, fetched](https://twardoch.com/download/polishhowto/intro.html):
- "The Polish alphabet contains 32 letters originally used in Polish language plus 3 letters used in foreign words. 9 letters are so-called diacritics."
- There are four diacritic elements: two accents (kreska, kropka) and two letter modifiers (ogonek, and the stroke, *kreska ukośna*).
- Today's letter shapes were largely set by the Kraków printer Jan Januszowski, whose *Nowy karakter polski* (1594) "is considered to be the first original Polish typeface".

**Kreska** — [kreska, fetched](https://www.twardoch.com/download/polishhowto/kreska.html):
- "The Polish kreska runs steeper and is shifted more to the right rather than centered above the base glyph. The middle of its 'impact' resides lower than that of the acute."
- On capitals it "may run flatter". Accents "should follow the thick-thin proportions of base letters".
- The Czech čárka (on vowels, marking length) and the Polish kreska (mainly on soft consonants) "are two fully different things".
- Palatino Linotype roman "suits the Polish language perfectly". Georgia's kreskas had to be repositioned and redesigned.
- He recommends OpenType language-sensitive substitution so that Polish text gets the steeper form.

**Ogonek** — [ogonek, fetched](https://www.twardoch.com/download/polishhowto/ogonek.html):
- "It all starts with the essentially wrong assumption that ogonek is an accent. No, it's not. It's much more a character element, just like a stem, a serif or a descent."
- It should be smoothly connected, usually reach the descender line, "never exceed the right boundary of the basic glyph", and sit near the right edge of *e*, "not attached centrally like cedilla".
- It should imitate the calligraphic technique of the typeface.
- On capital A it grows from the centre of the stem, or merges with the inner part of the base serif — never the outer part.
- The Polish and Lithuanian ogonek traditions differ, and "only very few typefaces produced in the Western world … contain correct ogoneks".

**Kropka** — [kropka, fetched](https://www.twardoch.com/download/polishhowto/kropka.html):
- It is the "least problematic". On *ż* it should be identical in shape and position to the dot on *i*, visually centred.
- On *Ż* it aligns with the kreska of *Ź*. In italics it must be centred on the slanted axis.

#### Historical typefaces designed for Polish
- **Antykwa Półtawskiego** was designed by Adam Półtawski in the 1920s specifically for Polish — [Wikipedia, snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antykwa_P%C3%B3%C5%82tawskiego); [CTAN, snippet](https://ctan.org/tex-archive/fonts/poltawski):
  - letters more frequent in Polish than in other languages (w, y, z, ł) were given specific shapes;
  - first cast at Jan Idźkowski's foundry, Warsaw (CTAN gives 1931);
  - long used for music publications, including Paderewski's complete Chopin edition;
  - the Polish TeX Users Group (GUST) digitised it in four weights with optical sizes.
- **Półtawski Nowy** (Google Fonts, OFL) digitises the 1928 design — [Google Fonts description, fetched](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/poltawskinowy/DESCRIPTION.en_us.html); [GitHub](https://github.com/kosmynkab/Poltawski-Nowy):
  - sources: Idźkowski i S-ka specimen scans and Monotype drawings and punches from 1934–1955 (Type Archive London);
  - intended for 10–18 pt;
  - extended diacritics, small caps and figure variants;
  - funded by the Ministry of Culture's Digital Culture 2020 programme.
- **Brygada 1918** (Google Fonts, OFL, variable, Latin/Greek/Cyrillic) — [Google Fonts description, fetched](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/brygada1918/DESCRIPTION.en_us.html); [GitHub, snippet](https://github.com/kosmynkab/Brygada-1918):
  - a revival for the centenary of independence in 2018;
  - based on a 1954 National Type Foundry catalogue entry and matrices found by Janusz Tryzno at the Book Arts Museum in 2016;
  - designers: Capitalics, Mateusz Machalski, Borys Kosmynka, Ania Wieluńska, Przemysław Hoffer;
  - it premiered at the Presidential Palace — [Design & Paper, fetched](https://www.designandpaper.com/?p=113827).
  - **Conflict:** one catalogue attributes the original to Półtawski in 1918 ([Luc Devroye, snippet](https://luc.devroye.org/fonts-61971.html)). The revival's own research says it was "most probably created during the interwar period" at Idźkowski ([Google Design, snippet](https://design.google/library/type-detectives-give-life-brygada-en)).
- **Antykwa Toruńska** was designed by Zygfryd Gardzielewski with Józef Gruszka and digitised by Janusz M. Nowacki (GUST) — [Polish Wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antykwa_toru%C5%84ska). The font's own metadata reads "Design by Zygfryd Gardzielewski. Digitized by Janusz M. Nowacki" (2005–2006) — [original analysis of the TeX Live `antt` package](https://mirrors.ctan.org/systems/texlive/tlnet/archive/antt.tar.xz).
- **Iwona and Kurier:** the font metadata reads "Design by Malgorzata Budyta – 1975, Warszawa, Poland", digitised by Nowacki (GUST) — [original analysis of the TeX Live `iwona` and `kurier` packages](https://mirrors.ctan.org/systems/texlive/tlnet/archive/iwona.tar.xz).

#### Contemporary Polish type scene
- **Capitalics** calls itself "the very first type foundry in Poland". Mateusz Machalski co-founded it and the BORUTTA group — [Design & Paper, fetched](https://www.designandpaper.com/?p=113827):
  - his *Bona Nova* was developed with Andrzej Heidrich, designer of Polish banknotes;
  - the page credits him with "over 30 type families" and "over a hundred" identities. - **Typoteka** is Capitalics' index of typefaces by people connected to Poland: "Typography is a cultural medium and its outcome" — [Typoteka, fetched](https://typoteka.pl/pl/typeface/polin-sans).
- **POLIN Sans** (Machalski and Ben Nathan, with Michał Gorczyca and Małgorzata Bartosik, 2016–2024) — [Typoteka, fetched](https://typoteka.pl/pl/typeface/polin-sans):
  - a geometric multi-script family (Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Hebrew; 10 styles);

- **Komunikat FA** (Fontarte, 2004) is Strzemiński-derived and includes Polish diacritics — [Typoteka, fetched](https://typoteka.pl/en/typeface/komunikat-fa).
- **Lato** — "Lato means 'Summer' in Polish". It was "started in the summer of 2010 by Warsaw-based designer Łukasz Dziedzic", who wanted it to feel "Serious but friendly" — [Google Fonts description, fetched](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/lato/DESCRIPTION.en_us.html).
- **Signika** (Anna Giedryś) is a wayfinding and signage sans that supports Polish and gained a GRAD axis in 2023 for dark backgrounds — [Google Fonts description, fetched](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/signika/DESCRIPTION.en_us.html).
- **Institutional practice:** the National Museum in Warsaw sets everything in Suisse BP Int'l and Romain BP (Swiss Typefaces). Its manual stresses that Suisse "Zawiera pełen zestaw polskich znaków" ("contains the full set of Polish characters") — [MNW identity manual, fetched](https://www.mnw.art.pl/gfx/muzeumnarodowe/en/defaultstronaopisowa/103/3/1/mnw_ci_manual.pdf).

#### Original analysis: open-source fonts and Polish specifics
Method:
- Downloaded the default roman file of 76 families from [google/fonts](https://github.com/google/fonts), plus 5 GUST fonts from [TeX Live on CTAN](https://mirrors.ctan.org/systems/texlive/tlnet/archive/).
- Tested each with fontTools for:
  - coverage of ą ć ę ł ń ó ś ź ż Ą Ć Ę Ł Ń Ó Ś Ź Ż;
  - a `locl` feature under the Latin `PLK ` language system;
  - whether ś and é share one accent component.

Results:
- **Coverage:** all 76 Google families and all 5 GUST fonts cover all 18 letters.
- **Polish-specific kreska** through `locl`/PLK (for example `sacute → sacute.loclPLK`, `oacute → oacute.loclPLK`) appears in only **5** families: **Playfair Display, Vollkorn, Cormorant Garamond, Archivo, Chivo**.
- **The rest,** including the Polish-designed Brygada 1918, Półtawski Nowy, Lato and Signika, use one acute design for both é and ś. That is not necessarily wrong: a Polish-first design can draw its single acute as a kreska. It needs visual review.
- **Served-subset check:** Google's CSS2 API (Chrome UA) was used to fetch the woff2 files.
  - The `latin-ext` subsets of Playfair Display, Archivo, Vollkorn and Cormorant Garamond keep the PLK lookups for ć ń ś ź.
  - Playfair's `latin` subset (U+0000–00FF, which holds ó/Ó) keeps `oacute → oacute.loclPLK`.
- The full per-family table is in the "Font recommendations" section below.

#### Original analysis: which diacritics Polish surnames actually use
Source: PESEL surname lists for living persons, snapshot 2026-01-20, [dane.gov.pl dataset 1681](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/1681,nazwiska-osob-zyjacych-wystepujace-w-rejestrze-pesel). Files: [male list](https://api.dane.gov.pl/resources/1148808,nazwiska-meskie-stan-na-2026-01-20/file), [female list](https://api.dane.gov.pl/resources/1148811,nazwiska-zenskie-stan-na-2026-01-20/file).
- **27.5% of register entries** (male and female lists alike) carry a surname with at least one Polish diacritic. By distinct surname: 12.5% of male and 14.9% of female surnames.
- Share of male entries whose surname contains each letter: **ł 9.4%, ń 8.1%**, ś 3.0%, ó 2.5%, ę 2.4%, ą 2.0%, ż 1.9%, ź 0.8%, ć 0.4%. Much of ń comes from ‑ński, which alone covers 6.6% of male entries.
- Surnames are stored in **UPPERCASE** in the files (e.g., "WIŚNIEWSKI"), so capital diacritics (Ł, Ś, Ż, Ó…) are the register's native form.

### Inferences
- **Diacritics as the visual signature.** A quarter of real register entries depend on diacritics, and the product's core invariant is that diacritics are identity-bearing (Łuczak ≠ Luczak). The four Polish marks (kreska, kropka, ogonek, stroke) can become the brand's glyph system:
  - the oblique **Ł-stroke** as the "one line" motif (divider, cursor, progress bar);
  - the **kreska** as the "accent of attention" (focus ring, active tab);
  - the **ogonek** as a structural hook, following Twardoch: it is part of the letter, not ornament;
  - the **kropka** as a data point.
- **Motion:** in comparison views, draw the diacritic that separates two names (the stroke of Ł, the kreska of ś). Never ASCII-fold in display.
- **Register voice:** set the canonical surname in widely spaced capitals as a documentary "register voice", with a mixed-case reading form beside it. This requires fonts whose capital kreska, ogonek and stroke are well drawn.
- **Półtawski and Brygada** carry an interwar, bibliophile tone. They are excellent for a "historical attestations" layer.
  - KITSCH RISK: used as the main UI face, they tip into interwar nostalgia.
- **Komunikat FA and POLIN Sans** show that constructivist lettering from Strzemiński is already a recognised Polish institutional language. A Name Atlas wordmark could draw on the same logic without copying either.
  - RULE RISK: POLIN Sans is tied to a Jewish-history institution and includes Hebrew. Avoid anything that codes surnames by ethnicity or religion through type.

### Gaps
- Twardoch's **stroke (Ł)** page stayed behind a bot check after several spaced retries, so no quoted Ł guidance is available.
- The design dates of Antykwa Toruńska (often given as the 1950s) were not verified.
- Licensing and availability of Komunikat FA and POLIN Sans (the latter is probably custom to the institution) were not verified.
- The font audit covered each family's default roman file only (variable fonts include all weights). Italics were not audited. "Shared accent" is a structural observation, not a quality judgement.

## 5. Archival and documentary material relevant to surnames, and how to abstract it

### Takeaway
Polish surname evidence is multilingual and multi-script by history:
- Latin, then Polish (about 1808–1868), then Russian in Cyrillic (from about 1868 in the Kingdom of Poland), with German in the Prussian partition;
- engraved (the 1843 "Mapa Kwatermistrzowska");
- printed (Polona);
- indexed (Geneteka: 68 million records);
- lexicographic (Rymut: more than 800,000 surnames from the 1990 PESEL);
- statistical (the 2026 PESEL lists).

Their native visual grammars can be abstracted into systems without sepia or parchment: hachure and conventional signs, register columns, handwriting ductus, suffix morphology, and a very long-tailed frequency distribution.

### Cited Findings
- **Language of parish records** — [Stephen Danko, snippet](https://stephendanko.com/blog/2732); [SGGEE, snippet](https://www.sggee.org/research/2018PerrySigrid_Cyrillic&Polish_Parish_Records.pdf); [FamilySearch catalogue "Kopie księg metrykalnych, 1808–1911"](https://www.familysearch.org/search/catalog/82273):
  - records were kept in Latin, the language of the Church;
  - from about 1808 to about 1868 in the Kingdom of Poland they were usually written in Polish, as detailed paragraphs;
  - after the January Uprising (1863–65) official records had to be kept in Russian, so from about 1868 they are in Cyrillic, until the First World War;
  - supplements appear in Latin, Polish, some Russian and some German, and surnames sometimes appear in both Cyrillic and Latin script.
- **Geneteka,** run by the Polskie Towarzystwo Genealogiczne, indexes baptisms, marriages and deaths from parish and civil registers — [Geneteka, fetched 2026-09-30](https://geneteka.genealodzy.pl/):
  - 6,276 parishes, 16,080 registers, **68,075,297 records**;
  - the largest regions are Mazovia (12.3M), Łódź (9.9M) and Lesser Poland (7M).
- **"Mapa Kwatermistrzowska" (Topograficzna Karta Królestwa Polskiego)** — [PAN Library, snippet](https://platforma.bk.pan.pl/en/bib_records/195119); [dzieje.pl, snippet](https://dzieje.pl/node/84315); [RCIN digital copy](https://rcin.org.pl/dlibra/publication/12322/edition/834):
  - work began in 1822 under Polish officers of the Quartermaster-General's staff and continued under the Russians after 1831;
  - fieldwork ended in 1839; published in Warsaw in 1843;
  - scale 1:126,000, 60 sheets plus an index sheet, colour copperplate engraving;
  - a legend of **54 basic topographic signs**;
  - title in Polish, Russian and French, content in Polish.
- **Polona** (National Library of Poland):
  - reached **3 million objects in December 2019**, adding around 2,000 a day;
  - holds books, periodicals, manuscripts, early prints, maps, drawings, engravings, photographs, postcards, sheet music and ephemera;
  - public-domain objects are free to use "for all purposes, including commercial ones" — [BN news, snippet](https://www.bn.org.pl/en/news/3840-three-million-objects-have-been-made-available-in-the-national-digital-library-polona.html);
  - launched in 2006, with an interface overhaul in October 2017 ("Polona/2milions") — [Wikipedia, fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polona).
- **Kazimierz Rymut,** *Słownik nazwisk współcześnie w Polsce używanych* — [JRI-Poland, fetched](https://jri-poland.org/blog/slownik-nazwisk-online/):
  - 10 volumes, more than 6,000 pages, **over 800,000 surnames**;
  - compiled from a 1990 PESEL database covering about 94% of the population;
  - each entry gives totals and a breakdown by province;
  - put online by Dom Polonii in Pułtusk; the JRI post links herby.com.pl.
  - Rymut's two-volume historical-etymological *Nazwiska Polaków* (A–K, L–Ż) is digitised — [Biblioteka Cyfrowa, snippet](https://bibliotekacyfrowa.pl/en/dlibra/publication/28107).
- **Surname formation** — [Wikipedia "Polish name", fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_name):
  - ‑ski comes from Proto-Slavic *ьskъ* (affiliation). It was long associated with the nobility, but in the 19th century "the bourgeoisie class, and even the peasantry" adopted it, so a ‑ski name does not denote nobility.
  - Only adjectival endings (‑ski, ‑cki, ‑dzki, ‑ny, ‑ty) have feminine forms (‑ska…). The ‑owa and ‑ówna forms are archaic.
  - Other common suffixes are ‑czyk, ‑czek, ‑czak, ‑czuk and ‑wicz.
  - Surnames appear from about the 13th century among the upper classes, spread to burghers by the end of the 17th century, then to peasants and Jews, "ended only in the mid-19th century".
  - About 35% of the 1,000 most popular surnames end in ‑ski/‑cki/‑dzki — [Wikipedia, snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_name).
- **PESEL, snapshot 2026-01-20** [original analysis of the [male](https://api.dane.gov.pl/resources/1148808,nazwiska-meskie-stan-na-2026-01-20/file) and [female](https://api.dane.gov.pl/resources/1148811,nazwiska-zenskie-stan-na-2026-01-20/file) lists, dataset [1681](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/1681,nazwiska-osob-zyjacych-wystepujace-w-rejestrze-pesel)]:
  - **Size:** male list 408,182 distinct surnames, 21,197,537 entries; female list 420,082 surnames, 21,560,302 entries.
  - **Minimum count is 2 in both files**, so single-bearer surnames never appear.
  - **Top 5:** NOWAK (98,387 male / 99,738 female), KOWALSKI/‑SKA, WIŚNIEWSKI/‑SKA, WÓJCIK, KOWALCZYK. This matches Wikipedia's PESEL-based list.
  - **Suffix shares, male entries (share of people / share of distinct surnames):**

### Inferences
- **Suffix as structure.**
  - Set stem and suffix as two typographic units (for example, stem in the display face, suffix in a mono face after a hairline).
  - Build a suffix-family grid, a periodic-table-like map: ‑ski | ‑cki | ‑dzki, then ‑ak | ‑ek | ‑ik | ‑czyk | ‑czak, then ‑wicz. Any name can be placed in it without an etymological claim.
  - Show gendered twins (‑ski/‑ska) as paired forms, because PESEL keeps them in separate lists.
  - RULE RISK: the ‑ski ending must never be styled or described as noble. Wikipedia explicitly notes its 19th-century spread to burghers and peasants. No crests, coronets or "szlachta" cues.
- **Rarity needs a logarithmic ruler.** Counts run from 2 to about 100,000 and the median is 5. A slide-rule or decade scale (10, 100, 1k, 10k, 100k) is honest; linear bars would make most names invisible.
  - **Absence ≠ zero:** single-bearer surnames are not published, so a miss must read "not in the published list (fewer than 2 bearers, or not recorded)", never "0 people".
- **Register grids → tables with ruled columns** (date, place, source, confidence), echoing *księgi metrykalne* layouts without imitating their paper.
- **Handwriting ductus → stroke-order motion.** Animate a pen path that draws the surname: a documentary gesture, not a faux-script font.
  - **Script shifts** (Latin → Polish → Cyrillic) can be shown as a transliteration morph *only where a source documents that form*.
  - RULE RISK: script reflects partition-era administration, not the bearer's ethnicity or religion. Never infer either.
- **Kwatermistrzowska cartography → a legend-first sign system.** A small set of conventional signs with an always-visible legend. Hachure-line density fields for distribution (the per-voivodeship PESEL files make this feasible). Line-only engraving quality; no antique paper textures (KITSCH RISK: sepia maps).
- **Archival imagery**
  - RULE RISK: showing an individual register page or directory entry next to a searched surname implies "your ancestor". Use aggregate, abstracted or clearly generic material with explicit source labels.

### Gaps
- Polona's current object count (latest verified: 3 million, December 2019) and its specific address-book and directory holdings were not verified.
- The dates for German-language civil registers in the Prussian partition were not verified.
- **Data-semantics flag for the product team:** the 2026-01-20 lists sum to 42.76 million entries (21.20M male + 21.56M female). What exactly the "living persons in PESEL" population includes (for example, people abroad, or foreigners holding PESEL numbers) was not verified here. Check this before labelling counts "people living in Poland".

## 6. Contemporary Polish (and Central-European) identities and digital studios that fuse heritage and modernity

### Takeaway
The strongest precedents fuse heritage and modernity through **systems rather than ornaments**:
- POLIN's square P/פ (pei) mark, derived from the building's windows, plus a revived Strzemiński-based multi-script typeface;
- the National Museum in Warsaw's MNW ligature, with an ornament derived from it, on a modernist asymmetric grid;
- the Warsaw Ghetto Museum's **generative signets built from personal names**, by the Lithuanian studio DADADA.

Polish web studios with Awwwards recognition exist (Unikorns, The Story), but were verified only at snippet level.

### Cited Findings
- **POLIN Museum:**

- **National Museum in Warsaw (MNW)** — [MNW identity manual, fetched](https://www.mnw.art.pl/gfx/muzeumnarodowe/en/defaultstronaopisowa/103/3/1/mnw_ci_manual.pdf):

  - the website credits "Projekt i wdrożenie … CG2";
  - the branches are branded with superscripts, "ms¹" and "ms²" — [msl.org.pl, fetched](https://msl.org.pl/en).
- **Poster Museum:** a division of the National Museum in Warsaw — [Poster Museum, fetched](https://postermuseum.pl/en/about).

- **Warsaw digital studios with Awwwards recognition:**
  - Unikorns (founded by Dima Miro) has had a Site of the Day — [LinkedIn, snippet](https://pl.linkedin.com/in/dimamiro);
  - The Story is a Warsaw studio "since 2009" making award-winning sites — [Awwwards profile, snippet](https://www.awwwards.com/thestory/).

### Inferences
- **Derive the mark from a structural element of the subject** (POLIN: windows → square).
  - For Name Atlas, the structural elements are Polish orthography (the kreska and the Ł-stroke) and the data (a dated snapshot, a log-scale count).
  - A mark built from the oblique stroke plus the kreska would be recognisably "Polish-literate" without national symbols.
- **An ornament derived from the symbol (MNW).** Generate per-surname ornaments from that surname's own letters and diacritics, keeping one grammar across all names.
- **Superscript as structure (Muzeum Sztuki ms¹/ms²).** A precedent for making sources and confidence visible as superscript tokens on every claim, which the product requires.
- **Generative, name-based signets (DADADA/WGM)** are the closest existing precedent to per-surname identity marks.
  - RULE RISK: that system belongs to a memorial context. Name Atlas must not look like a memorial register: no "remember everyone" rhetoric, no terror/hope colour semantics, no Hebrew-derived letterforms, nothing implying victimhood, ancestry or religion.
- **Across these precedents, "Polish and modern" reads as:**
  - a Swiss- or modernist-grade grid;
  - one structural mark;
  - a typeface with Polish provenance or scrupulous Polish support;
  - an intellectual reference (Strzemiński, the poster school);
  - and never eagles, crests or folk roosters.

### Gaps
- The designers and dates of the Culture.pl (Adam Mickiewicz Institute) identity, the Muzeum Sztuki institutional identity (only the website builder was found), the current Polona interface, and the MNW identity (the manual is uncredited) were not verified.
- Polish pavilions after 2010 (Milan 2015, Dubai 2020, Osaka 2025) were not verified.
- Awwwards recognition was verified at snippet level only (awwwards.com was unreachable through the session proxy).
- **UNVERIFIED leads, from the researcher's recall; no source fetched; do not cite without checking:** Warsaw and Kraków studios often associated with heritage-meets-modern work, such as Homework, Podpunkt, Studio Otwarte, Uniforma and Huncwot.

## Design principles derived from Polish visual culture

### Takeaway
Take **method, not motif**. The following give Name Atlas Polish cultural depth that a Polish audience would recognise as intelligent, with no folk kitsch, heraldry or sepia nostalgia:
- counting as a trace of time;
- crowds of non-identical variants;
- layered loops that never merge;
- afterimages and historically conditioned seeing;
- calculated 8:5 rhythm;
- fold symmetry;
- one poster-grade metaphor;
- diacritics treated as identity.

### Cited Findings
The anchoring facts are cited in sections 1–6. Each principle below repeats its key source link.

### Inferences
Each bullet reads: source → principle → how it could look.

1. **Abakanowicz's crowds** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Abakanowicz)) → "variants of a prototype; nature's abhorrence of exact repetition" → a *crowd field*: log-scaled marks (from the PESEL count), each a seeded variant of one glyph (the initial, or a kreska), never identical, never human figures.
2. **Opałka's 1965/1–∞** ([DailyArt](https://www.dailyartmagazine.com/?p=132344)) → a number as a dated trace of time → the count set in large tabular numerals, counted up once and calmly, with the snapshot date typeset as part of the figure ("98 387 · PESEL 2026-01-20"). Older snapshots render progressively lighter: data ageing, not people fading.
3. **Rybczyński's *Tango*** ([Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/work/tango-zbigniew-rybczynski)) → many independent loops in one room that never interact → a timeline "room" where each attestation or source is its own looping trace. Spelling variants (Łuczak / Luczak) share the room as separate loops, a visible form of the no-merge rule.
4. **Strzemiński's *Afterimages*** ([Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/work/suns-afterimage-wladyslaw-strzeminski)) → memory as a retinal residue → the typed name leaves a soft, complementary-colour afterimage that becomes the result page's background form. Loaders fade as afterimages instead of spinning.
5. **Strzemiński's *Theory of Vision* and Unism** ([MoMA](https://post.moma.org/?p=1964)) → seeing is historically conditioned; unify form and plane → each evidence layer is drawn in its medium's "visual consciousness" (engraved hairline, letterpress rhythm, database grid) on one continuous, non-hierarchical plane. No hero photo with text laid over it.
6. **Kobro's 8:5 space–time rhythm** ([Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/artist/katarzyna-kobro)) → calculated rhythm, "open to infinity" → an 8:5 (1.6) ratio for grid, type scale and motion timing. Compositions bleed off-screen to imply bearers beyond the frame.
7. **Berlewi's Mechano-Faktura** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Berlewi)) → mechanical texture from simple elements, flat, restricted colour → generative textures where the surname's letters become rhythmic bars and dots, in ink and paper with one red accent. KITSCH RISK: red and white must not dominate.
8. **Strzemiński's Komunikat / a.r. alphabet** ([Typoteka](https://typoteka.pl/en/typeface/komunikat-fa)) → letters constructed from lines and arcs; "readability is a matter of habit" → one module set (line, arc, dot) builds the wordmark *and* the diacritic marks. Wordmark and display only, never data.
9. **Twardoch's diacritic doctrine** ([kreska](https://www.twardoch.com/download/polishhowto/kreska.html), [ogonek](https://www.twardoch.com/download/polishhowto/ogonek.html)) → the kreska is steeper and right-shifted; the ogonek is structure, not ornament → diacritics as the signature. Comparison views animate the mark that separates Ł from L or ś from s. Display never ASCII-folds. `lang="pl"` is set so Polish kreska forms apply.
10. **PESEL diacritic frequencies** ([dataset 1681](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/1681,nazwiska-osob-zyjacych-wystepujace-w-rejestrze-pesel), original analysis) → ł (9.4%) and ń (8.1%) are the surname-defining marks → the **oblique Ł-stroke** and the **kreska** become the two core brand marks (divider, cursor, focus and progress motifs).
11. **Surname morphology** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_name) plus PESEL shares) → suffix families are structural (‑ski/‑cki/‑dzki = 29.3% of male entries) → a "stem | suffix" typographic split and a suffix-family grid, with gendered twins shown as pairs. Never used to imply nobility, region or origin.
12. **PESEL long tail** (original analysis) → median surname has 5 bearers; two-thirds have 2–9 → a logarithmic "rarity ruler" (decades 10…100k), floored at 2. A miss reads "not in the published list", never "0".
13. **Mapa Kwatermistrzowska, 1843** ([PAN Library](https://platforma.bk.pan.pl/en/bib_records/195119)) → engraved relief with 54 conventional signs and an always-present legend → a legend-first sign system and hachure-line density fields for distributions. Line-only; no parchment.
14. **Metrical registers crossing Latin, Polish and Cyrillic** ([Stephen Danko](https://stephendanko.com/blog/2732)) → the same name travels between scripts and hands → documented script variants shown as a morphing ductus line, and handwriting as stroke-order motion (not a faux-script font). No inference from script to ethnicity.
15. **Polish School of Posters** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_School_of_Posters), [IVPDA](https://www.ivpda.com/content/1502/The-Polish-School-of-Posters--A-Remarkable-Period-in-Graphic-Design-History)) → one succinct metaphor, lettering fused with image, pictorial economy → each screen carries one data-true metaphor and one "lettered" surname. Wit over decoration; no pictorial etymology.
16. **Fangor's soft-edged paintings and Metro Line 2 lettering** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Fangor), [Icon](https://www.iconeye.com/design/warsaw-metro)) → suffused colour halos, "positive illusory space" → the surname headline carries a blurred chromatic halo. Sharpness encodes *evidential confidence* (crisp = well-supported, diffuse = uncertain) and never rights or licensing status, in line with ADR-0001's separation.
17. **Kurpie single-fold wycinanki and WWAA's pavilion** ([CREES](https://crees.ku.edu/wycinanki), [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2010/06/03/polish-pavilion-for-shanghai-expo-2010-by-wwaa-architects/)) → symmetry from one fold, figure/ground, a change of scale and material → a share card that mirrors the surname's outlines on one axis in one colour, as a perforated plane with light behind it. No roosters, flowers or leluja.
18. **Hansen's Open Form** ([Metalocus](https://www.metalocus.es/en/news/oskar-hansen-and-open-form)) → architecture as a "perceptive background" completed by its users → a recessive, spacious UI frame that is "finished" only by the user's surname.
19. **Robakowski's *From My Window*** ([U-jazdowski](https://ko-lekcjawyborow.u-jazdowski.pl/en/kolekcja/z-mojego-okna-1978-1999-)) → a fixed viewpoint observed over decades → the same composition re-rendered for each annual PESEL snapshot (2023–2026) as a quiet "window" time-lapse.
20. **Telakowska's IWP and the MNW ornament system** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Telakowska), [MNW manual](https://www.mnw.art.pl/gfx/muzeumnarodowe/en/defaultstronaopisowa/103/3/1/mnw_ci_manual.pdf)) → folk as method; ornament derived from the symbol → per-surname ornaments generated by one grammar from each name's own letters and diacritics. Polishness comes from process and orthography, not borrowed motifs.

### Gaps
- None of these principles has been user-tested with a Polish or diaspora audience. Their recognisability as "intelligent and ours" is an interpretation.

### Inferences
- **Primary pairing, "Archive & Signal":**
  - **Półtawski Nowy** for the canonical surname and historical attestations (Polish provenance; ornamental but controlled);
  - **Archivo** for UI and headlines (Polish kreska forms; width axis for constructivist, Blok-style compression and expansion);
  - **IBM Plex Mono** for counts, dates and source IDs (tabular and documentary).
- **Alternative, "Quiet modern":** Chivo + Vollkorn + JetBrains Mono. Every text face in this pairing has PLK kreska forms.
- **Alternative, "Polish-made open source":** Brygada 1918 + Lato (or Signika for its GRAD axis in dark mode) + Plex Mono. Before shipping, visually QA ś ć ń ź at 14–16 px, because these use a single shared acute.
- **Signature or wordmark:** consider licensing *Komunikat FA* or commissioning a small Strzemiński-logic lettering (lines, arcs, dots) whose diacritics are the brand marks. Keep POLIN Sans out, since it is tied to an institution.
- **Use with care:**
  - Playfair Display reads as generic "luxury editorial" (KITSCH RISK of lifestyle-brand cliché).
  - Cormorant is too thin for small UI.
  - GUST fonts are OTF-only and need a licence check before woff2 conversion and web embedding.
- **Implementation rules:**
  - put `<html lang="pl">` (and `lang` on name elements) so browsers apply PLK `locl`;
  - request both `latin` and `latin-ext` subsets, or self-host full files;
  - forbid synthetic bold and italic (missing styles produce fallback diacritics);
  - test with a pangram containing all nine lowercase diacritics, "Zażółć gęślą jaźń", plus uppercase register-style strings such as "ŁUCZAK / LUCZAK", "ŹRÓDŁO", "ŚLĘZAK" and "WIŚNIEWSKA".

### Gaps
- Italic styles and every static weight were not audited for PLK; only each family's default roman or variable file was.
- The GUST Font License terms for web use were not reviewed.
- Twardoch's Ł-stroke guidance (bot-blocked) is missing, so Ł quality needs manual review in every candidate font.
- Visual quality of the "shared acute" families' kreska was not assessed (no rendering comparison was made).
