## 1. Academic state of the art for non-experts: which channels read as "less certain", and what works for ORDINAL VERBAL confidence levels?

### Takeaway
The strongest empirical guidance for *ordinal* uncertainty on discrete items comes from MacEachren et al. (2012). **Fuzziness, location and value (lightness)** were rated "good", while **arrangement, size and transparency** were "acceptable". **Saturation, hue, orientation and shape were "unacceptable"**, and each channel only works in one direction: fuzzier, lighter, further from centre, more disordered, smaller or more obscured = less certain. For verbal scales, the evidence says three things. Words are interpreted variably and regressively (towards the middle). People treat unlabelled evidence as high quality. And qualitative confidence statements *do* shift trust and decisions appropriately, with only a small trust cost to the communicator.

### Cited Findings (FACT)

**Frameworks and typologies**
- MacEachren et al. build on a typology that matches **nine uncertainty types** to space, time and attribute: accuracy/error, precision, completeness, consistency, lineage, currency, credibility, subjectivity and interrelatedness (27 conditions). Their table gives attribute-level examples: *consistency* "5 say M; 2 say T"; *lineage* "# of input sources … transformations"; *currency* "C = Tpresent − Tinfo … census data"; *credibility* "analyst vs. informant"; *subjectivity* "fact / guess"; *interrelatedness* "same author" — [MacEachren et al. 2012, IEEE TVCG 18(12), PDF](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf); [IEEE VIS page](https://ieeevis.org/year/2012/paper/infovis/visual-semiotics-uncertainty-visualization-empirical-study)
- van der Bles et al. (2019) propose a communication framework: who, what (object, source, level, magnitude), form, to whom, and effect. They distinguish **direct uncertainty**, which covers the fact itself *including "relative support for competing hypotheses"*, from **indirect uncertainty**, a "meta-level reflection on the adequacy of evidence upon which to make any judgement whatever" — [van der Bles et al. 2019, Royal Society Open Science (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549952)
- The same paper ranks **nine expressions of direct uncertainty**, from most to least precise: (1) full probability distribution; (2) summary (intervals, error bars, fan charts); (3) rounded figure, range or order of magnitude; (4) **predefined categorisation** (e.g. "likely" = 66–100%); (5) qualifying verbal statements; (6) **list of possibilities**; (7) **humility, i.e. mentioning that uncertainty exists**; (8) no mention; (9) explicit denial — [van der Bles et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549952)
- Indirect uncertainty is usually codified as quality-of-evidence ratings: GRADE, the IPCC evidence/agreement approach, and the UK Education Endowment Foundation's "padlock" ratings. The authors note "broad categorical ratings are used when the impact of poorer quality evidence is difficult to quantify" — [van der Bles et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549952)
- Box 2 of that paper recommends acknowledging an "other" category for unidentified possibilities (Cromwell's law, "the humility to admit the possibility of being wrong"), noting "It will generally be challenging to place a probability on this 'other' category" — [van der Bles et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549952)

**Visual channels read as "less certain"**
- MacEachren et al. tested 11 of 12 visual variables as three-step ordinal symbol sets: location, size, hue, value, grain, orientation, shape, saturation, arrangement, clarity/fuzziness and transparency. **Resolution was omitted** because it "is applicable to line symbols and images only" — [MacEachren et al. 2012](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)
- Results, quoted: "we find **fuzziness, location, and value to be good** for visualizing discrete entity uncertainty reported at the ordinal level"; "**arrangement, size, and transparency** to be **acceptable**"; "the remaining symbol sets (**saturation, hue, orientation, and shape**) … were therefore deemed as **unacceptable** … particularly interesting for saturation, which is a commonly cited variable thought to be intuitively related to uncertainty" — [MacEachren et al. 2012](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)
- **Direction matters:** "only one direction was deemed intuitive … fuzziness: more fuzzy = less certain; location: further from center = less certain; value: lighter = less certain; arrangement: poorer arrangement = less certain; size: smaller = less certain; transparency: more obscured = less certain" — [MacEachren et al. 2012](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)
- Iconic versus abstract signs: "iconic sign-vehicles can be more intuitive and more accurately judged when aggregated … the abstract sign-vehicles can lead to quicker judgments … iconic sign vehicles only work well if users understand both the aspect of uncertainty being signified and the metaphor". Overall, "the level of iconicity did not have a consistent influence on accuracy" — [MacEachren et al. 2012](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)
- Category-specific "intuitiveness winners" (Table 2): for **attribute + precision**, the iconic winner was a **pencil** (significant). For **attribute + trustworthiness**, the listed winners were a "pie fill" set (abstract) and a "stop light" set (iconic), neither marked significant. The row's column split is ambiguous in the text extraction ("pie fill consistency stop light"), so treat this as weak evidence. The authors say they may not have designed apt metaphors for the "lights" set — [MacEachren et al. 2012](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)
- The authors explicitly list as open questions whether such symbols "work if reduced in size for use on mobile devices" and how uncertainty works in interactive settings, where users control "when uncertainty signification is visible" — [MacEachren et al. 2012](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)
- Boukhelifa et al. (2012) evaluated blur, dashing, grayscale and "sketchiness" (hand-drawn perturbation from "child to adult strokes") for lines. They found "sketchiness … is as intuitive as blur", but "people subjectively prefer dashing style over blur, grayscale and sketchiness" — [AVIZ/Inria project page](https://www.aviz.fr/Research/UncertaintySketchy); [Isenberg publication page](https://tobias.isenberg.cc/p/Boukhelifa2012ESV)
- Wood et al. (2012), *Sketchy Rendering for Information Visualization*: "degree of sketchiness may be judged on an ordinal scale but … its judgement varies strongly between individuals"; "relative area judgment is compromised by sketchy rendering"; "where a visualization is clearly sketchy, engagement may be increased", and users were more positive about annotating sketchy charts — [IEEE VIS 2012 page](https://ieeevis.org/year/2012/paper/infovis/sketchy-rendering-information-visualization)
- Value-Suppressing Uncertainty Palettes (VSUPs) "allocate larger ranges of a visual channel to data when uncertainty is low, and smaller ranges when uncertainty is high". In a crowdsourced evaluation, VSUPs led people "to more heavily weight uncertainty information in decision-making" than bivariate maps did — [Correll, Moritz & Heer, CHI 2018](https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/value-suppressing-uncertainty-palettes)

**Verbal/ordinal scales: interpretation and trust**
- IPCC AR5 uses two metrics. **Confidence** is qualitative, built from evidence (limited/medium/robust) and agreement (low/medium/high), with five qualifiers from "very low" to "very high". **Likelihood** is probabilistic. The Guidance Note states: "Confidence should not be interpreted probabilistically, and it is distinct from 'statistical confidence'"; findings with low and very low confidence "should be reserved for areas of major concern, and the reasons for their presentation should be carefully explained"; authors should "provide a traceable account"; and "Confidence cannot necessarily be assigned for all combinations of evidence and agreement" — [IPCC AR5 Uncertainty Guidance Note (PDF)](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf)
- Calibrated confidence terms "are typeset in italics, e.g., *medium confidence*" (via search summary of the Guidance Note and [Mastrandrea et al. 2011, Climatic Change](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10584-011-0178-6)). In my text extraction of the PDF, the calibrated word appears in a separately encoded font, consistent with italics, but I could not extract the rule sentence verbatim — [IPCC Guidance Note](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf)
- Budescu and colleagues found that lay interpretations of IPCC verbal terms are **regressive** (closer to 50% than intended). For example, "very likely" was mostly read as about 70% rather than over 90%. A **dual verbal + numeric** format increased differentiation and consistency. This held across 25 samples in 24 countries and 17 languages — [Budescu et al. 2014, Nature Climate Change (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcli/v4y2014i6d10.1038_nclimate2194.html); [Fordham news summary](https://now.fordham.edu/?p=28823); [Budescu, Por & Broomell 2012, Climatic Change](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/s10584-011-0330-3)
- van der Bles et al. (2019), reviewing the literature: "There is considerable variation in how verbal expressions of uncertainty are interpreted, both between different people and within the same person in different contexts" — [van der Bles et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549952)
- van der Bles et al. (2020, PNAS) ran five experiments, including a BBC News field experiment, with n = 5,780. People "do perceive greater uncertainty when it is communicated," but "we observed only a **small decrease in trust** in numbers and trustworthiness of the source, and **mostly for verbal** uncertainty communication" — [University of Groningen record / abstract](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/the-effects-of-communicating-uncertainty-on-public-trust-in-facts/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1913678117)
- Padilla, Powell, Kay & Hullman (2021) added **qualitative forecaster-confidence statements** (low/medium/high) to quantile-dotplot forecasts. Participants rated "Low-confidence forecasts as the least trustworthy, Medium- as more trustworthy, and High-confidence forecasts as the most trustworthy", and "where forecasters were less sure … participants made more conservative judgments". The authors "recommend that forecasters present qualitative expressions of model confidence whenever possible" — [Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.579267/full)
- Schneider, Freeman, Spiegelhalter & van der Linden (2021, PLOS ONE; two randomised trials on COVID-19 interventions): telling people the evidence quality was "low" lowered perceived trustworthiness and effectiveness; "high" made no significant difference versus no cue. So **without quality cues, people responded as if the evidence were high quality** (via search summary) — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/10dc6f83311943838557e06ff4be64a9)
- Blastland, Freeman, van der Linden, Marteau & Spiegelhalter, "Five rules for evidence communication" (Nature 2020): inform, not persuade; balance, not false balance; disclose uncertainties; **state evidence quality**; pre-empt misunderstandings. Also: "Avoid unwarranted certainty, neat narratives and partisan presentation" — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v587y2020i7834d10.1038_d41586-020-03189-1.html); [Oxford MPLS summary](https://www.mpls.ox.ac.uk/public-engagement/latest/read-five-rules-to-communicate-evidence)
- The US intelligence standard ICD 203 separates **likelihood** from **analytic confidence** (based on "quantity and quality of source material"). A product should state both, "but not in the same sentence" — [Wikipedia: Analytic confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_confidence) (secondary; the dni.gov PDF did not load)
- GRADE certainty of evidence is a 4-level ordinal scale with a **filled/empty glyph count**: High ⊕⊕⊕⊕, Moderate ⊕⊕⊕◯, Low ⊕⊕◯◯, Very low ⊕◯◯◯. Each has a plain-language definition (e.g., Low: "the true effect may be substantially different") — [GRADE definitions table (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8564158/table/table4-23969873211024143)
- gwern.net tags essays with a **"confidence" tag** from the Kesselman List of Estimative Words (certain, highly likely, likely, possible, unlikely, highly unlikely, remote, impossible), plus a separate 0–10 "importance" tag. This is adapted from Muflax's "epistemic state" tags — [gwern.net/about](https://gwern.net/about)

**Evaluation practice, frequency framing, animation**
- Hullman et al.'s survey of 86 publications (372 evaluation paths) found a bias toward evaluating uncertainty displays by accuracy rather than **decision quality**, and confusion about uncertainty semantics — [MU Collective project page](https://mucollective.northwestern.edu/project/uncertainty-eval-survey); [Tableau Research](https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/pursuit-error-survey-uncertainty-visualization-evaluation)
- Hypothetical Outcome Plots (HOPs) animate individual draws. "With HOPs, people made much more accurate judgments about plots of two and three quantities. Accuracy was similar … for most questions about distributions of a single quantity." Rationale: HOPs "enable viewers to think in finite terms (i.e., counts)" — [Hullman, Resnick & Adar 2015, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0142444)
- Hullman (2019) surveyed 90 visualisation authors and interviewed 13. Creators acknowledge uncertainty's value yet omit it, and she argues that depicting uncertainty "necessarily reduces degrees of freedom in viewers' statistical inferences" — [arXiv 1908.01697](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01697)

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)
- Name Atlas's scale is **indirect uncertainty** in van der Bles's sense (quality and agreement of evidence), like IPCC "confidence" and GRADE. IPCC explicitly treats this kind of scale as **non-probabilistic**, which is a strong precedent that a percentage-free ordinal scale is legitimate rather than a compromise.
- The six statuses are **not one line**. VERIFIED → LIKELY → POSSIBLE → UNCONFIRMED is an ordinal *support* ladder. CONFLICTING is MacEachren's *consistency* type ("5 say M; 2 say T"), a structural state. INSUFFICIENT_DATA is *completeness*: silence, not weakness. Encoding all six on one opacity or blur ramp would falsely imply that CONFLICTING is "a bit less certain than UNCONFIRMED".
- MacEachren validated **three** ordinal steps. Name Atlas needs **four** on the ladder, and Wood et al. show that single-channel sketchiness is read inconsistently between people. So the ladder should use **redundant channels** (fill count + clarity + value + arrangement) plus the always-present word, never one channel alone.
- **Avoid hue and saturation** as the uncertainty carrier (rated unacceptable), and avoid traffic-light semantics: the stop-light "win" was weak, and red would read as "error", which conflicts with INSUFFICIENT_DATA being a dignified state.
- Budescu's fix (adding numbers) is unavailable, so the anchoring work must be done instead by: (a) always showing the **whole scale** with the current position, as GRADE shows ⊕⊕◯◯; (b) a **concrete evidence basis** in words ("2 independent records, 1790–1820"); (c) a fixed, plain-language definition per status. Counts of records are facts, not probabilities, so they fit the "no percentage" rule.
- Schneider et al. imply that **absence of a label reads as VERIFIED**. Every claim, including verified ones, must carry its status, so that "no hedge" never becomes the silent signal of certainty.
- van der Bles 2020 (verbal cues cost slightly more trust than numeric ranges) and Padilla 2021 (qualitative confidence shifts trust *appropriately*) together suggest that vague hedges ("there is some uncertainty") are the worst option. **Specific, evidence-grounded verbal statuses** are the best available option without numbers.
- VSUP's principle transfers directly: **as confidence falls, reduce the display resolution of the value**. For example: centuries rather than years, regions rather than towns, rarity bands rather than precise ranks. The display itself then refuses to over-claim.
- ICD 203's "not in the same sentence" rule suggests layouts should keep **"which hypothesis" (the claim) and "how solid" (the status)** in separate, consistent slots, and the prose generator should never stack hedges ("possibly likely").

## 2. Missing data and absence: making suppressed, missing or unknown data visible and dignified

### Takeaway
Across critical data studies, visualisation research, statistical-agency practice, genealogy and memorial art, one principle recurs: **absence has kinds and causes, and each deserves its own explicit, named mark**. Statistical agencies separate "did not occur" from "not available" from "confidential". Genealogists separate "nothing found" from "negative evidence". Memorials give absence the *same footprint* as presence (a chair, a shelf sized for 20,000 books). Visualisation research shows that explicit, highlighted gaps preserve perceived data quality better than broken, unexplained gaps.

### Cited Findings (FACT)

**Critical data studies**
- Mimi Onuoha defines missing datasets as "the blank spots that exist in spaces that are otherwise data-saturated" and argues "That which we ignore reveals more than what we give our attention to." She gives four reasons data goes missing: (1) those with collection resources lack incentive; (2) the phenomenon resists quantification; (3) collection costs more than its perceived benefit; (4) absence advantages someone, including protecting vulnerable groups — [Onuoha, "On Missing Data Sets" (GitHub)](https://github.com/MimiOnuoha/missing-datasets)
- D'Ignazio's *Counting Feminicide* describes **counterdata**: "data production, analysis, visualization and circulation … outside of – and often in opposition to – mainstream counting institutions" (via search summary) — [MIT DUSP page](https://dusp.mit.edu/node/642). *Data Feminism* (D'Ignazio & Klein 2020) has a chapter titled "What Gets Counted Counts" (via search summary); the book site returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches — [data-feminism.mitpress.mit.edu](https://data-feminism.mitpress.mit.edu/)
- Accurat / Giorgia Lupi: "Data isn't the infallible god that's keeper of our truth". Co-partner Gabriele Rossi: "Different datasets have different ways to approximate reality. No dataset tells the whole and only truth." The studio designs to acknowledge uncertainty, bias and missing information — [AIGA Eye on Design, Liz Stinson, 2 July 2018](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/accurats-perfectly-imperfect-approach-to-data-visualization/)

**Visualisation research on missing data**
- Song & Szafir, "Where's My Data? Evaluating Visualizations with Missing Data" (IEEE TVCG 25(1), 2018/19), tested highlight, downplay, annotation and removal encodings, with zero-filling, linear interpolation and marginal-mean imputation, in bar and line charts. **Highlighting** the missing (imputed) data and **local linear imputation** gave higher perceived confidence and data quality. Designs that **break visual continuity** reduced perceived quality and could bias interpretation — [CU Boulder VisuaLab project page](https://cmci.colorado.edu/visualab/MissingData); [CU Experts record](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_251271)
- Datawrapper documents that empty cells can surface as "0" in tooltips, "which can be misleading", and recommends replacing nulls with an explicit label — [Datawrapper Academy](https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/my-empty-cells-get-displayed-as-zeros-in-tooltips). It offers **pattern overlays** for "special" map regions — [Datawrapper blog](https://datawrapper.de/blog/pattern-overlay-in-choropleth-maps). It also lets line charts either keep real gaps or connect series, recommending line symbols so readers can see where points exist — [Datawrapper blog](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/connect-all-points-in-line-charts)

**Statistical-agency conventions (suppressed ≠ zero)**

**Absence as evidence (genealogy)**
- Elizabeth Shown Mills' evidence model separates the **source** (the container), the **information** (each assertion) and the **evidence** (information relevant to a question). Evidence may be *direct*, *indirect* or **negative**: "evidence arising from an absence of specific information in extant records where that information could be expected and where that absence suggests an answer". The revised 3×3 Evidence Analysis Process Map was unveiled at NGS 2013 — [Evidence Explained: "Negative Evidence: Evidence that Negates?"](https://evidenceexplained.com/node/1516); [Family Locket: negative search results vs negative evidence](https://familylocket.com/negative-search-results-vs-negative-evidence-when-nothing-found-means-something); [NGS session](https://conference.ngsgenealogy.org/sessions/negative-evidence-negative-findings-appraising-the-significance-of-missing-information/); [GeneaMusings on the 2013 map](https://www.geneamusings.com/2013/05/changes-to-evidence-analysis-process-map.html)

**Absence in art and memorials**
- **Micha Ullman, *Bibliothek / The Empty Library* (1995), Bebelplatz, Berlin** is a subterranean room of **empty white bookshelves** seen through a glass plate in the pavement. - **Rachel Whiteread, Judenplatz Holocaust Memorial ("Nameless Library"), Vienna (unveiled 25 Oct 2000)** casts library shelves inside-out, with book **spines turned inward**: "their titles unknown and their contents hidden". - **Periscopic, "U.S. Gun Deaths" (2013)** draws each 2010 victim as an arc: lived years in orange, **"stolen" potential years as a white continuation**, totalling 413,908 stolen years for 9,595 people (via search summary) — [Oregon Business](https://oregonbusiness.com/12877-lost-years-due-to-gun-deaths-charted-by-oregon-web-dev-firm/); [FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/?p=28506); [Wikipedia: Kim Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Rees)

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)
- Name Atlas needs **at least four distinct absence marks**, never collapsed:
  - *suppressed by the source* (a [c]-type: the number exists but is withheld; show "fewer than N", never 0);
  - *not in our sources / not yet researched* ([x]);
  - *not applicable* ([z]);
  - *true zero* (the GUS "–"), used only where coverage supports it, as `ARCHITECTURE.md` already requires.
  GUS's single *kropka* merges "no information" with "confidentiality". The UK split is the better model for a consumer product.
- Use **words in brackets or small caps rather than bare symbols** for these marks. This follows GAF's accessibility reasoning and avoids "NA", which is ambiguous.
- Whiteread's closed library is a precise visual metaphor for a **suppressed PESEL count**: the record *exists* but is sealed. Ullman's empty shelves fit a **record not collected, or not surviving**: the space is sized for what should be there. Using two different metaphors keeps "withheld" and "absent" apart emotionally as well as semantically.
- Mills' negative evidence gives INSUFFICIENT_DATA a positive, rigorous face. "We searched these archives, where such a name would be expected, and found no attestation before 1800" is a **finding**, not a failure. It may be presented as evidence only where the record would reasonably be expected; otherwise it is a "negative search result".
- Song & Szafir imply that an **explicitly marked gap** reads as more trustworthy than a silently broken or missing element. The unknown slot should be drawn and labelled, not removed from the layout.
- Periscopic's ghost arc suggests a timeline treatment for surname history. **Undocumented centuries are drawn as a faint, labelled continuation** ("the record is silent here"), so the reader sees the unknown span as part of the story rather than as the edge of the chart.
- Emotional register: for Polish and diaspora users, archival absence can carry war, migration and Holocaust history (the Kraków chairs are in the product's own cultural landscape). Absence copy and visuals should therefore be **quiet and respectful**, never cute, and never error-red.

- I could not read *Data Feminism*'s text (HTTP 403), so no chapter-level quotes are included.

## 3. Evidence and provenance aesthetics: what works and what feels gimmicky or untrustworthy

### Takeaway
Trust-building evidence interfaces share three moves: **show the chain, keep the reader in place, and mark gaps explicitly**. Examples include museum provenance punctuation, Wikidata's referenced and ranked statements, gwern-style sidenotes and recursive popups, and Forensic Architecture's time-and-space evidence maps. The weak points are citation theatre (inline citations that do not actually support the sentence) and verdict meters that flatten nuance. Research shows AI-search citations often fail to support their claims. Rating scales help in some conditions, but reputable fact-checkers such as Full Fact reject them as reductive.

### Cited Findings (FACT)

**Investigative aesthetics**
- Forensic Architecture (Goldsmiths; founding year given as 2010 in one source and 2011 in another, via search summary) uses architecture as a method to cross-reference "new media, remote sensing, material investigation and witness testimony". Weizman frames forensics as "an 'aesthetic' practice because it involves the modes and the means by which incidents are sensed and evidence is presented" — [Goldsmiths Research Online: *Forensic Architecture: Towards an Investigative Aesthetics*](https://research.gold.ac.uk/27327); [e-flux announcement](https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/93328/forensic-architecture)
- FA's open-source **TimeMap** is "a tool for exploration, monitoring and classification of incidents in time and space". It maps incidents, filters them on "an adjustable timeline that allows to zoom in and out", and styles them by tag and category. It has been used, for example, for the Battle of Ilovaisk investigation, and the repository is "NO LONGER ACTIVELY MAINTAINED" — [GitHub: forensic-architecture/timemap](https://github.com/forensic-architecture/timemap)
- Bellingcat's beginner guide stresses documenting and archiving ("record/archive what you are doing"; "archive everything that you may need"), says "the real skill is methodology", and points to the Berkeley Protocol for workflow standards — [Bellingcat, Nov 2021](https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/2021/11/09/first-steps-to-getting-started-in-open-source-research/)

**Provenance conventions**
- Museum provenance text (AAM-derived convention) is chronological, from the earliest known owner. **A semicolon means the work passed directly between owners; a period marks a gap**, or a transfer not known to be direct. Dealers go in parentheses, and uncertainty is stated with "possibly", "probably", "reportedly" or "purportedly" (via search summary across museum guides) — [Krannert Art Museum: AAM resource, how to read a provenance narrative](https://kam.illinois.edu/resource/aam-resource-how-read-provenance-narrative); [National Gallery of Art: Reading collection information](https://NGA.gov/collection/collection-information.html); [Yale University Art Gallery: Provenance research](https://artgallery.yale.edu/research-and-learning/provenance-research)
- **Wikidata ranks**: *preferred* = "the most current statement or statements that best represent consensus"; *normal* = default, "no judgement or evaluation of a value's accuracy"; *deprecated* = errors or outdated knowledge. Any number of statements may share a rank. "Ranks are not a way of asserting your view for a disputed value". Disputes use the qualifier "statement disputed by (P1310)", and ranks can be explained with "reason for preferred rank (P7452)" and "reason for deprecated rank (P2241)" — [Wikidata Help:Ranking](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Ranking)
- Wikidata's qualifier **"sourcing circumstances" (P1480)** gives a controlled vocabulary for epistemic hedges: *circa, approximately, near, presumably, possibly, allegedly, hypothetically, uncertain, disputed, dubious, unconfirmed, according to some sources, misprint, misattribution*, etc. — [Wikidata Property:P1480](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P1480)

**Reading interfaces for sources**
- gwern.net places sidenotes "in the left (space permitting) or inline in italics (on mobile)" so readers keep their place. Popups are "a small 'mini-page'" with metadata and abstract, and "popups can be recursive". The principle is to "progressively expose more beyond the default as the reader requests it, and make requesting as easy as possible" (semantic zoom), and link icons give an "instant hint" of a link's content — [gwern.net/design](https://gwern.net/design)
- Hohman et al., "Communicating with Interactive Articles" (Distill 2020): interactive articles "have been shown to be more engaging, can help improve recall and learning". However, the NYT reported "only a fraction of readers interact with non-static content", production is "closer to building a website than writing a blog post", and "interactivity may be distracting … or the functionality may go unused". The article credits Bret Victor's *Explorable Explanations*, the explorables community (170+ articles) and Nicky Case's works (e.g., *Parable of the Polygons*, *LOOPY*) — [Distill](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)

**Claim-with-citation interfaces**
- Liu, Zhang & Liang audited Bing Chat, NeevaAI, perplexity.ai and YouChat. On average, **only 51.5% of generated sentences were fully supported by citations, and only 74.5% of citations supported their associated sentence** — [arXiv 2304.09848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848v2) (Findings of EMNLP 2023)
- scite's Smart Citations classify each citing sentence as **supporting, contrasting or mentioning** and show the citation-context sentence (via search summary) — [CASRAI dictionary](https://www.casrai.org/dictionary/term/scite-smart-citations). A 2021 JMLA review notes that the model was trained on 40,000+ statements and that "most citations are classified as 'mentions', because the classifier is trained to be cautious". It also warns that a high count of mentions "may provide little more nuance … than a total citation count" — [Brody 2021, JMLA (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8608186)
- Consensus's **Consensus Meter** shows the share of retrieved studies answering yes, no or possibly to a yes/no question. Guides caution that it "reflects the retrieved sample, not a formal meta-analysis" (via search summary; secondary sources) — [CASRAI guide to Consensus](https://casrai.org/guides/consensus-ai-search-engine-guide); [The Neuron review](https://theneuron.ai/tools/consensus-ai)

**Fact-check meters**
- PolitiFact's **six-level Truth-O-Meter** runs TRUE, MOSTLY TRUE, HALF TRUE, MOSTLY FALSE, FALSE, PANTS ON FIRE ("not accurate and makes a ridiculous claim"). Ratings are decided by a vote of three editors, and "The burden of proof is on the speaker" — [PolitiFact principles](https://www.politifact.com/article/2018/feb/12/principles-truth-o-meter-politifacts-methodology-i/)
- Full Fact deliberately does not use a true–false rating scale "because the real world isn't that simple". Ratings can be reductive, and a claim is often "missing vital context" (via search summary) — [Full Fact FAQ](https://fullfact.org/about/frequently-asked-questions/)
- Amazeen, Thorson, Muddiman & Graves (JMCQ) found that visual **truth scales make corrections more effective in some conditions**, particularly non-political misinformation, and do **not** increase partisan backlash. Corrections work less well on identity-laden political issues (via search summary) — [Institute for PR summary](https://instituteforpr.org/correcting-political-and-consumer-misperceptions-the-effectiveness-and-effects-of-rating-scale-versus-contextual-correction-formats/); [SSRC MediaWell citation](https://mediawell.ssrc.org/citation/correcting-political-and-consumer-misperceptions-the-effectiveness-and-effects-of-rating-scale-versus-contextual-correction-formats/)

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)
- **What works:**
  - *Provenance punctuation*: the semicolon for a direct link and the period for a gap give Name Atlas's chain (Source → Record → Evidence → Claim → Confidence → Citation) a ready grammar. Draw a **solid joint** where a link is directly documented and an **open joint or gap** where it is inferred.
  - *Wikidata's model*: keep multiple referenced values, rank without deleting, and give reasons for ranks.
  - *gwern-style semantic zoom*: one tap from claim to chain without losing place. This fits mobile better than footnotes.
- **What reads as untrustworthy or gimmicky:**
  - *Citation theatre*: numbered chips that do not support the sentence (Liu et al.). Name Atlas's chain is structural, not decorative, so the interface should make a citation **open onto the actual record excerpt** (transcription, archive, signature, date), not just a link.
  - *Snark*: "Pants on Fire"-style names undermine a professional tone.
  - *Sample counts presented as consensus*: vote-count meters (Consensus) that count retrieved papers as if they were evidence weight.
- The Amazeen result supports a **compact visual scale** for non-political, low-identity claims like surname facts. Full Fact's critique argues for **always pairing the badge with its reason**, never a bare verdict.
- The repo requires Source authority, EvidenceItem verification and ConfidenceAssessment to stay separate. Visually, this means **three distinct layers in the provenance drawer**: who the source is, whether the record is verified, and how strongly the claim is supported. They should not be merged into one "trust score".
- FA's TimeMap (time × place × evidence tags) is a strong template for the **historical dimension**: attestations as dated, placed points, each opening its record, with gaps visible on the timeline.
- Interactivity is optional for most readers (Distill). The **default static view must already carry status, reason and date**, and interaction should only deepen it.

### Gaps
- **NYT Visual Investigations** methods and **Bellingcat** case-study visuals (e.g., side-by-side geolocation overlays) were not verified. Only Bellingcat's methodology guide was read.
- No retrieved study of **museum visitors' comprehension** of provenance punctuation.
- Elicit's supporting-quote tables and Semantic Scholar's citation-intent features were not verified this session.
- scite's per-class classifier accuracy figures (e.g., "97% / 64% / 59%") appeared only in a secondary search summary and were **not** confirmed in the review I read. Do not cite them.

## 4. Showing competing hypotheses side by side, and showing source conflict without resolving it

### Takeaway
Rigorous traditions make **conflict first-class and explicit, rather than averaged**: ACH matrices, genealogy's "resolution of conflicting evidence", Wikidata's "statement disputed by", and museum "possibly/probably". Resolution is required only when it can be soundly reasoned. The research also warns that structured methods (ACH) do not automatically de-bias, and that even experts given identical data reach different answers (NYT's four pollsters). Showing *why* conflict exists is therefore more honest than implying a hidden right answer.

### Cited Findings (FACT)
- **Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (Heuer, *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis*, ch. 8)**: build a matrix with hypotheses as columns and evidence as rows, mark each cell consistent, inconsistent or not applicable, and try to **disconfirm** hypotheses rather than confirm them, to counter confirmation bias — [CIA (PDF)](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf); [SANS ISC summary](https://isc.sans.edu/diary/22460)
- **ACH's limits**: in an experiment with 50 intelligence analysts, those trained in ACH "did not follow all of the steps". There was "mixed evidence for ACH's ability to reduce confirmation bias", and ACH "may increase judgement inconsistency and error" — [Dhami, Belton & Mandel 2019, Applied Cognitive Psychology (Strathprints)](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/69049/)
- **Same data, different answers**: the NYT Upshot gave one poll's raw data to four reputable pollsters, who returned four different results because of judgement calls such as likely-voter models and weighting — [FlowingData summary of Cohn, NYT Upshot, Sept 2016](https://flowingdata.com/2016/09/21/statistics-lesson-on-polling/)
- The **Genealogical Proof Standard** has five elements: "Reasonably exhaustive research", "Complete and accurate source citations", "Thorough analysis and correlation", "**Resolution of conflicting evidence**", "Soundly written conclusion based on the strongest evidence" — [Board for Certification of Genealogists](https://bcgcertification.org/ethics-standards/)
- **Wikidata** keeps conflicting values side by side with references, flags disputes with "statement disputed by (P1310)", and forbids using ranks to push a view on disputed values — [Wikidata Help:Ranking](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Ranking). Hedges such as "disputed" and "according to some sources" come from P1480 — [Wikidata P1480](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P1480)
- MacEachren's typology treats **consistency** (e.g., "5 say M; 2 say T") as a distinct uncertainty type from accuracy or precision. It also treats **interrelatedness** ("same author", "source proximity") as a distinct type, i.e., whether sources are independent — [MacEachren et al. 2012](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)
- van der Bles et al. class "relative support for competing hypotheses" as direct uncertainty and list "list of possibilities" as a legitimate expression (#6 of 9). They also recommend acknowledging an unidentified "other" possibility — [van der Bles et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549952)
- scite's "contrasting" class makes published disagreement visible at the level of individual citation sentences (via search summary) — [CASRAI](https://www.casrai.org/dictionary/term/scite-smart-citations)
- Kialo describes itself as a tool "to engage in thoughtful discussion, understand different points of view, and help with collaborative decision-making" — [Kialo About](https://www.kialo.com/about). Its pro/con tree structure was not verified (see Gaps).

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)
- For etymology with competing hypotheses, a **compact ACH-style card** works well: hypotheses in columns (each with its own ladder status), and key evidence rows marked supports / contradicts / silent. Present it as an *explanation of the status*, not as an algorithmic verdict (Dhami). The matrix should also carry an explicit "other / not yet identified" column (van der Bles, Box 2).
- **CONFLICTING should mean "sources disagree and we have not soundly resolved it"**, the honest pre-GPS state. The UI should say *what* the disagreement is (a different root word, a different region, a different date) and *who says what*. Each side is shown **crisply** with its own sources, because conflict is not vagueness, so blur is the wrong channel.
- Show **source independence** (MacEachren's *interrelatedness*). Two dictionaries repeating the same 19th-century author are one voice, not two. A "shared origin" connector between citations prevents false corroboration from looking like consensus.
- Use **juxtaposition on a shared baseline** (equal size, equal visual weight) when the evidence is balanced. Only order hypotheses when their ladder statuses differ. Never centre one hypothesis and marginalise the other if the evidence doesn't justify it.
- The Upshot's four-pollsters piece is a useful narrative pattern: **"Here is how different reasonable readings of the same records lead to different answers."** It dignifies disagreement rather than hiding it.

## 5. Immersive or artistic works that make uncertainty or probability beautiful, or felt

### Takeaway
Uncertainty becomes *felt* through motion, multiplicity and disorder: the NYT needle's jitter, 538's swarms of simulated outcomes, HOPs' animated draws, Nees's *Schotter*, and wind-particle maps. The same channels can also produce **anxiety or false readings**. The needle became a symbol of 2016's "wayward predictions", and the hurricane cone's border is misread as a growing storm. The lesson is to use motion and texture as *ambient, meaningful and user-invoked* signals, never as decoration that invents meaning.

### Cited Findings (FACT)
- **NYT election needle (2016)**: it "fluttered with a constantly updating probability" and "trembled toward a likely Trump presidency". Social media "painted the jittery Times visualization as a catchall symbol for the wayward predictions". For 2018, editor Amanda Cox said "part of our strategy this year is to help people understand more what it is" and "Lots of people left 2016 feeling hurt about polling, or confused about what it is" — [CJR, David Uberti, 18 Oct 2018](https://www.cjr.org/?p=76433)
- A Business Insider report described the jitter as "an artificially added dramatic element" (via search summary; page redirected and was not verified) — [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/new-york-times-election-needle-2016-11/). In 2020 the Times explained that the needle updates from pre-election estimates to modelled estimates as votes arrive, and paired graphics with reporter modules stressing that "polls are useful but not infallible" — [Reuters Institute 2020](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/moving-needle-how-new-york-times-aims-guide-readers-through-americas-most-uncertain-election)
- **FiveThirtyEight 2020 forecast**: Monte Carlo simulations shown as discrete dots conveyed the range of outcomes "more explicitly than the 2016 bar". The page opened with a series of possible maps rather than one, and a mascot, **Fivey Fox**, gave interpretive hints, compared by commentators to Microsoft's Clippy (via search summaries) — [Nieman Lab, July 2020](https://www.niemanlab.org/2020/07/this-is-how-fivethirtyeight-is-trying-to-build-the-right-amount-of-uncertainty-into-its-2020-election-data-analysis/); [FlowingData, Aug 2020](https://flowingdata.com/2020/08/12/fivethirtyeight-launches-2020-election-forecast). Observed on 2026-09-30: the original 538 design article URL now redirects to ABC News and could not be read — [538 URL](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-we-designed-the-look-of-our-2020-forecast)
- **Hurricane "cone of uncertainty"**: NOAA's literature review reports that the cone "may be both the most viewed and the most misinterpreted product". It is widely misunderstood as the storm's impact area, and viewers "incorrectly believe that it depicts the size or intensity of the hurricane, which grows as the storm approaches land". The cone's **border** is a suspected cause (Padilla et al. 2017; Ruginski et al. 2016). The track (centre) line was criticised for "leading residents to believe they were safe" and for conveying "more certainty than actually exists" (Broad et al. 2007). The review's Table 1 also lists believing one is "safe" if located outside the cone — [NOAA AOML, Cone of Uncertainty Literature Review (PDF, 2019)](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/general/lib/lib1/nhclib/Bibliographies/Cone%20of%20Uncertainty%20Literature%20Review_4_10_19%20%281%29%20%281%29.pdf)
- **HOPs** turn probability into an experience of successive concrete outcomes and improved multi-variable judgements (Section 1) — [PLOS ONE 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0142444)
- **Georg Nees, *Schotter* (1968–70)**: a computer-generated plotter work that "starts with a standard row of 12 squares and gradually increases the magnitude of randomness in the rotation and location of the squares" down the rows. It is an exploration of order versus disorder, held by the V&A — [V&A collection O221321](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O221321/schotter-print-nees-georg/); [Wikipedia: Georg Nees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Nees)
- **earth.nullschool.net** (Cameron Beccario) animates global wind and ocean data from GFS (updated every three hours), OSCAR and others. It was inspired by the **HINT.FM Wind Map** by Fernanda Viégas and Martin Wattenberg — [earth.nullschool.net/about](https://earth.nullschool.net/about.html)
- **Sketchiness** increases engagement and willingness to annotate (Wood et al.), and Accurat treats imperfection as expressive material — [IEEE VIS 2012](https://ieeevis.org/year/2012/paper/infovis/sketchy-rendering-information-visualization); [AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/accurats-perfectly-imperfect-approach-to-data-visualization/)
- Periscopic's ghost arcs make **counterfactual absence** emotionally legible (Section 2) — [FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/?p=28506)

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)
- ***Schotter* is a near-literal art-historical match to MacEachren's finding** that "poorer arrangement = less certain". A "Schotter gradient" (ordered → displaced marks) is a beautiful, legible, professional signature for the VERIFIED → UNCONFIRMED ladder. It needs no hue and survives greyscale.
- The needle warns that **perpetual motion reads as anxiety**, and is resented if it is not data-driven. Name Atlas claims are not live forecasts, so they should be **still by default**. Motion should be reserved for user-invoked exploration, for example "show me the competing readings" cycling HOPs-style with equal dwell times, and must honour reduced-motion settings.
- The hurricane cone shows that **a crisp outline around an uncertain region gets read as a thing**. For Name Atlas, a hard boundary around "where the name comes from" on a map will be read as a territory. Low-confidence geography should be drawn as **soft density or scattered attestation points**, never a filled, bordered region. This is also VSUP-style resolution reduction.
- 538's multiple maps and dot swarms show the value of **plural renderings**. For CONFLICTING etymologies, showing each hypothesis as its own small, complete "possible story" can feel richer and more honest than one compromise paragraph. A mascot (Fivey Fox) is a risky tonal fit for a product about family names and possibly traumatic histories.
- Wind maps show that **ambient, slow, continuous texture** can make a dataset feel alive without anxiety. A subtle living grain on UNCONFIRMED marks (paper fibre, pencil tooth) can say "still being drawn" without alarm, provided it stays static or very slow.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a primary NYT or Gregor Aisch explanation of what the 2016 jitter encoded. The claim that it was "artificial" rests on a secondary summary only.
- No lay study found on generative-art disorder (e.g., Schotter-like displacement) as an uncertainty cue beyond MacEachren's "arrangement" result.
- The IEEE VIS 2020 paper "Beautiful Visualizations Slain by Ugly Facts: Redesigning the National Hurricane Center's 'Cone of Uncertainty' Map" was found but not read — [VIS 2020 page](https://virtual.ieeevis.org/year/2020/paper_w-comm-1023.html).

## 6. Design implications for a six-level verbal confidence scale

### Takeaway
Treat the six statuses as **one ordinal ladder of support (4 rungs) plus two structural states**. Encode the ladder with **redundant, evidence-backed channels**: fill count (GRADE-like), edge clarity (fuzziness), ink value (lightness) and arrangement (a Schotter-like displacement). **The word is always present and always primary.** Encode CONFLICTING as a *split* (crisp and plural) and INSUFFICIENT_DATA as a *reserved, labelled space*. Never use hue, blur on text, or perpetual motion as the carrier, and never let rights restrictions borrow the uncertainty channels.

### Cited Findings (FACT: evidence base for the recommendations)
- Good/acceptable/unacceptable channels and their directions, three-step ordinal tests, pencil as the iconic winner for attribute precision — [MacEachren et al. 2012](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)
- Dashing preferred; sketchiness as intuitive as blur — [Boukhelifa et al. 2012](https://www.aviz.fr/Research/UncertaintySketchy). Sketchiness is ordinal but varies between people and increases engagement — [Wood et al. 2012](https://ieeevis.org/year/2012/paper/infovis/sketchy-rendering-information-visualization)
- Filled-glyph ordinal convention — [GRADE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8564158/table/table4-23969873211024143)
- Non-probabilistic confidence; low confidence needs explained reasons; traceable account; confidence not assignable for every combination — [IPCC Guidance Note](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf)
- Unlabelled evidence is treated as high quality — [Schneider et al. 2021](https://doaj.org/article/10dc6f83311943838557e06ff4be64a9). Verbal confidence shifts trust and decisions appropriately — [Padilla et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.579267/full). The trust cost of uncertainty communication is small — [van der Bles et al. 2020](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/the-effects-of-communicating-uncertainty-on-public-trust-in-facts/)
- Verbal terms are read regressively; anchoring helps — [Budescu et al. 2014](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcli/v4y2014i6d10.1038_nclimate2194.html)
- Likelihood and confidence belong in separate statements — [ICD 203 via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_confidence)
- Value suppression under uncertainty — [VSUP, Correll, Moritz & Heer 2018](https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/value-suppressing-uncertainty-palettes)
- Conflict kept plural, with references — [Wikidata Help:Ranking](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Ranking). Consistency is a distinct uncertainty type — [MacEachren et al. 2012](https://geography.wisc.edu/cartography/projects/publications/MacEachrenEtAl_2012_TVCG.pdf)
- Suppressed ≠ zero, named absence marks — [GUS](https://olsztyn.stat.gov.pl/files/gfx/olsztyn/en/defaultstronaopisowa/761/1/2/2024_objasnienia_znakow_umownych.pdf); [UK GAF](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/symbols-in-tables-definitions-and-help/)
- Motion as anxiety — [CJR on the NYT needle](https://www.cjr.org/?p=76433). Crisp borders around uncertain areas are misread — [NOAA cone review](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/general/lib/lib1/nhclib/Bibliographies/Cone%20of%20Uncertainty%20Literature%20Review_4_10_19%20%281%29%20%281%29.pdf)

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION: recommended encodings)

**A. The ladder (ordinal support): VERIFIED > LIKELY > POSSIBLE > UNCONFIRMED**

| Status | Mark (primary glyph) | Edge / stroke (clarity) | Ink value & surface | Position & arrangement | Motion | Words (always shown) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VERIFIED** | Closed vessel/seal, **4 of 4** segments filled | Crisp vector edge, solid full-weight stroke | Darkest ink, flat (no grain) | On the grid/baseline, nearest the name's "core" | None | Status word + **source + dataset date** inline (e.g., "PESEL registry · as of <date>") |
| **LIKELY** | **3 of 4** filled | Crisp edge, solid slightly lighter stroke | One clear value step lighter | On grid, first ring out | None | Status + one-clause basis ("2 independent records agree") |
| **POSSIBLE** | **2 of 4** filled | **Dashed** outline, or a soft feather/halo on the *mark only* | Mid value | Slight rotation/offset (Schotter step) | None | Status + "one of N explanations" link when alternatives exist |
| **UNCONFIRMED** | **1 of 4** filled | **Pencil-sketch** outline ("pencilled in, not inked") | Light graphite on paper, faint static grain | Visibly loosened from grid, outer ring | None (at most a very slow static-feeling texture) | Status + what is missing ("single secondary source; no primary record yet") + invitation ("Have a record?") |

Rationale per rung:
- **Fill count**: GRADE-like counts show both position and maximum, which counters the regressive reading of words (Budescu). MacEachren's "pie fill" set was a (non-significant) winner for attribute trustworthiness.
- **Clarity and value**: fuzziness and lightness were rated "good" (MacEachren). Dashing was preferred by users (Boukhelifa). Pencil was an intuitive iconic sign for imprecision (MacEachren), and sketchiness invites annotation (Wood).
- **Arrangement and location**: "poorer arrangement" and "further from center = less certain" (MacEachren), echoed by *Schotter*.
- **Redundancy is mandatory**: only three ordinal steps were validated, and sketchiness is read variably, so no single channel carries the ladder.

**B. Structural states (not rungs)**

| Status | Mark | Edge / stroke | Ink | Layout | Motion | Words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CONFLICTING** | **Split vessel**: two (or more) half-marks with a visible seam, *or* a double-exposure offset. Each half keeps **its own ladder fill** for its own hypothesis | **Crisp on both sides** (conflict is between claims, not vagueness within one) | Each side at its own value; neutral seam; no red | **Juxtaposed on a shared baseline, equal size**; order only if ladder statuses differ; "shared origin" connector when sources aren't independent | Optional, user-invoked alternation (HOPs-style, equal dwell); off under reduced motion | "Sources disagree" + who says what + "not resolved" + link to the evidence matrix (supports / contradicts / silent, with an "other" column) |
| **INSUFFICIENT_DATA** | **Empty vessel of identical footprint** with a fine dotted rim: a *reserved place*, not a missing element and not a lone "?" | Hairline dotted | Paper tone; no grain (silence, not noise); never error-red | Sits **exactly where the answer would be** (the chair at the table) | None | "We don't know yet" + **reason class** + **what we checked, and when** + what would change it |

INSUFFICIENT_DATA sub-marks (distinct from each other and from zero):
- **Suppressed by source**: a *sealed* vessel (Whiteread) with the text "fewer than N — the source does not publish smaller counts".
- **Not in our sources / not yet researched**: open, empty shelves (Ullman).
- **Not applicable**: [z]-type plain text.
- **True zero**: a dash "–", used only where coverage supports it (per `ARCHITECTURE.md`).

**C. System rules**
1. **The word is the carrier; visuals reinforce.** Status words get one consistent typographic treatment (e.g., small caps or italics, following the IPCC practice of marking calibrated terms) linked to a fixed definition. Do not use colour alone. Keep hue for brand and categories, not for confidence (hue and saturation were rated unacceptable).
2. **Every claim is labelled, including VERIFIED.** An unlabelled claim will be read as high quality (Schneider).
3. **Always show the full scale in miniature** (four segments, plus the two structural icons in the legend), so a status is read *relative to the scale* rather than as a free-floating word (Budescu).
4. **Reason on demand, within one tap.** Each status opens its traceable account (IPCC): Source → Record → Evidence → Claim → Confidence → Citation. Use provenance punctuation: solid joints for documented links, open gaps for inferred links. Add the assessment date and method version, because the repo versions ConfidenceAssessment.
5. **Low statuses carry their reason inline**, not hidden (IPCC: low confidence "should be carefully explained"; Full Fact: never a bare verdict).
6. **Value suppression**: lower status means coarser displayed precision (years → centuries; towns → regions; exact rank → band), following VSUP. Uncertain geography should never get a crisp bordered region (the hurricane-cone lesson).
7. **Separate layers, separate channels.** Source authority, evidence verification, claim confidence and **rights** must be visually distinct. **Rights restrictions must not use fade, blur or transparency**: MacEachren found "more obscured = less certain", so users would misread a licence restriction as doubt. Use a distinct "held at source / shown elsewhere" affordance, with the confidence status still visible (ADR-0001).
8. **One status per sentence** in generated prose, with no stacked hedges (ICD 203). For example: "Likely: two independent records agree", not "this likely possibly derives…".
9. **Stillness by default.** Motion only on request, never jitter-as-signal (the NYT needle lesson), and always respecting reduced motion.
10. **Blur never touches text.** Clarity effects apply to marks, halos and edges, keeping words fully legible. Sketchiness and grain should look **crafted** (pencil, paper), never like a rendering failure. This is an inference; accessibility-specific evidence was not collected here.

### Gaps
- None of these encodings has been tested with Polish or diaspora lay users. The priority tests are:
  1. Can users order the four rungs correctly on mobile at small sizes? (MacEachren flagged mobile scaling as open.)
  2. Do users read the split vessel as "disagreement" rather than "half-certain"?
  3. Do users read the empty vessel as "unknown" rather than "zero" or "error"?
  4. Polish translations of the six terms, and whether they are read regressively.
- No evidence gathered on colour-vision or screen-reader performance of these specific glyphs. GAF's screen-reader guidance on symbols is the only accessibility evidence here.

## 7. Patterns for dignified absence

### Takeaway
"We do not know" should look **designed, placed, named and dated**: the same footprint as an answer, a distinct mark per kind of absence, a stated cause, a record of what was checked, and a quiet, respectful tone. It should never look like an error, a zero, or an empty layout.

### Cited Findings (FACT: evidence base)
- Distinct symbols for zero, not available and confidential — [GUS](https://olsztyn.stat.gov.pl/files/gfx/olsztyn/en/defaultstronaopisowa/761/1/2/2024_objasnienia_znakow_umownych.pdf); [UK GAF](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/symbols-in-tables-definitions-and-help/); [Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/72-210-g/2016001/userinfo-usagerinfo-eng.htm)
- Nulls shown as zeros mislead — [Datawrapper Academy](https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/my-empty-cells-get-displayed-as-zeros-in-tooltips). Highlighted gaps beat broken continuity — [Song & Szafir](https://cmci.colorado.edu/visualab/MissingData)
- Causes of missingness — [Onuoha](https://github.com/MimiOnuoha/missing-datasets). Negative evidence — [Evidence Explained](https://evidenceexplained.com/node/1516)
- Memorial language of absence — [Ullman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Empty_Library); [Whiteread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judenplatz_Holocaust_Memorial); [Kraków chairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohater%C3%B3w_Getta_Square); [OKC chairs](https://memorialmuseum.com/experience/the-memorial/). Ghost continuation — [Periscopic](https://flowingdata.com/?p=28506)
- Humility as a legitimate expression of uncertainty — [van der Bles et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549952). Confidence not always assignable — [IPCC](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf)
- Semantic zoom on request — [gwern.net/design](https://gwern.net/design). Sketchy or unfinished forms invite contribution — [Wood et al.](https://ieeevis.org/year/2012/paper/infovis/sketchy-rendering-information-visualization)

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION: patterns)
1. **The reserved seat.** Draw the unknown in the exact place and at the exact size the answer would occupy, like an empty chair at the table. Removing the slot makes the product look broken; keeping it makes absence part of the composition.
2. **Name the kind of absence.** At minimum, separate: *suppressed by source* / *not in our sources yet* / *not applicable* / *true zero*. Each has its own mark and wording (GUS, UK and StatCan all separate these).
3. **Suppressed is a band, not a blank.** Show "fewer than N (the source does not publish smaller counts)". That is informative, true, and never "0".
4. **State the cause in one line.** Use Onuoha's causes as a copy taxonomy: not collected, withheld for privacy, not digitised, not surviving, not yet researched by us.
5. **Show the search trail.** "We checked: [archives/datasets], on [date]." This turns silence into a documented result. It qualifies as *negative evidence* only where a record would be expected; otherwise say "not found" (Mills).
6. **Say what would change it.** For example: "A dated parish record naming this surname before 1800 would let us assess this." Absence then becomes an open question rather than a dead end.
7. **Date the absence.** Unknowns are time-stamped claims too (MacEachren's *currency*). "As of <dataset date>" keeps "we don't know" honest as sources grow.
8. **Quiet, warm visual register.** Use paper tone, hairline dotted rims and generous whitespace. Never red, warning triangles, spinners or error pages. Absence is a state, not a fault.
9. **Two metaphors for two absences.** Use the sealed library (Whiteread) for *exists but withheld*, and the empty shelves (Ullman) for *not collected or not surviving*. The emotional reading then matches the semantic difference.
10. **Let the reader lean in.** Absence rewards attention, as in Ullman's glass plate. Keep the default calm, and put the full explanation one tap deeper (gwern-style semantic zoom), never in an alarming banner.
11. **Ghost continuation on timelines.** Draw undocumented centuries as a faint, labelled continuation ("the record is silent here") instead of cropping the axis, so the unknown span is visible as history (Periscopic).
12. **No false fill.** Never interpolate, impute or generate a plausible value to avoid a gap. If any estimate is ever shown elsewhere, mark it visibly (Song & Szafir). INSUFFICIENT_DATA is preferred over a guess (`PRODUCT.md` rule 7).
13. **Absence is on the same scale as everything else.** INSUFFICIENT_DATA appears in the same legend and uses the same vessel system as the other statuses, so users learn it as the sixth state, not an exception screen.
14. **Invite without obliging.** An unfinished (pencil or dotted) form can carry a gentle "Do you hold a record?" (Wood: sketchy forms invite annotation; D'Ignazio: counterdata). Contributions must enter as Source → Record → Evidence and never bypass the chain.
15. **Respect the history behind silence.** For many Polish and diaspora families, missing records reflect war, border changes, emigration or persecution. Wording such as "the records are silent" and a memorial-grade visual restraint fits better than product cheerfulness (Kraków, Vienna and Berlin memorial precedents).
16. **Separate absence from restriction.** Something withheld for **rights** reasons is not unknown. Show "available at the source" with its confidence status intact, and never render it as the empty vessel or with fade or blur (ADR-0001; MacEachren "more obscured = less certain").

### Gaps
- No user research found on how lay audiences feel about "we don't know" states in consumer genealogy or name products. Patterns 1, 8, 9, 10 and 15 are design interpretations drawn from memorial and art practice, not tested findings.
- The PESEL suppression threshold (N) and the source's exact wording must be confirmed from the dataset documentation before writing pattern 3's copy.
