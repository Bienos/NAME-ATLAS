## 1. Signature works and visual languages of key practitioners (core metaphor, how emotion arises, URL)

### Takeaway
In these works, emotion does not come from spectacle. It comes from three moves. (a) Individuals stay individually visible: Periscopic's "stolen years", the 9/11 names, Dear Data. (b) A hand-made, openly "imperfect" texture signals that people made it: Lupi, Posavec, Du Bois. (c) Scale emerges from many small marks instead of one headline number: Flight Patterns, All Streets, Multiplicity. The closest precedent for Name Atlas's core interaction (type a name and the visual responds) is Wattenberg's 2005 NameVoyager, which reacted letter by letter (see §2).

## 2. Name-specific visualisations: what exists, and what is fresh vs. generic

### Takeaway
There is one much-loved canonical names visualisation: NameVoyager (2005). Its key idea, a chart that responds letter by letter while you type and so reveals prefix families, is still under-used and transfers directly to a surname search. Surname products otherwise converge on one generic pattern: a choropleth map of "where people with your surname live" plus a count. Fresh territory remains in three places: showing variants and misspellings as a family of forms, separating living from all-time populations, and presenting a count without a map.

### Cited Findings (FACT)
- **NameVoyager** (Martin Wattenberg with Laura Wattenberg, 2005): "The Name Voyager lets you type in a name and see a graph of its popularity over the past century. But there's a twist: as you type the visualization shows, letter by letter, the overall popularity of the letters you've entered so far". Examples: "'Kr-' names peaked in the 70s". A 2008 "Name Mapper" showed geographic trends over four decades, and the site received "millions of visits" — [bewitched.com](https://www.bewitched.com/namevoyager.html)
- Each name is a stacked stripe whose thickness is proportional to its frequency per million babies, coloured pink or blue, with brightness set by recent popularity. It drew 500,000+ visits in the first two weeks and about 6,000 a day eight months later — [Wattenberg paper (PDF)](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/8001/wattenberg06.pdf) (via search summary)

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)

| Pattern | Examples | Verdict for Name Atlas |
|---|---|---|
| Choropleth of "where your surname lives" | nazwiska-polskie.pl, moikrewni/MyHeritage, Forebears, WorldNames | **Generic.** It is the incumbent Polish pattern, and the brief says regional data may be unavailable. Avoid it as the hero. |
| Popularity line or stacked stream over time | NameVoyager, NameGrapher | A classic for first names built on yearly birth data. PESEL gives a snapshot, not yearly surname births, so a time stream would be invented. |
| Letter-by-letter narrowing as you type | NameVoyager | **Fresh for surnames and a very strong fit.** Each keystroke can show the real count of surnames and bearers matching the prefix. For Polish it must branch at diacritics (Ł ≠ L). |
| Variant and misspelling families | Pudding "Gyllenhaal" | **Fresh.** Show documented spellings as neighbours, not as descendants. |
| Living vs. all-time population | FiveThirtyEight (archived) | A conceptually fresh distinction. PESEL is living-only and has no deceased data, so only the living side can be drawn. |
| Historical spread of "family groups" | GBNames (UK census microdata) | Works in the UK because of I-CeM microdata. Name Atlas's stated sources (PESEL counts plus archive attestations) do not support "spread" or "family group" claims. |

- The PESEL exclusion of single-bearer surnames has a design consequence. A surname missing from the list may have 0 or 1 living bearer, or may be spelled differently. A "not found" state must never render as "0 people". This matches the repository invariant "Suppressed or absent source data is not zero".

## 3. Making a count or rarity felt: unit visualisation, "1 in X", scale reveals, human framings

### Takeaway
The research evidence is sobering. People-shaped "anthropographics" have at best small, uncertain effects on compassion. Icon or unit arrays, however, measurably improve how accurately people perceive proportions: they cancel the "1-in-X" inflation. For a figure like 4,318 bearers, a literal one-mark-per-person field fits on a phone. The most honest "felt" devices are a scale descent from the whole population to this name, and translating the count into time. Faces or figures are not.

### Cited Findings (FACT)
**Unit and individual framings**
- Isotype's first rule: "greater quantities are not represented by an enlarged pictogram but by a greater number of the same-sized pictogram". The method was developed at Vienna's Gesellschafts- und Wirtschaftsmuseum (1925–34) by Otto Neurath, Gerd Arntz and Marie Reidemeister (later Neurath), with the aim of bringing "dead statistics" to life — [Wikipedia: Isotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotype_(picture_language))
- The Vietnam Veterans Memorial lists names "in the chronological order of the dates of casualty": 57,939 at the 1982 dedication and 58,320 by May 2018 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Veterans_Memorial). The Memorial Fund says chronological order lets families pick out their person from others with the same common name — [vvmf.org](https://www.vvmf.org/About-The-Wall/the-names/) (via search summary; bot-blocked)
- 9/11 Memorial names were arranged by relationship adjacency (see §1) — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/september-11-memorial)
- The AIDS Memorial Quilt was first displayed on 11 October 1987 with 1,920 panels, while participants read the names aloud. By October 1992 it had over 21,000 panels — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS_Memorial_Quilt). The Quilt's site offers "Search for Names on the AIDS Quilt" — [aidsmemorial.org/quilt](https://www.aidsmemorial.org/quilt)

  - In 1965, in his Warsaw studio, he began painting numbers "from one to infinity". Every canvas ("Detail") measures 196 × 135 cm, "the dimension of his studio door in Warsaw".

**Evidence on what these devices do**
- A design space of "anthropographics" built from 105 visualisations has seven dimensions. Four concern what is shown: granularity, specificity, coverage, authenticity. The paper reports a qualitative study pointing to "deeper understanding and emotional engagement" — [arXiv:2605.31026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31026)
- **The 1-in-X effect**:
  - "1 in 200" is perceived as bigger than an equivalent "5 in 1000". The effect "is not attenuated by… verbal analogy, but it disappears with an icon array visual aid" (Pighin et al., *Medical Decision Making* 2011) — [iris.unitn.it](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/89289)
  - A 2023 meta-analysis found decisive evidence for the effect, though smaller than previously estimated — [Pighin et al. 2023 (PDF)](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/fc1b9690-a934-486d-b5d4-b13f9653495d/Pighin_etal_2023.pdf) (via search summary)
  - UK family physicians prefer the 1-in-X format (80.4%, n = 131) — [Medical Decision Making 2018 via RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/medema/v38y2018i3p366-376.html) (via search summary)

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION): fit for "about 4,318 living people in Poland carry this name"
1. **A literal unit field is feasible and honest.**
   - 4,318 marks fit on a phone. With a 390 px viewport and 16 px gutters (358 px usable), 34 columns give a pitch of about 10.5 px. Because 4,318 = 34 × 127, it fills a gap-free 34 × 127 block, about 1,337 px tall, or roughly 1.6 screens of scroll.
   - For other counts, use a fixed column count and let the last row stay partial. The ragged last row is an honesty device: it proves the number was not rounded.
   - Place marks by a stable random seed, never on a map and never alphabetically. The Vietnam Wall shows that ordering creates meaning.
2. **Scale descent works better than "rare!".** Scroll from one mark per N people for the whole PESEL population down to the name's own 4,318 marks. This is The Deep Sea / Size of Space grammar applied to people. The denominator must come from the same dataset and date; it was not researched here.
3. **Show "1 in X" only with an icon array.** Pighin et al. show the icon array removes the inflation that the ratio alone creates.
4. **Count as time** (an Opałka-style translation). Counting 4,318 people at one per second takes about 72 minutes. It is an optional, clearly labelled translation of the exact count.
5. **Avoid human figures and faces.** Research shows at best small empathy gains, and realistic figures would imply age, gender or ethnicity that the product must not infer.
6. **Wording.** "About 4,318" signals approximation, but PESEL counts are exact as of a date. The honest form is "4,318 living people (PESEL, as of [date])". That is precision with provenance rather than vagueness.

## 4. Techniques for time layers, lineage and emergence

### Takeaway
Every technique listed has beautiful precedents. For Name Atlas, though, the test is data honesty. Streamgraphs and ridgelines need dated counts, which a single PESEL snapshot lacks. Trees and constellations imply links that surname data does not contain. Strata or cores, braids, barcodes and emergent unit fields fit what the product actually has: one dated count, dated archive attestations, and claims with confidence states.

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)

| Technique | Honest use for Name Atlas | Risk if misused |
|---|---|---|
| Streamgraph | Letter-by-letter prefix families from the *current* snapshot (no time axis), or suffix neighbourhoods by count | A stream over centuries of bearer counts would be fabricated, since there is no dated count series |
| Ridgeline | Only if multiple dated snapshots exist (e.g., future PESEL releases), one ridge per release | The Joy Division look invites a "pulse through history" reading that the data can't support |
| Radial or branching tree | Documented etymological derivations only, with evidence per edge | Implies common ancestry of bearers, or lineage from a coat of arms |
| L-system or growth | Animating the *search process* (branches = sources queried; a bloom = evidence found; withering = searched, nothing found) | Implies the name or population "grew" historically |
| Flow field | Ambient texture clearly marked as decorative, or not used | Reads as migration or dispersal data that does not exist |
| Constellation | Placing documented variant forms near each other, with lines only where a source links them | Edges between people or variants that are construction choices (Albrecht) presented as facts |
| Strata or core | Attestation "layers" by century, each layer present only where a dated source exists; voids labelled "no record found" | Solid fill between attestations implies continuous presence |
| Weaving or textile | Braids for competing origin hypotheses (parallel, never knotted); a quilt of claim patches; an evidence barcode | Threads connecting bearers imply kinship |
| Emergence | A letterform or silhouette that forms from exactly N marks; typing that crystallises the field | A "you" mark, or implied personal membership (see §6) |

## 6. Synthesis for Name Atlas: which metaphors transfer and which mislead

### Takeaway
(INTERPRETATION grounded in §1–5.) The data supports an honest, immersive "emergence" concept built from a small set of true things:
- one dated count, drawn as exactly that many marks;
- the act of typing, which crystallises the field letter by letter;
- a vertical descent through scale;
- archive attestations laid down as dated layers, with voids where nothing was found;
- confidence rendered as material.

Everything that implies links between people, movement across space, or continuity through time is where the product's hard rules are broken.

### Transferable metaphors for a surname product
1. **One grain per living bearer** → the PESEL count at date D → Isotype's one-to-one rule. Exactly N marks, a ragged last row that shows no rounding, and positions that mean nothing.
2. **Declared-value marks for common names** ("1 mark = 10 people", printed on screen) → very large counts → Neurath's fixed-value rule avoids area distortion.
3. **Letter-by-letter crystallisation** (NameVoyager) → as the user types, the field narrows to surnames sharing the prefix, with a real count at every keystroke → every intermediate number is true.
4. **The diacritic fork** → at "L" versus "Ł" the field splits into two populations that never re-merge → it encodes "Łuczak ≠ Luczak" as identity.
5. **Scale descent** (Deep Sea / Size of Space for people) → scroll from the whole living population down to this name's bearers → rarity is felt through real denominators from the same date, not through adjectives.
6. **Icon array beside any "1 in X"** → rarity → the 1-in-X inflation disappears with an icon array (Pighin 2011).
7. **Counting time** (Opałka) → an optional "count them" mode (4,318 at one per second ≈ 72 minutes) → an exact, labelled translation of the count, with no new claim.
8. **"Same, same, but different"** (Multiplicity) → many distinct people sharing one label, each mark with seeded micro-variation → conveys individuality without inventing attributes; the variation is declared decorative.
9. **Meaningless order, stated** (Vietnam Wall lesson) → marks placed by a stable random seed, with a note that "order means nothing" → avoids implied groupings such as region, family or rank.
10. **Evidence core / strata** (History Flow, Ben Fry's Traces) → a vertical core by century. A layer exists only where a dated attestation exists, and each links to its source → gaps render as labelled voids: absence is not zero.
11. **Spelling traces** → forms recorded in sources, aligned by date as parallel traces → variation is documented, not presented as descent.
12. **Evidence barcode** (warming-stripe grammar) → one stripe per century: filled means a record was found; hatched means not searched or unknown → compact and shareable. It encodes records, not people.
13. **Confidence as material** → VERIFIED solid ink; LIKELY dense hatch; POSSIBLE sparse hatch; UNCONFIRMED outline; CONFLICTING two interfering textures; INSUFFICIENT_DATA an empty labelled frame → Lupi's call to "visualize uncertainty"; rights get a separate, orthogonal badge.
14. **Braided hypotheses** → competing origin explanations as parallel threads that never knot into one → CONFLICTING stays visibly unresolved.
15. **Evidence-only edges** (the Albrecht lesson) → related forms placed nearby, with a line only where a source asserts the link, and the line carrying its citation → no constructed connections pass as facts.
16. **Emergent letterform** (All Streets) → exactly N dots settle into the surname's own letters, declared as a typographic container → the form comes from the count and the name, with no map or geography implied.
17. **Discovery growth** (Hyphae / Physarum) → animation of the product's search: branches are sources queried; a node blooms when evidence is found and withers when none is → honest about process, and shows "we looked here and found nothing".
18. **Quilt of claims** (AIDS Quilt, community quilting) → each answered question is a patch with its source stitched on; unanswered ones remain empty frames → modular and additive; missing panels stay visible.
19. **Postcard with a legend on the back** (Dear Data) → a share card: the data portrait on the front, and the dataset date, confidence and sources on the back → shareable without shedding its caveats. The legend-on-back convention comes from prior knowledge of Dear Data, not re-verified.
20. **Sourced human-scale anchor** (Chalabi) → compare N to a familiar, cited quantity → makes the count graspable, but only with a cited comparator and never an invented one.
21. **Zoom to one anonymous mark** (Zoomable Empathic Visualizations) → zoom into a single dot: "one of N living people in Poland with this surname; we know nothing else about them" → the individual becomes felt while the data's limits are stated.
22. **Nonlinear portrait** (Lupi/Accurat) → one dense composition with several entry points (count, rarity, meaning, attestations) → people can get "happily lost", and every element is a claim with a status.
23. **Suffix neighbourhood** → where the name sits among morphologically similar surnames by *current* counts → a snapshot-only comparison; "neighbourhood" is linguistic, not genealogical.

### Metaphors that would mislead
- **Family trees and branching lineages rooted in the surname.** They imply shared descent among unrelated bearers and cross the rule against claims about users' ancestors. Royal Constellations works only because it uses documented genealogy.
- **Constellation lines between bearers**, or between variants, without a sourced link. Albrecht shows that network edges are construction choices, so drawn lines read as facts.
- **Choropleths, heatmaps and "where your name lives" maps as the hero.** This is the incumbent generic pattern, regional data may be unavailable, and it implies precise distribution.
- **Migration arrows, flight trails or wind-style flows** for the name's "spread". Flight Patterns and Wind Map show measured movement; Name Atlas has none.
- **Streamgraphs or ridgelines of bearer counts across centuries.** There is no dated count series. Archive attestations are records, not populations.
- **Solid strata or tree rings between attestations.** They imply continuous presence or an unbroken lineage between two documents.
- **"Ghost" areas for the deceased, or implied birth waves** (FiveThirtyEight style). PESEL's published list excludes deceased persons, and no per-surname birth series is in scope.
- **Growth or decline animations of the population.** From a single snapshot they imply a trend.
- **Human figures, faces or silhouettes per bearer.** Realism implies age, gender or ethnicity, which the product must not infer, and measured empathy gains are small at best.
- **A highlighted "you are one of N" mark.** Diaspora users may not be in the PESEL count, and the product makes no claims about the user.
- **"Extinct", "0 people" or an empty field for names not found.** PESEL omits single-bearer surnames, so absence is not zero.
- **A bare "1 in X" rarity badge, or "rarest name" leaderboards.** They inflate perceived magnitude (Pighin) and turn rarity into status.
- **Crests, heraldic ornament, or noble framing for -ski names.** Coat-of-arms claims are forbidden, and -ski does not denote nobility.
