# ADR-0043 — First names given to children: count, rank and trend as claims

- **Status:** Proposed 2026-10-06. The first build (TASK-137) is internal: no page, no contract and no API field reads these claims. **Amended 2026-10-06 (TASK-139):** the trend compares shares of the children on each list, not counts (D-044 §6, amended; Decision 4 below).
- **Deciders:** the project owner decided the section (D-044) and its trend rule (D-044 §6) on 2026-10-06, and asked for this as the second build step. This ADR records how the claims are derived. The product rules (who sees what, the paid tier) are D-044's and ADR-0037's and are not changed here.
- **Supersedes:** nothing. It extends ADR-0042 (the stored lists) and follows ADR-0032 §6's chain.

## Context

1. ADR-0042 stores dataset 219's national first-name lists, one per year of birth (2000–2025) and sex, in `given_name_birth_lists` (with the publisher's cut-off, `publication_threshold`: 5 up to 2012, 2 from 2013) and `given_name_birth_counts`. Nothing derives from them yet.
2. TASK-122 (migration 0038) derives `GIVEN_NAME_POPULATION` and `GIVEN_NAME_RANK` from the living-register lists (ADR-0032) through evidence → claim → confidence → citation, with a `BASIC_GIVEN_NAME` run, `SHARED` scope, and a rank refused by the completeness gate (`completeness.ts`) unless the stored names are the complete official file.
3. D-044 §6 as first decided: the latest full year's count against the count five years earlier, on the same list; more than 10% higher is rising, more than 10% lower is falling, anything else is steady. Absent five years earlier but on the latest list is rising; absent from the latest list has no direction. Other edge cases are left to this task, which may not treat an absent count as zero.
4. The children on the published lists fell from 186,590 boys and 176,829 girls in 2020 to 126,404 and 119,788 in 2025 (−32%, TASK-136's record). Under the count rule almost every popular name read FALLING: ZOFIA was rank 1 in 2025 but FALLING, though her share of listed girls went only from 4.0% to 3.7%. The owner amended D-044 §6 on 2026-10-06 to compare shares ("yes switch to share and merge it").

## Decision

1. **Three claim types, `SHARED` scope** (migration 0041, check `claims_given_name_birth_scope`), each about one given name:
   - `GIVEN_NAME_BIRTH_COUNT`: the publisher's count on one list. Key `GIVEN_NAME_BIRTH_COUNT|{given_name_id}|{list_id}`.
   - `GIVEN_NAME_BIRTH_RANK`: the competition rank on that list, with the list's size as `universe_size`. Key `GIVEN_NAME_BIRTH_RANK|{given_name_id}|{list_id}`.
   - `GIVEN_NAME_BIRTH_TREND`: `RISING`, `STEADY` or `FALLING`, on the latest list of one sex against the list of the same sex five years earlier. Key `GIVEN_NAME_BIRTH_TREND|{given_name_id}|{latest_list_id}|{earlier_list_id}`.
   Runs reuse `BASIC_GIVEN_NAME`. Counts take the source-native confidence rule; ranks and trends take the derived-statistic rule (`docs/canonical/14` §29–30). A claim is cited to every source record it rests on: one for a count or a rank, two for a trend whose lists are in different resources.
2. **Lists apart.** A count and a rank are about one list; a trend is about two lists of one sex. The boys' and the girls' lists, and lists of different years, are never summed or ranked together.
3. **The rank is exact for every listed name.** A list leaves out only the names given fewer times than its cut-off. Every one of those has a count below the cut-off, and every listed name has a count at or above it, so no unlisted name has a greater count than a listed one. "One plus the number of listed names with a greater count" is therefore also "one plus the number of all names with a greater count". Ties share the lowest rank (`COMPETITION`, as every rank here).
   - The rank is still refused unless the list is complete: the source record's checksum must be the registry's `official_sha256` and the names stored on the list must equal the list's `row_count`, read from the file at import (TASK-009's gate, with the list's own row count). An excerpt is not the list. A list with a publisher placeholder would fail the row count and be refused; no 2000–2025 national list has one (ADR-0042, Context 3).
4. **The trend rule, with its parameters as named constants** (`packages/truth/src/given-name-births.ts`):
   - `BIRTH_TREND_WINDOW_YEARS = 5`, `BIRTH_TREND_CHANGE_PERCENT = 10`. A change of either, or of the rule, is a new method version: `given_name_birth_trend@2` today, the share rule below. `@1` (TASK-137) compared raw counts.
   - **The share** of a name on a list is its count ÷ that list's `people_sum`, the children on that published list as read from the file at import (ADR-0042). **This is a share of the children on the published list, not of all births:** names given fewer times than the list's cut-off are not on the list, so their children are not in the denominator.
   - **The latest year** is the latest year of birth with an active list of that sex. It is read from the data, never written in code (2025 for both sexes on 2026-10-06). The earlier year is the latest minus 5 (2020). Half-year lists are not imported, so every stored list is a full year.
   - **Both lists must pass the completeness gate** (§3). A share needs the list's own `people_sum`, and an excerpt's sum is not the list's; an absence from an excerpt says nothing about the list. Otherwise no trend is written (the gate's reason).
   - **Both listed:** with L and Ls the latest count and `people_sum`, and E and Es the earlier, the latest share is more than 10% above the earlier when L/Ls > 1.1·E/Es. In whole numbers: `100·L·Es > 110·E·Ls` is `RISING`, `100·L·Es < 90·E·Ls` is `FALLING`, else `STEADY`; exactly 10% either way is steady. Each factor fits a PostgreSQL `integer`, so a product can pass `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`: the products are computed in `BigInt`.
   - **Absent five years earlier, on the latest list:** the earlier count is an unknown whole number below the earlier list's stored `publication_threshold` (T), never 0. `RISING` is written only if it holds for the largest such number: `100·L·Es > 110·(T − 1)·Ls`. Otherwise no trend is written (`BELOW_CUTOFF_TOO_CLOSE`). With T = 2 this fails only if the latest list is much smaller than the earlier relative to L; for 2025 against 2020 it holds for every L ≥ 2.
   - **No earlier list stored:** no trend (`NO_EARLIER_LIST`).
   - **Absent from the latest list:** no trend claim, and no count or rank for that year, since no row exists.
   - **What a trend claim states:** the direction; for each list its year, count (null when absent, with `below_publication_threshold` set to T), `people_sum` and `share_per_million` (rounded down, for reading; the comparison uses the exact fraction); and `comparison`, the whole-number test and the earlier count it used (T − 1 when absent). The derived evidence holds the same.
   - **A method version change supersedes.** When the derivation runs for a name, every ACTIVE trend claim of that name with another method version is set `SUPERSEDED`, never deleted, whether or not the new rule writes a claim with the same key. A new claim with that key names the old one in `supersedes_claim_id`. No `@1` trend stays ACTIVE once `@2` has run.
5. **A flagged form gets no claim.** A given name with `form_review` set (ADR-0032 §4, ADR-0042 §5) is skipped and counted as flagged. This differs from TASK-122, which writes claims for flagged forms and leaves them to the surface. No 2000–2025 national list holds such a form.
6. **Idempotent.** An ACTIVE claim with the same key is reused; a name's run is written only when a claim is. A second run over the same lists writes nothing.
7. **A new year is new claims.** The keys name the lists, so a new year's lists (or a superseding version of a resource, whose lists are new rows) give new count, rank and trend claims. The claims on the older lists stay ACTIVE: each is still true of its own lists. Choosing the latest trend to show is the page's read, a later task.

## Consequences

- `pnpm truth:derive-given-name-births` (`-- --name ZOFIA` for one name) derives every claim. On the full 2000–2025 import (TASK-137's record): 35,598 counts, 35,598 ranks, 1,904 trends, no refusal. Under the share rule (TASK-139's record) the same 1,904 trends, with every `@1` trend superseded: boys 771 rising, 68 steady, 126 falling (under `@1`: 621, 98, 246); girls 727, 63, 149 (580, 104, 255).
- No page, contract or API field reads the claims yet; the free/paid split of D-044 §2 is the page task's.
- **Amendment 2026-10-06 (TASK-151):** the baby-names page's share ("3,7% wszystkich dziewczynek z listy") and its "1 na N" are a count claim's count ÷ the same list's stored `people_sum`, computed by the reader (`birthShare` in `packages/truth/src/given-name-birth-facts.ts`): the share in parts per million rounded down, identical to the trend claim's `share_per_million` (§4), and N the sum ÷ the count rounded to a whole number. The page also shows the trend claim's own `latest.share_per_million` and `earlier.share_per_million`. No new claim type: these are readings of the count claim and the list, never stored.
- Shares of all births, voivodeships, half-years, second names and civil registry offices are not derived. Each needs its own lists first (ADR-0042, Consequences).
- Nothing is applied or derived on staging or production by this ADR.
