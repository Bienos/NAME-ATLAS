# Evidence, not counts, will defend Name Atlas

As of September 2026, Name Atlas cannot stand out on PESEL counts. The official surname files are CC0 (public domain), every annual national snapshot since 2019 is still downloadable, and surname.pl, a free site listed on the government data portal in July 2026, already offers 2026 counts, voivodeship maps, a 2019–2026 trend, pronunciation audio and a 65-archive research guide for 390,648 surnames. No Polish or international competitor offers correctness with provenance: per-claim citations with confidence labels and an explicit "we don't know", counts that handle gender forms and diacritics and name their snapshot, dated attestations beyond the ~30,000 surnames in the IJP PAN dictionary, declension for both genders, and a paid surname-level report. Rivals instead mix data years, merge spelling variants without saying so, publish LLM-written etymologies or cite invented books, and in Forebears' case put Poland's third-commonest surname at 315 bearers instead of 107,256. Four moats hold up: an evidence graph gated by publication rights and anchored by a licence from IJP PAN, which produces the dictionary and most of the scholarship behind it; relationships with the institutions that hold data; a trust and citation brand fed by an annual "what changed" release; and a paid report offered only when evidence exists, which would sit in an empty price band between a $30–50 surname book and professional research from $900. The snapshot archive, exclusive Ministry tables and crowd-sourced family data are not moats, and crowd-sourced family data is also a GDPR liability. Beyond its current source list, Name Atlas can lawfully use SGJP inflection data (BSD-licensed) for declension, a BSD-licensed tool for IPA, the official PRNG gazetteer's 44,665 place adjectives for candidate source places, and historical and diaspora counts from the US Census, INSEE, Argentina's RENAPER and a CC0 German First World War casualty dataset. No open source says what a surname means, though, so outside licensed scholarship the honest etymology answer stays INSUFFICIENT_DATA. The sharpest threats are surname.pl's near-identical positioning, version 2.0 of IJP PAN's dictionary (grant-funded, about 50,000 surnames with 2024 counts by 2030) and Google's AI answers in Polish. Polish synthetic voices, bulk archive access, finer Ministry tables and ISNP reuse rights all need legal counsel before they reach a paid surface.

## A free rival, surname.pl, already offers most of the planned free tier

The free tier's core input is a commodity by design. dane.gov.pl dataset 1681 is CC0 1.0, updated every year, and has logged **3,279,006 views and 1,939,483 downloads**. All 21 of its resources are still listed and downloadable, from the 2019-06-30 national files to the 2026-01-20 national and voivodeship files ([dane.gov.pl API](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/1681); [resource list](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/1681/resources)). The portal's showcase registry records the sites built on it: Szukaj Nazwiska (December 2024), BazaImion.pl (April 2026) and surname.pl (July 2026). poland.gg and polskienazwiska.pl republish the same files outside the registry ([showcase search](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/showcases?q=nazwisk); [poland.gg](https://poland.gg/pl/imiona-i-nazwiska/nazwiska); [polskienazwiska.pl](https://polskienazwiska.pl/)). With open data and statically generated pages, a clone costs almost nothing to build, so more are likely after each January release.

surname.pl is the rival that matters. Each of its **390,648 surname pages** carries the 2026 count, the male/female split and rank, and a voivodeship map with a correct caveat that it covers only registered permanent residents. Each page also has a 2019–2026 "Zmiany w czasie" (changes over time) series, syllabified pronunciation with an MP3, and a Cyrillic transliteration. It lists famous bearers drawn from Wikidata, plus record counts from the 2010 US Census, openarch.nl and the "Otwarta lista" of Soviet repression victims ([surname.pl](https://surname.pl/); [Szczepański page](https://surname.pl/szczepanski)). Its archive guide lists **65 archives and databases in 12 countries or areas**, each with search instructions and a licence note, under the line "linkujemy, nie pobieramy danych" (we link, we don't download data) ([surname.pl/archiwa](https://surname.pl/archiwa)). The site is free, with no login, paywall or ads. It calls itself neither a genealogy service nor a tool for identifying living people ([surname.pl/o-nas](https://surname.pl/o-nas)), which is Name Atlas's positioning almost word for word. Its weak spot is the layer Name Atlas cares about most. Etymologies for 10,465 surnames are labelled "Etymologia wygenerowana · AI" and come from "Etymologia LLM (Claude)". Every other surname gets an "algorithmic" line with no content. The methodology page admits that no etymology on the site has been checked against onomastic literature ([surname.pl/metodologia](https://surname.pl/metodologia)).

The rest of the Polish field fills in around it. szukaj-nazwiska.pl adds engagement features: a daily "Pojedynek Nazwisk" (surname duel) guessing game, a "Nazwisko dnia" (surname of the day) and daily Facebook posts. It embeds IJP PAN dictionary text on each page and earns money through a MyHeritage affiliate link ([szukaj-nazwiska.pl](https://szukaj-nazwiska.pl/)). Networks of multilingual SEO sites funded by AdSense ads, such as pochodzenienazwiska.com and mapanazwisk.com, hold a page for every surname in nine languages ([pochodzenienazwiska.com](https://pochodzenienazwiska.com/grzegorczyk)). nazwiska-polskie.pl hosts comments from people looking for relatives under the tagline "wyszukuj osoby po nazwiskach" (search for people by surname) ([nazwiska-polskie.pl](https://nazwiska-polskie.pl/)). moikrewni.pl, once the standard surname map, now redirects to MyHeritage ([moikrewni.pl](https://www.moikrewni.pl/)).

The scholarly incumbent is about to fix its biggest weakness. IJP PAN's "Internetowy słownik nazwisk w Polsce" (ISNP) covers about **30,000 surnames borne by 75–80% of the population**, with dated attestations and hedged etymologies. Its counts, however, come from PESEL 2014, and its geography stops at the top 16 powiats and gminas ([Górny 2024, Onomastica](https://onomastica.ijppan.pl/index.php/ONOM/article/view/533/730)). A grant from the National Programme for the Development of Humanities now funds version 2.0 for 2025–2030. It will add about 20,000 mid-frequency surnames (target about 50,000, covering ~95% of Poles), PESEL 2024 frequencies and wider powiat and gmina detail ([nazwiska.ijppan.pl](https://nazwiska.ijppan.pl/); [Górny 2024 PDF](https://onomastica.ijppan.pl/index.php/ONOM/article/download/533/682)). ISNP drew **nearly one million users in 2022 and 570,000 in 2023**. That makes it both the strongest content rival and the most valuable potential partner.

Google is the competitor nobody has measured. AI Overviews reached Poland in Polish on 26 March 2025 for signed-in adults ([Spider's Web](https://spidersweb.pl/2025/03/google-przeglady-overviews-ai-w-polsce.html)), and AI Mode followed on 8 October 2025 ([chip.pl](https://www.chip.pl/2025/10/google-wprowadza-tryb-ai-w-wyszukiwarce-google-w-polsce)). In Pew's US panel, AI summaries appeared on **60% of question-form searches but only 8% of one- or two-word searches**. Users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits that showed a summary, against 15% without one ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)). Ahrefs measured click-through for the first result about 58% lower under an AI Overview ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/)), and Seer measured a 61% fall in organic click-through ([Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update)). "Skąd pochodzi nazwisko X" (where does surname X come from) is a question, so meaning-and-origin content is the most exposed part of the free tier. No Polish-language click-through study exists, and the research tools could not observe google.pl results.

The big international platforms are not building Polish surname reference. Their AI work targets records and family trees. FamilySearch's free Full-Text Search covered English, Spanish and Portuguese as of October 2025. French, German, Dutch, Italian and Chinese are planned; Polish is not mentioned ([FamilySearch](https://www.familysearch.org/en/blog/what-is-full-text-search)). MyHeritage's Scribe AI (5 March 2026) transcribes and translates documents in any language the site supports, and needs a subscription after a few free images ([MyHeritage blog](https://blog.myheritage.com/2026/03/introducing-scribe-ai/)). MyHeritage also runs a lastnames subdomain with pages for very rare Polish feminine forms, but their content could not be retrieved ([MyHeritage lastnames](https://lastnames.myheritage.com/last-name/gielarowska)). Forebears lost 17% of its visits month on month in August 2026, and two-thirds of its traffic comes from organic search ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/forebears.io/)). No AI-first surname startup turned up, though that may reflect the limits of the search rather than the market.

## Rivals miscount Polish surnames by up to 99% and invent their sources

The Polish sites get basics wrong that anyone can check against the official files. szukaj-nazwiska.pl labels its data "Dane z dnia: 20.01.2026" (data as of 20 January 2026), yet its figures equal the **2026 male file plus the 2025 female file**. Nowak shows 198,706 where the correct 2026 total is 198,125 ([szukaj-nazwiska.pl](https://szukaj-nazwiska.pl/); [2026 male file](https://api.dane.gov.pl/media/resources/20260128/2_-_nazwiska_m%C4%99skie-osoby_%C5%BCyj%C4%85ce.xlsx); [2025 female file](https://api.dane.gov.pl/media/resources/20250123/nazwiska_%C5%BCe%C5%84skie-osoby_%C5%BCyj%C4%85ce_efby1gw.xlsx)).

surname.pl folds the spelling without Polish diacritics, SZCZEPANSKI (39 men), into SZCZEPAŃSKI (16,419) and reports 16,458 under an unexplained "2 Wariantów" (2 variants). It skips 2022 in that name's trend series. It labels Czekierda, ranked 13,282nd with 429 bearers, "Bardzo popularne nazwisko" (very popular surname). It also calls every surname in the register "polskie", billing "Kbiltsetskhlashvili" as the longest Polish surname ([surname.pl/szczepanski](https://surname.pl/szczepanski); [surname.pl/czekierda](https://surname.pl/czekierda); [surname.pl](https://surname.pl/)). pochodzenienazwiska.com is worse. It cites evidently fabricated works ("Johna Smitha… 2005"; "Anna Johnson, Academic Press, 2010") and grafts an English Danelaw story onto Grzegorczyk. For Czekierda it offers filler about "czasów starożytnych i legendarnych postaci" (ancient times and legendary figures), where ISNP simply says "Niejasne" (unclear) ([Grzegorczyk](https://pochodzenienazwiska.com/grzegorczyk); [Czekierda](https://pochodzenienazwiska.com/czekierda); [ISNP text via szukaj-nazwiska.pl](https://szukaj-nazwiska.pl/Czekierda)).

International sites fail harder, and exactly where diaspora users are exposed. Forebears is the only global site that shows a Polish count. It gives Nowak **268,732** bearers, 35.6% above the official 198,125. It gives Wiśniewski 315 and Wisniewski 1,125 bearers, against **107,256** in the register (−99.7% and −99.0%). It gives Kowalska 2,978, against 67,308 (−95.6%). Its only map year is 2014, it names no source, and its copyright page prohibits copying ([Forebears: Nowak](https://forebears.io/surnames/nowak); [Wiśniewski](https://forebears.io/surnames/wi%C5%9Bniewski); [Wisniewski](https://forebears.io/surnames/wisniewski); [Kowalska](https://forebears.io/surnames/kowalska); [copyright](https://forebears.io/copyright)). These figures came through an automated fetch, so they need a human check before any public comparison.

Ancestry and FamilySearch both use Oxford's *Dictionary of American Family Names* (DAFN). Ancestry gives Kowalska no meaning, does not link it to Kowalski, and describes Nowak's "distribution" as 100 families in Illinois in 1880. FamilySearch's "distribution" counts profiles in its own Family Tree ([Ancestry: Nowak](https://www.ancestry.com/name-origin?surname=nowak); [Ancestry: Kowalska](https://www.ancestry.com/name-origin?surname=kowalska); [FamilySearch: Nowak](https://www.familysearch.org/en/surname?surname=nowak)). Two different dictionaries with the same title disagree on Wiśniewski, and neither site flags the conflict. Hanks's DAFN derives it from places called Wiśniewo, Wiśniew or Wiśniewa; Smith's 1956 dictionary derives it from Wisznia ([FamilySearch: Wiśniewska](https://www.familysearch.org/en/surname?surname=wi%C5%9Bniewska); [Forebears: Wisniewski](https://forebears.io/surnames/wisniewski)).

House of Names derives Nowak from Czech "novy" and sells crest downloads with no warning about who is entitled to use them. SurnameDB's earliest record for Nowak is a 1655 Czech christening ([House of Names](https://www.houseofnames.com/nowak-family-crest); [SurnameDB](https://www.surnamedb.com/Surname/Nowak)). Even the standard English reference, Hoffman's *Polish Surnames* (PGSA, 2012), carries counts from 2002 ([PGSA](https://pgsa.org/product/polish-surnames-origins-and-meanings-3rd-ed-2-volume-book-set/)).

Users say plainly what is missing. Since October 2022, ISNP's authors report, hundreds of emails have asked them to add a missing (often rare) surname. Writers ask when and where their surname first appeared, and which parts of Poland, down to gmina level, it is common in. They ask for the 2014 counts to be updated ("Mają Państwo chyba bardzo nieaktualne dane", your data seems very out of date), for a list of the rarest surnames, and even to order a family tree. Some argue that their name is noble ([Górny 2024](https://onomastica.ijppan.pl/index.php/ONOM/article/view/533/730)). Only about 14% of ISNP users return. In 2022 some 67% were in Poland, with most of the rest in the UK, US, Germany, France and Canada. Diaspora forums trace Americanised spellings back to Polish one case at a time, for instance "Zintak" to "Ziętak" in a thread seen only as a search snippet (UNVERIFIED) ([PolishOrigins forum](https://forum.polishorigins.com/viewtopic.php?p=76124)). The diaspora audience is large: about **8.6 million** people in the US report Polish ancestry alone or in combination, per a secondary source ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Americans)).

The gap is the combination. No product brings together official counts that are internally consistent and labelled with their snapshot, gender pairing and variant handling that are shown rather than hidden, per-claim citations with confidence labels and an honest "we don't know", attestations for surnames beyond ISNP's 30,000, declension for both genders, and a versioned paid report without heraldry. Three more gaps sit underneath. First, since moikrewni.pl went dark, open data has produced no current map below voivodeship level: ISNP's top-16 lists date from 2014, and nazwiska-polskie.pl's locality figures cite no source.

Second, no one explains what a PESEL count measures. The files list living people by *current* surname, so most married women appear under their married name. They omit surnames borne by only one person. The register also covers foreigners living in Poland (Art. 7). Their registration most likely drives much of the rise from 277,108 distinct male surnames in 2019 to 408,182 in 2026, though the files carry no citizenship field to prove it ([dataset 1681 description](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/1681); [Population Records Act](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2026/384/text.pdf); [2019 male file](https://api.dane.gov.pl/resources/18990,nazwiska-meskie-stan-na-2019-06-30/file); [2026 male file](https://api.dane.gov.pl/resources/1148808,nazwiska-meskie-stan-na-2026-01-20/file)). The 2026 files add up to about 42.76 million entries. The research judged, without checking GUS figures (UNVERIFIED), that this exceeds Poland's resident population, so rivals' "osób w Polsce" (people in Poland) wording is likely misleading.

Third, diaspora users lack a resolver from Americanised spellings back to Polish candidates, and guidance by partition, archive and record language. FamilySearch documents that civil registers in Russian Poland were kept in Russian from April 1868 until 1918, but no surname page ties that fact to where a name is concentrated ([FamilySearch Wiki](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Russian_Poland_Civil_Registration)).

Finally, searches for "herb nazwiska" (surname coat of arms) are served by armorial databases and House of Names-style crests ([gajl.wielcy.pl](https://gajl.wielcy.pl/); [szlachtarp.pl](https://szlachtarp.pl/lista-nazwisk-indeks11-UruskiRodzina.html); [MyHeritage wiki](https://www.myheritage.com/wiki/Polish_nobility_surnames)). A sourced explanation of why a surname does not come with arms is open ground. The link between Polish heraldic clans and surnames needs its own evidenced explainer, which this research did not cover.

## Four moats hold; the snapshot archive and crowd data do not

Management research doubts that data alone makes a moat. Hagiu and Wright argue that learning from data resembles a network effect but is "not as powerful or as enduring", and that most firms "grossly overestimate" it ([BU Questrom summary](https://insights.bu.edu/when-data-creates-competitive-advantageand-when-it-doesnt/?amp=1)). Casado and Lauten of a16z add that the cost of adding unique data rises while the value of each addition falls ([a16z](https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/)).

Genealogy bears this out. The products that lasted hold data that grows through labour or contribution. Geneteka has **68,062,579** volunteer-indexed records ([Geneteka](https://geneteka.genealodzy.pl/index.php?op=se&lang=pol)), and JRI-Poland signed an indexing agreement with the Polish State Archives in 2013 ([JRI-Poland guide](https://jri-poland.org/help/Guide_to_Images_Linked_by_JRI.pdf)). Products built on a single snapshot decayed. Germany's Geogen still runs on 2002 phone books ([GIGA](https://www.giga.de/tipp/wie-oft-gibt-es-meinen-namen-in-deutschland-diese-karte-weiss-es/)). moikrewni.pl redirects to MyHeritage. Géopatronyme redirects to Filae, which MyHeritage bought in 2021 for its more than one billion records ([Geneamusings](https://www.geneamusings.com/2021/08/myheritage-to-acquire-filae-french.html)). Buyers paid for owned records, not for republished statistics.

| Relationships with institutions that hold data | **Real moat** | Access is granted by agreement, so the first to arrive sets terms, although any single table can end up as open data |
| Trust and citation brand | **Real moat, built slowly** | Grows with each annual edition, backlink and AI citation |
| Paid report offered only when evidence exists | **Real moat, conditional** | No Polish rival sells a surname-level report; the format can be copied, the contents cannot |
| Crowd-contributed "my family came from X" data | Not a moat, and a liability | Weak network effect; risk that it counts as sensitive data by inference; likely needs a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) |

### IJP PAN's copyrighted scholarship anchors the evidence moat

The first and strongest moat is a curated evidence graph that rivals cannot or will not build. ISNP took from 2014 to 2021 to reach about 30,000 entries. Each entry cites dated attestations by source, volume and page. When nothing is found, the entry says "brak poświadczeń w dostępnych nam źródłach historycznych" (no attestations in the historical sources available to us) ([ISNP O słowniku](https://nazwiska.ijppan.pl/info/oslowniku)). surname.pl chose an LLM instead. The scholarship underneath stays in copyright for decades. Rymut's ten-volume register, with 405,670 surnames and 1990 counts across 49 voivodeships, is protected until the end of 2076. Taszycki's SSNO, a dictionary of Old Polish personal names recorded before 1500, is protected until 2049. SEMSNO, a dictionary of the origins of Old Polish names, is protected longer still, because its co-editor Cieślikowa died in 2018 ([Skowronek et al. 2018](https://onomastica.ijp.pan.pl/index.php/ONOM/article/download/51/33); [Wikidata Query Service](https://query.wikidata.org/)). IJP PAN publishes ISNP, SEMSNO and the Rymut register, which makes it the natural single licensing partner; Ossolineum published SSNO. ISNP's method also fits Name Atlas's confidence labels directly. It gives "possible and probable" etymologies, often several. Bieńkowska, for example, may come from the place Bieńki, the personal name Bieniek or the masculine Bieńkowski. Name Atlas should show such cases as CONFLICTING or POSSIBLE rather than collapse them into one story ([Skowronek et al. 2018](https://onomastica.ijp.pan.pl/index.php/ONOM/article/download/51/33)).

Timing matters. ISNP publishes no content licence, and szukaj-nazwiska.pl already embeds its text on every page with unknown permission ([szukaj-nazwiska.pl](https://szukaj-nazwiska.pl/Czekierda)). The first formal licensee is likely to shape the terms for everyone else. That is an inference rather than a finding.

### Granted access beats downloaded data, though single tables leak

The second moat is access that has to be granted rather than downloaded. IJP PAN received its PESEL 2014 extract, with powiat and gmina detail, free from the Interior Ministry's population records department ([ISNP O słowniku](https://nazwiska.ijppan.pl/info/oslowniku)). The Population Records Act now allows PESEL data to be provided to "jednostkom organizacyjnym" (organisational units) for research and statistical purposes. The condition is that the data are modified after use so that no one can be identified. Re-use then follows on request under the 2021 Open Data Act ([Population Records Act, Art. 46](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2026/384/text.pdf)).

PTG's Geneteka, the Wielkopolska, Lublin and Pomeranian volunteer indexes, the State Archives (following the JRI-Poland model), and CompGen and Die Maus in Germany could all supply negotiated feeds of counts per surname, parish and decade. No scraper can lawfully copy those. The caveat is that single tables do not stay exclusive. The Open Data Act directs public bodies to publish reusable information as open data where they can ([Open Data Act](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2023/1524/text.html)), so a powiat table obtained on request may soon be public. The lasting edge is the relationship, the speed and the processing, not ownership of any one file.

### An annual "what changed" edition builds trust over time

The third moat is a reputation for being right, and it builds slowly. Coverage of each PESEL release is reliable but shallow, with the Ministry and the outlets that follow it repeating top-10 lists. The only year-on-year story found, Forsal's 2023 "Melnyk goni Kaczyńskiego" (Melnyk catches up with Kaczyński), needed two archived snapshots ([Forsal](https://forsal.pl/gospodarka/demografia/artykuly/8650868,najpopularniejsze-nazwiska-w-polsce-melnyk-goni-kaczynskiego.html); [gov.pl](https://www.gov.pl/web/cyfryzacja/nowak-znowu-pierwszy)).

Media attention turns into visits. ISNP's traffic peaks coincided with press coverage: about 86,000 users on 13 November 2022, and about 60,000 on 27 December 2022, the day Interia ran "Skąd się wzięło twoje nazwisko?" (where does your surname come from?) ([Górny 2024](https://onomastica.ijppan.pl/index.php/ONOM/article/view/533/730); [Interia](https://kobieta.interia.pl/porady/news-skad-sie-wzielo-twoje-nazwisko-latwo-to-sprawdzisz,nId,6328683)).

A yearly "what changed" edition, released after the data lands in late January and tying every figure to its file, would earn the backlinks and citations that count in AI answers. Only **37.9% of URLs cited in AI Overviews** now rank in Google's top ten ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/)). In Seer's data, brands cited in an AI Overview got 0.70% organic click-through against 0.52% for others, though that is a correlation, not proof of cause ([Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update)).

The edition must avoid Forsal's "eastern-origin" framing. The files carry no citizenship field, so Name Atlas can report changes in counts of surname forms but must mark their causes INSUFFICIENT_DATA.

### A paid report would fill the empty band between $50 and $900

The fourth moat depends on the other three, because a paid report is only as defensible as the evidence inside it. The format has a proven model in autoDNA, the Polish-founded vehicle-history seller. Its free check shows "the range of information displayed in the paid autoDNA report" ([autoDNA](https://www.autodna.com/vin-check)). A search snippet from its homepage adds that buyers can "preview the types and quantity of available information" before paying about €24.99 for a single report (snippet only, UNVERIFIED) ([autoDNA](https://www.autodna.com/)). That is the same logic as Name Atlas's truthful pre-purchase inventory. No Polish surname site sells anything at surname level; they rely on AdSense or MyHeritage affiliate links.

Observed prices leave an empty middle. At the bottom, House of Names sells surname-history PDFs at $9.03–$12.05 and crest-and-history PDFs at $14.80–$19.75, marketed as gifts ([House of Names](https://www.houseofnames.com/)). Hoffman's two-volume *Polish Surnames* costs $30 as a download or $50 in print ([PGSA](https://pgsa.org/?p=140366)). Membership costs $50 a year for Gesher Galicia and $100 a year for JewishGen's extra services ([Gesher Galicia](https://www.geshergalicia.org); [JewishGen](https://jewishgen.org/JewishGen/ValueAdded.asp)).

Then prices jump. A diocesan archive charges 60 zł per hour of searching ([Archiwum Diecezjalne](https://archiwum.diecezjazg.pl/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Informacja-dla-kwerendzistow-12.09.2023.pdf)). PolishOrigins charges a **$900 minimum**, though its FAQ gives 2,500 zł instead (CONFLICTING) ([PolishOrigins](https://polishorigins.com/genealogy/); [PolishOrigins FAQ](https://polishorigins.com/?p=2613)). Legacy Tree charges $108 an hour, in blocks of 25–100 hours ([Legacy Tree](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-hire-a-genealogist)).

No Polish willingness-to-pay data exists. Free-to-paid consumer products typically convert 2–5% of users, per a secondary source ([Crazy Egg](https://www.crazyegg.com/blog/free-to-paid-conversion-rate/)). ISNP's 14% return rate favours a one-off purchase over a subscription. The gift framing that rivals rely on works without heraldry, because the evidence file itself is the gift.

### Archives, exclusive tables and crowd data only look like moats

Three assets look like moats but are not.

The first is the snapshot archive. The moat idea assumed that each year's file becomes a time series only if someone keeps it. In fact dane.gov.pl keeps them all and surname.pl already charts them. A local archive is insurance against future deletion, not an advantage ([resource list](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/1681/resources)).

The second is exclusivity over new Ministry tables, which the Open Data Act pushes towards publication.

The third is crowd-contributed data such as "my family came from region X". Its network effect fails Hagiu and Wright's test: extra reports add little for common surnames and are too scarce for rare ones. It is also a legal hazard. In ruling C-184/20, the EU Court of Justice held that data which indirectly reveal special categories, through cross-referencing, get the extra protection of Article 9 ([Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/eu-data-protection/special-category-data-by-inference-cjeu-significantly-expands-the-scope-of-article-9-gdpr/)). The Polish data protection authority's list requires a DPIA whenever two or more of its risk criteria apply ([M.P. 2019 poz. 666](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/MP/2019/666/text/O/M20190666.pdf)). The EDPB's draft Guidelines 02/2026 on Anonymisation, open for comment until 30 October 2026, test aggregate data for three risks: singling out a record, linking records and inferring facts ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/public-consultations/guidelines-022026-on-anonymisation_en); [Covington](https://www.covingtonblogs.com/2026/08/04/edpb-publishes-draft-guidelines-on-anonymisation/)). Aggregates for rare surnames, combined with PESEL counts, could fail that test.

A surname-variant dictionary sold to businesses is a plausible by-product. Identity checks and customer databases need Polish name matching: SAS ships a Polish name-parsing locale ([SAS QKB](https://support.sas.com/documentation/onlinedoc/qkb/33/QKBCI33/Help/Content/qkbci-plpol/qkbci-plpol-pars-name-multiname.html)), and a sanctions-screening vendor calls name variation "the real hard nut to crack" ([sanctions.io](https://www.sanctions.io/blog/the-problem-of-name-matching-in-sanctions-screening)). But no buyer was found. The nearest market with real revenue is inferring nationality or ethnicity from names (Namsor; Forebears' OnoGraph at $1–3 per 1,000 queries), which is exactly what Name Atlas forbids ([GenderPortal](https://genderportal.eu/node/1545); [OnoGraph](https://forebears.io/onograph)).

## Open licences cover grammar, places and diaspora counts, but not etymology

Open data that allows commercial use now covers almost everything on a surname page except the question users ask first. The research found lawful sources for current and historical counts, grammar, pronunciation, candidate places and diaspora distribution. It found no open resource that states where a surname comes from. The grammatical dictionaries record no derivational links between names, the public-domain dictionaries explain words rather than names, and Wikidata's 32,590 Polish-labelled family-name items carry only 30 transliterations ([Wikidata Query Service](https://query.wikidata.org/)). Outside licensed IJP PAN works, openly licensed journal articles and ISNP-style citations, the right answer on etymology is INSUFFICIENT_DATA.

### PESEL holds birth surnames that no published file shows

The Ministry of Digital Affairs publishes more than the living-persons snapshot. Dataset 568 covers surnames **including deceased persons**. It has national files from 2017 to 2025, a 2017 voivodeship file carrying TERYT codes and, oddly, only a female file for 2026 ([dataset 568](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/568)). Dataset 1082 counts permanent registrations per gmina. It can serve as the population figure for surname density, provided the product notes that registration is not the same as residence ([dataset 1082](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/1082)).

All are CC0 1.0 at dataset level, and all need cleaning. Column headers drift from "nazwisko, liczba" to "Nazwisko aktualne, Liczba" and, in one file, to a misspelt "Nawisko aktualne". Older CSVs use spaces as thousands separators. The 2026 voivodeship file drops the TERYT column. Every file omits surnames borne by only one person ([2026 voivodeship file](https://api.dane.gov.pl/resources/1148786,nazwiska-meskie-wg-wojewodztwa-zameldowania-na-pobyt-stay-stan-na-2026-01-20/file)). Two of the files that include deceased persons contradict the living series. The 2017 file lists 92,867 male Nowaks, against 101,473 living ones in 2019. The April 2019 file lists fewer distinct surnames (270,006) than the living file of June 2019 (277,108). Both files should be treated as not comparable until the Ministry explains them ([dataset 568](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/568); [2019 male file](https://api.dane.gov.pl/resources/18990,nazwiska-meskie-stan-na-2019-06-30/file)).

The bigger prize is data the register holds but does not publish. PESEL stores each person's **birth surname** (nazwisko rodowe) and the parents' birth surnames (Art. 8), while every published file uses the current surname ([Population Records Act](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2026/384/text.pdf)). Article 46 allows PESEL data to go to organisations for research and statistics, on condition that they are de-identified after use. Re-use is requested under the Open Data Act. That Act requires an answer within 14 days, or two months at most. Access is free by default, but cost-based fees are allowed, including for anonymisation. Public bodies are excused from "disproportionate" work ([Population Records Act](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2026/384/text.pdf); [Open Data Act](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2023/1524/text.html)). The most promising route to finer geography is a request for surname tables by powiat or gmina, by birth surname and by birth decade, with a stated minimum cell size. Journalists have already obtained gmina-level top-surname counts from local offices, though one gmina's system could not produce them ([Beskidzka24](https://beskidzka24.pl/jakie-sa-najpopularniejsze-nazwiska-w-powiecie-cieszynskim/)). Whether a company qualifies under Article 46 **needs counsel**.

### SGJP, PRNG and open tools cover grammar, sound and places

The language resources are unusually well licensed. The SGJP inflection data distributed with Morfeusz 2 is BSD-2-Clause. Its August 2026 release holds **95,401 surname entries (60,962 distinct base forms)** with fully tagged declension tables ([Morfeusz 2 licence](https://morfeusz.sgjp.pl/doc/license/); [SGJP dump](http://download.sgjp.pl/morfeusz/20260823/sgjp-20260823.tab.gz)). The tables include Nowakowie and Kowalscy in the plural, a feminine Nowak that does not decline, both Gołąba and Gołębia, and the adjectival Lindego. Three limits apply. Only the inflection data is BSD-licensed, not the rest of the sgjp.pl website. The release includes pejorative (deprecative) plurals such as "Nowaki" and "Kowalskie", which must be filtered out. And coverage is thin at the long tail: the 60,962 base forms are only about 15% of the more than 400,000 surname forms in use, even though they match 91.1% of Wikidata's Polish surname labels once traditional -owa/-ówna forms are set aside. Forms for rarer names must therefore be generated by rule and labelled with lower confidence. PWN's usage advice can be cited as normative claims: "Nowakowie" covers the whole family, and bearers generally prefer the unaltered "Gołąba" ([PWN Poradnia](https://sjp.pwn.pl/poradnia/haslo/Do-kogo-odnosi-sie-forma-Nowakowie;20691.html); [PWN](https://sjp.pwn.pl/poradnia/haslo/odmiana-nazwisk;3099.html)). sjp.pl's word list, under CC BY 4.0 or Apache 2.0 at the user's choice, serves as an untagged cross-check ([sjp.pl](https://sjp.pl/sl/odmiany/)).

Pronunciation and place evidence are open too. The CLARIN-PL Polish grapheme-to-phoneme tool (BSD-2) generates IPA ([CLARIN-PL](http://hdl.handle.net/11321/295)). The state gazetteer PRNG's September 2026 export lists 124,247 localities, **44,665 of them with an official adjective form** (Abramów → "abramowski"). That gives a lawful bridge from a -ski surname to candidate source places. Confidence should fall as the number of places sharing a name rises ([PRNG export](https://opendata.geoportal.gov.pl/prng/PRNG_MIEJSCOWOSCI_XLSX.zip); [dataset 780](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/780)).

The public-domain dictionaries support "the word X meant Y", not "the surname comes from X". They are Brückner's 1927 etymological dictionary (public domain in Poland since 2010), Linde, Karłowicz and the Słownik warszawski, the last pending a check of its contributors. Brückner is dated enough that its etymologies warrant LIKELY or POSSIBLE rather than VERIFIED ([Wikisource](https://pl.wikisource.org/wiki/S%C5%82ownik_etymologiczny_j%C4%99zyka_polskiego); [AMU thesis](https://repozytorium.amu.edu.pl/handle/10593/27024)).

Recent Onomastica articles are CC BY-ND 4.0, which allows commercial redistribution with citation but no adapted versions. The safe pattern is to store the Polish quotation as the EvidenceItem and show an English paraphrase as the Claim ([Górny 2024 PDF](https://onomastica.ijppan.pl/index.php/ONOM/article/download/533/682)). Rozprawy Komisji Językowej ŁTN has been CC BY 4.0 since 2024 ([Rozprawy](https://czasopisma.ltn.lodz.pl/Rozprawy-Komisji-Jezykowej/pl)). Cyrillic spellings matter for the Russian-language registers kept after 1868. They must be generated by rule and labelled POSSIBLE, because the standard guides are copyrighted ([PGSA *In Their Words*](https://pgsa.org/?p=1301)).

### Diaspora counts are clean where they exist at all

Official diaspora counts exist in fewer countries than expected, but the ones that exist are clean. The US Census Bureau released its 2020 last-name table on 14 April 2026. It covers 156,621 surnames with at least 100 bearers, with statistical noise added (±2 at 95%) and diacritics stripped. So WIŚNIEWSKI appears as WISNIEWSKI (11,873 bearers), beside NOWAK (18,297) and KOWALSKI (16,721) ([Census 2020 names](https://www.census.gov/topics/population/genealogy/data/2020_names.html); [technical brief](https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/decennial/2020/c2020br-14.pdf); [data file](https://www2.census.gov/topics/genealogy/2020surnames/Names2020_LastNames_RaceHispanic.xlsx)). With the 2010 (CC0) and 2000 files, it gives US counts from three censuses. Ingestion must drop the race and Hispanic-origin columns so that no ethnicity signal reaches a Claim ([data.gov 2010](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/2010-decennial-census-of-population-and-housing-surnames); [Census 2000](https://www.census.gov/topics/population/genealogy/data/2000_surnames.html)).

France's INSEE offers two sources under Licence Ouverte 2.0, which allows commercial reuse with the credit "Source : Insee". The Fichier des noms counts surnames of people born in France from 1891 to 2000, by decade and département, for names given at least 30 times. A monthly deaths file records the birth country of people born abroad ([INSEE Fichier des noms](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/3536630); [data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/fr/datasets/fichier-des-personnes-decedees/); [INSEE reuse terms](https://www.insee.fr/fr/information/2008466)).

The most valuable new historical source is the Verein für Computergenealogie's First World War casualty-list dataset. It was released on Zenodo on 21 January 2026 under CC0, with commercial use explicitly allowed. It holds **8.5 million entries with birthplace and district, 1914–1919**, across all German contingents. That includes men born in the Prussian-ruled Polish lands (Posen, West Prussia, Silesia) and in the Ruhr ([Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18330751); [documentation](https://zenodo.org/api/records/18330751/files/Verlustlisten%20Erster%20Weltkrieg.pdf/content)).

Argentina's RENAPER (by province), Finland's DVV and Norway's SSB, all CC BY 4.0, add smaller current counts ([datos.gob.ar](https://datos.gob.ar/api/3/action/package_search?q=apellidos); [avoindata.fi](https://avoindata.suomi.fi/data/dataset/57282ad6-3ab1-48fb-983a-8aba5ff8d29a); [SSB](https://www.ssb.no/375410/etternavn-brukt-av-200-eller-flere)). Chronicling America's public-domain Polish-language US newspapers give dated, citable mentions rather than counts: 18,255 results, 9,322 of them from Dziennik Chicagoski ([loc.gov](https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/?fa=language:polish&fo=json&c=1&at=facets); [rights](https://www.loc.gov/item/sn83045747/?fo=json)).

Several of the largest recent Polish communities have no official counts at all. The UK's ONS publishes no surname statistics ([ONS FOI](https://www.ons.gov.uk/aboutus/transparencyandgovernance/freedomofinformationfoi/frequencyanddistributionofthesurnameastorintheunitedkingdom)). The Czech interior ministry stopped after its 2016 data ([CNN Prima](https://cnn.iprima.cz/urady-sedm-let-taji-udaje-o-poctu-jmen-i-prijmeni-nevime-kolik-je-krystofu-fialu-ani-gottu-412444)). German surname scholarship rests on a 2005 Deutsche Telekom extract that is not open ([idw](https://idw-online.de/de/news809731)). For those countries, "we don't know" is the honest answer.

Two more Polish sources are lawful in principle but need work. The first is printed historical lists. Official documents fall outside copyright altogether (Copyright Act Art. 4 pkt 2). Works whose rights sit with a publisher, or whose author is unknown, stay protected for 70 years from publication (Art. 36) ([Copyright Act](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2025/24/text.pdf)). So the Lista strat Wojska Polskiego 1918–1920 (the Polish Army casualty list) in the Jagiellonian Digital Library, and the address books and name lists in the Wielkopolska Digital Library, can yield dated surname-by-place evidence ([JBC](https://jbc.bj.uj.edu.pl/dlibra/publication/263227/edition/251272?language=pl); [WBC](https://wbc.poznan.pl/dlibra/collectiondescription/185?language=pl)). Counts must be built from the originals rather than from modern commercial indexes, and each list's social bias recorded in the ConfidenceAssessment.

The second is the State Archives' portal, Szukaj w Archiwach. It holds more than 81 million digital copies, and a 2025–2029 EU-funded project (87.9 million zł) will add 7.1 million and build an API ([AAN](https://aan.gov.pl/wp/2026/04/08/aan-beneficjentem-funduszy-europejskich-na-rozwoj-cyfrowy/)). Transkribus's "Polski Wieszcz" model, built with JRI-Poland and the State Archives, reads 17th–19th-century parish registers at a 7.37% character error rate ([Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/models/polski-wieszcz)). Surname counts per parish and decade from 19th-century registers look lawful on copyright and data-protection grounds. But the portal blocks automated access, so those counts need the future API or an agreement.

An open licence is not ethical clearance. The Latvian 1949 deportation list is CC0 but belongs in the same risk class as Arolsen, straty.pl or the Holocaust databases. A surname-level signal saying "appears in victim database X" acts as a stand-in for ethnicity, religion or fate, which the product's standing rules forbid ([data.gov.lv](https://data.gov.lv/dati/lv/dataset?q=uzv%C4%81rdi); [Arolsen terms](https://arolsen-archives.org/aroa/documents/terms-of-service_en_2022-11-24.pdf)). surname.pl already shows exactly this kind of signal: counts from a list of Soviet repression victims on ordinary surname pages ([surname.pl/szczepanski](https://surname.pl/szczepanski)). Israeli surname counts are excluded for the same reason, since for Polish-origin names they would imply Jewish ancestry ([secondary source](https://bloodandfrogs.com/2018/05/101-most-common-surnames-israel.html)). Any feature showing Yiddish or Hebrew spellings should go to `docs/DECISIONS_NEEDED.md` for a decision, not straight into code.

### Open historical evidence favours the formerly Prussian west

The lawful historical evidence is lopsided. Without agreements, the only historical distribution inside Poland that is ready to use comes from the CC0 German casualty lists, which cover the formerly Prussian-ruled west and north. Congress Poland needs Geneteka or the archives, and Galicia needs the Austro-Hungarian index, which has no licence. Even Geneteka is uneven: it holds 18,716 birth records for Dolnośląskie against 6,338,638 for Mazowieckie ([Geneteka](https://geneteka.genealodzy.pl/index.php?op=se&lang=pol); [CompGen](https://compgen.de/tag/oesterreich-ungarn)). Printed lists can narrow the gap, but only after OCR work and a legal check.

## Ten legal questions must be settled before a paid launch

None of the items below is resolved, and each touches a paid surface. They are research readings, not legal advice.

| Open question | Why it matters | What the research found |
|---|---|---|
| Can Piper's Polish voices be used commercially? | Pronunciation audio | Voice data is CC0 or CC BY, but the darkman, gosia and mc_speech models are fine-tuned from the US "lessac" voice, whose Blizzard 2013 licence bars commercial speech synthesis ([licence](https://www.cstr.ed.ac.uk/projects/blizzard/2013/lessac_blizzard2013/license.html); [model card](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/main/pl/pl_PL/darkman/medium/MODEL_CARD)). **High risk.** The espeak-ng speech engine is GPL-3; whether its output carries obligations needs confirming |
| Can ISNP content be reused? | Core etymology evidence | ISNP pages show no licence; szukaj-nazwiska.pl embeds the text anyway; a 15-year database right may still run for a work published online 2014–2021 |
| Does a company qualify under Art. 46 ust. 2 pkt 2? | Tables by powiat, gmina and birth surname | The statute says "jednostkom organizacyjnym … w celach badawczych, statystycznych" (organisational units, for research and statistics); "market research" wording appears only on an older ministry page ([MSWiA archive](https://archiwum.mswia.gov.pl/pl/sprawy-obywatelskie/udostepnianie/12993,Udostepnianie-danych-z-rejestru-PESEL.html)) |
| Can Rymut's 1990 counts be reused? | A historical count for 405,670 surnames | On the statute's wording, the database right would have lapsed around 2009 ([Database Act](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2024/1769/text.pdf)); copyright in the selection and arrangement, and Rymut's copyright to 2076, are unresolved |
| What exactly do the State Archives' terms allow? | Counts per parish and decade | The exact terms could not be retrieved past the portal's bot protection ([SWA](https://www.szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl/ponowne-wykorzystanie)); a constantly updated description database may keep restarting its 15-year term; rules on automated harvesting are unknown |
| Is translating a CC BY-ND quotation an adaptation? | English pages for diaspora users | Arguably yes, so paraphrase instead |
| Which licence statement applies? | PRNG, PESEL files, US 2020 file, IBGE | PRNG is CC BY 4.0 on dane.gov.pl but "any use" on the geoportal, so apply the stricter one; individual dane.gov.pl files carry no licence field, only the dataset-level CC0; the US 2020 page has no statement; IBGE unconfirmed |
| How should data about the dead be handled? | Counts including deceased persons, INSEE deaths file | GDPR excludes the dead (Recital 27), but relatives' personal rights in Poland and heirs' objections in France (Art. 85) still apply |
| Can crowd contributions ever be anonymised? | Any "my family came from X" feature | May count as sensitive data by inference (C-184/20); a DPIA is likely; the final text of EDPB Guidelines 02/2026 is pending |
| Are specific historical publications free to use? | Lista strat, address books, Słownik warszawski | Each title needs checking under Art. 4 pkt 2 and Art. 36; co-authored works are protected until 70 years after the last surviving co-author dies; digital-library terms (Polona, JBC, WBC) unverified |

Several factual gaps also limit confidence in this analysis. The research tools were US-based, so no google.pl ranking, AI Overview or AI Mode answer for a Polish surname query was observed. A manual test from a Polish IP address (for example "pochodzenie nazwiska Czekierda", origin of the surname Czekierda) is the cheapest next step. No Polish keyword volumes, Polish click-through studies or willingness-to-pay surveys were found, so demand evidence rests on ISNP's self-reported traffic and on price anchors from other markets. Competitor pages were read through automated tools that fetch and summarise pages. Headline numbers, especially Forebears' Wiśniewski and Kowalska figures, need a check in a browser before any public comparison. surname.pl's traffic and plans are unknown, though its "o nas" (about us) page promises no paywall. Finally, the exact scope of the PESEL register, and the anomalies in the files that include deceased persons, should be confirmed in writing with the Ministry of Digital Affairs before Name Atlas uses any "people in Poland" wording.

## Conclusion

The research turns a natural assumption on its head: Name Atlas's strictest rules are its commercial moat. No LLM etymologies, rights-first sourcing, confidence labels and "we don't know" look like handicaps against fast free sites. In fact they describe the one layer the market leaves unserved. Every independent or commercial rival fills its meaning layer with generated, borrowed or dictionary text: Claude at surname.pl, ISNP excerpts at szukaj-nazwiska.pl, Oxford's DAFN at Ancestry and FamilySearch. That text is either being absorbed by AI answers or visibly wrong. The real question is therefore less "what can we show that others cannot?" than "what can we prove that others cannot?", and the answer depends more on licences than on code. That makes the order of work decisive. The IJP PAN conversation comes first, because ISNP v2.0 will refresh its counts and grow from about 30,000 to about 50,000 surnames by 2030, and the first formal licensee is likely to set the terms. The Article 46 request comes second, because finer geography is what users most visibly ask for. The open datasets can be ingested at any time.

The data map also changes what the free tier can honestly promise. Lawful historical evidence is strongest where Polish emigrants and the Prussian partition left open records. It is weakest for Congress Poland and Galicia until archive and volunteer-index agreements exist. So for many surnames the honest historical dimension at launch will be INSUFFICIENT_DATA. That is not a weakness to hide. It is visible proof that the pre-purchase inventory tells the truth, and it names the partnerships to pursue. The Galician casualty index, Geneteka totals and the archives' API would each turn a visible "we don't know" into evidence Name Atlas can sell.
