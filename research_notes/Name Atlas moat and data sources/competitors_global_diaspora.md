## 1. Forebears (forebears.io): what it shows for Polish surnames, where its Polish counts come from, methodology caveats, OnoGraph API pricing/terms, traffic

### Takeaway
Forebears is the only global site that puts a per-country Polish count and rank in front of diaspora users. It does not say where its Polish numbers come from, the only date on its maps is 2014, and the numbers do not match the official PESEL register. Nowak is overstated by about 36%. Wiśniewski, the #3 surname in Poland, is almost missing: 1,125 and 315 bearers across its two spelling entries against 107,256 in PESEL. Feminine forms (-ska) and diacritic spellings are counted as separate names. Meanings come from 1956/1965 dictionaries and user submissions, not Polish scholarship. Around two-thirds of its traffic comes from organic search, it is mostly Anglophone, and it was falling in August 2026.

### Cited Findings

Map years offered are **2014 and 1880**. There is **no voivodeship/regional breakdown** within Poland. Alternate forms "Nowák (6)" and "Nöwak (1)" are listed. The page links to the OnoGraph API. — [Forebears: Nowak](https://forebears.io/surnames/nowak)

### Inferences
- Forebears' Polish counts are best treated as undisclosed, probably ~2014-vintage estimates, not register data. Three things point that way: the only map year is 2014, the counts are inflated for invariant names (Nowak), and feminine forms seem to be mostly folded into the masculine entry (Kowalski exceeds official Kowalski+Kowalska, while Kowalska is tiny). The Wiśniewski result looks like a source or encoding defect, not modelling noise.
- For a diaspora user the failure is at its worst exactly where Name Atlas is strong. The Americanised spelling ("Wisniewski") returns a Polish rank of 5,214 for a name that is #3 in Poland. Current official counts that are aware of gender and diacritics (PESEL CC0) are a differentiator that can be shown, and a side-by-side "official register versus third-party estimate" is legitimate if every figure is cited.
- OnoGraph's main job, inferring nationality or ethnicity from a name, is what Name Atlas's invariants rule out. Its licence wording ("within your organization") suggests public redistribution is not covered. With the copyright page prohibiting copying, Forebears data is not a lawful input for a public product without a separate agreement. (The full API terms were not read, so that last point is UNVERIFIED.)
- Traffic is two-thirds organic search and fell 17% in a month. That fits exposure to AI answers and search-engine changes, but I found nothing that establishes the cause.

- Whether Forebears serves ads on surname pages, and how much revenue the API and ads bring in, is UNVERIFIED.
- The exact Similarweb visit period (monthly versus three-month) is ambiguous in what was captured.
- The Wiśniewski anomaly should be re-checked by a human in a browser (see the method note).

## 2. Ancestry "last name meaning" pages and FamilySearch surname pages: content source, Polish treatment, quality

### Takeaway
Both use Oxford University Press's *Dictionary of American Family Names* (DAFN): Ancestry the 2nd edition (2022), FamilySearch the 1st edition (© Patrick Hanks 2003, 2006). The *Oxford Dictionary of Family Names in Britain and Ireland* was not seen on any Polish example. The DAFN text is short and generic, gives no Polish attestations, and tells you nothing about the name in Poland today. Distribution shown is US 1880–1920 census data (Ancestry) or counts of profiles in users' trees (FamilySearch), not population counts. Ancestry does not recognise feminine forms; FamilySearch handled one diacritic feminine form correctly. Both pages exist mainly to send users into records, sign-up or a trial.

### Cited Findings
- Ancestry, Nowak: "Nickname for a newcomer to a place or someone new to a profession or occupation from a derivative of Polish and Sorbian *nowy* 'new'", plus a note that it is also a "Germanized or Americanized form" of Czech Novák and related names. "Extremely common in Poland and also very frequent in Germany." Source: **Dictionary of American Family Names 2nd edition, 2022**. Distribution: "In 1880 there were 100 Nowak families living in Illinois" (23% of US Nowaks); found in the US, UK and Canada 1880–1920. Record counts: 265,000 total (115K birth/marriage/death, 49K census, 24K immigration, 21K military, 54K member trees). The call to action is a free **14-day trial**. — [Ancestry: Nowak](https://www.ancestry.com/name-origin?surname=nowak)
- Ancestry, **Kowalska** (the feminine of Kowalski, 67,308 living bearers in Poland per PESEL 2026): **no meaning is given**, only boilerplate ("Historically, surnames evolved as a way to sort people into groups…"). The page does not say it is the feminine form of Kowalski, although it lists Kowalski as "similar". It shows 1880 data ("2 Kowalska families" in Ohio) and record counts (9K birth/marriage/death, 3K immigration, 1K census, 37 military, 2K trees). — [Ancestry: Kowalska](https://www.ancestry.com/name-origin?surname=kowalska)
- FamilySearch, Nowak: the same DAFN meaning text plus Germanized/Americanized forms of Czech, Slovak, Slovenian, Croatian, Serbian, Ukrainian, Rusyn and Jewish variants. Source: "*Dictionary of American Family Names* © Patrick Hanks 2003, 2006". "Distribution" is **people in the FamilySearch Family Tree**: US 6,924; Poland 6,641; Germany 5,928. Variants: Novak, Noack, Noak, Nowack, Nowaczyk, Nowik, Nowka. 1,792,351 historical records. The page pushes sign-up. — [FamilySearch: Nowak](https://www.familysearch.org/en/surname?surname=nowak)
- FamilySearch, **Wiśniewska** (diacritic, feminine): returns the DAFN entry, "habitational name for someone from any of the many places in Poland called Wiśniewo, Wiśniew, or Wiśniewa, named with *wiśnia* 'cherry'", relates it to **Wiśniewski** and gives Americanised comparisons (Wisneski, Wisnewski, Wisnieski). Family Tree counts: Poland 488, Germany 13, US 13. 672,164 records. — [FamilySearch: Wiśniewska](https://www.familysearch.org/en/surname?surname=wi%C5%9Bniewska)
- Conflicting etymologies across dictionaries: DAFN (Hanks) derives Wiśniewski from places called Wiśniewo/Wiśniew/Wiśniewa. Smith's 1956 DAFN, as shown on Forebears, says "from Wisznia… in Poland". Neither cites Polish archival attestations. — [FamilySearch: Wiśniewska](https://www.familysearch.org/en/surname?surname=wi%C5%9Bniewska); [Forebears: Wisniewski](https://forebears.io/surnames/wisniewski)

### Inferences
- The "meaning" layer at the two largest genealogy brands is one licensed OUP text: a single sentence or two, no citations to Polish sources, no confidence signal. Any LLM trained on the open web has absorbed these texts or paraphrases of them. This is the most commoditised layer in the market.
- Neither gives current counts for Poland, regional distribution within Poland, gender-aware aggregation, or historical attestations. Their "distribution" answers "where are records/tree profiles?", not "how many people carry this name in Poland today, and where?".
- Two different works share the title "Dictionary of American Family Names" (Smith 1956; Hanks/OUP 2003/2022). Consumers see different etymologies under the same title. Name Atlas's CONFLICTING label and per-claim citation fit this directly.
- Ancestry's feminine-form failure matters. In the PESEL 2026 fixtures the -ska form is about half of all bearers for each -ski name checked (Kowalska 67,308 against Kowalski 66,589; Wiśniewska 54,177 against 53,079; Kamińska 46,723 against 45,818; Lewandowska 46,268 against 44,466). Diaspora users working from Polish documents will often have the -ska form.

### Gaps
- Who wrote the Polish entries in DAFN (1st and 2nd editions) was not confirmed. UNVERIFIED.
- The licence terms between OUP and Ancestry/FamilySearch (and whether OUP would license DAFN Polish entries to a third party) were not found. UNVERIFIED.
- Use of the *Oxford Dictionary of Family Names in Britain and Ireland* was not observed on any Polish example checked (Nowak, Kowalska, Wiśniewska). Its absence across all Polish names is not verified.

## 3. MyHeritage, House of Names, SurnameDB, Behind the Name, names.org, Geneanet, WikiTree/Geni: what each offers for Polish names and their weaknesses

### Takeaway
The long tail splits two ways. One group is commercial "heritage merchandise" (House of Names, SurnameDB), which sells surname-level coats of arms or scrolls and gets Polish origin wrong or treats it as Czech. The other is funnel content from the big platforms (MyHeritage wiki and "last-name" pages) that is generated at scale and lightly sourced. Behind the Name is accurate but minimal (one line plus IPA). I could not retrieve Geneanet, names.org, WikiTree or Geni in this session.

### Cited Findings
- **House of Names**, Nowak: "The name Nowak is a nickname or descriptive surname, formed from the Czech 'novy'". It sells a "Family Crest Download (JPG) Heritage Series – 600 DPI" (377 reviews) and PDF histories, marked "SALE". It lists seven sources, including the *New Dictionary of American Family Names* and passenger/crew lists. There is **no disclaimer** that coats of arms belong to individuals rather than surnames. — [House of Names: Nowak](https://www.houseofnames.com/nowak-family-crest)
- Heraldic commentary: "It is a popular misconception that there is a coat-of-arms for each surname and that everyone bearing that surname is entitled to use those arms." — [CAGenWeb, "There is NO SUCH THING as a Family Coat of Arms"](https://www.cagenweb.org/plumas/nosuchthing.htm) (surfaced via search, not fetched in full)
- **SurnameDB**, Nowak: "of Slavonic origin… recorded throughout Eastern Europe", from "Novak" 'the new one'. The earliest recording cited is **Czech**: "Jakub Novak… June 24th 1655, a christening witness, at Semcice, Mlade Boleslaw". It describes a coat of arms ("A blue field, charged with three gold crescents…", said to be granted in Galicia). One Polish example (Josepha Nowakowska, 1793) but **no Polish archival sources**. "© Copyright: Name Origin Research 1980–2024". It sells "surname scrolls" ("Buy 2 or more scrolls Get free shipping"). — [SurnameDB: Nowak](https://www.surnamedb.com/Surname/Nowak)
- **Behind the Name**, Nowak: "Polish cognate of Novak. This is the most common surname in Poland." Pronunciation /ˈnɔ.vak/, root novŭ, Slavic cognates (Novák, Novak, Novikov…). Last updated 21 January 2022. — [Behind the Name: Nowak](https://surnames.behindthename.com/name/nowak)
- **MyHeritage wiki**, "Polish American surnames": a named contributor (Alina Borisov-Rebel), with community contributions invited. Dated 3 July 2025 per its citation block. It opens with a Polish-ancestry population figure (8.6 million, attributed to the 2020 US Census). It gives Americanisation examples, including **Smoliński → Small**, Bankiewicz → Baker and Nowakowski → Novak, **without per-example sources**. It lists three references (Bukowczyk 1986; Brożek 1985; Lyra 1966). Calls to action: build a tree, search records, take a DNA test. — [MyHeritage wiki: Polish American surnames](https://www.myheritage.com/wiki/Polish_American_surnames)
- MyHeritage also runs "Polish surnames" and "**Polish nobility surnames**" wiki pages. — [MyHeritage wiki: Polish surnames](https://myheritage.com/wiki/Polish_surnames); [MyHeritage wiki: Polish nobility surnames](https://www.myheritage.com/wiki/Polish_nobility_surnames) (found via search; not fetched)
- MyHeritage runs a **lastnames.myheritage.com** subdomain ("Discover the story behind the surname") with pages for very rare Polish surnames, **including feminine forms** (e.g., gielarowska, libydziewicz, heywowski-poray), plus person-name pages (e.g., /names/izabela_bajkowska). — [lastnames.myheritage.com/last-name/gielarowska](https://lastnames.myheritage.com/last-name/gielarowska); [myheritage.com/names/izabela_bajkowska](https://www.myheritage.com/names/izabela_bajkowska). **Page content could not be retrieved** (it renders client-side, and the fetches came back empty), so what they contain and where it comes from is UNVERIFIED.
- **Geneanet** surname page: HTTP 403 to automated fetch. — [Geneanet: NOWAK](https://en.geneanet.org/surnames/NOWAK). Content UNVERIFIED.
- **names.org**: HTTP 403 to automated fetch. — [names.org: Nowak](https://www.names.org/n/nowak/about). Content UNVERIFIED.
- **WikiTree** surname index: empty fetch. — [WikiTree: NOWAK](https://www.wikitree.com/genealogy/NOWAK). Content UNVERIFIED. **Geni** was not checked. UNVERIFIED.

### Inferences
- House of Names and SurnameDB make money from the exact claim Name Atlas refuses to make (a surname-level coat of arms or noble descent). For Polish names they also frame the #1 Polish surname through Czech sources. Name Atlas can position "no invented heraldry" as a trust signal. How Polish herb (clan) arms relate to surnames would need its own evidenced explainer; it was not researched here and is UNVERIFIED.
- MyHeritage creating pages programmatically for rare, feminine Polish surnames suggests it is working the long-tail SEO space Name Atlas also targets. Whether those pages are AI-generated could not be verified. If they are, they are the direct commodity competitor for "meaning" queries.
- Behind the Name gives an IPA pronunciation, which the DAFN-based pages do not. Pronunciation is a real gap, but on the sites checked it is covered at best with one IPA string and no audio. names.org is known for user-submitted audio, but that is UNVERIFIED here.

### Gaps
- Geneanet, names.org, WikiTree and Geni content for Polish names was not observed (403, empty, or not checked).
- Whether MyHeritage's lastnames pages and wiki pages are AI-generated is UNVERIFIED.
- houseofnames.com prices, and its corporate relationship to the Dublin "House of Names" shop, were not verified.

## 4. Polish-diaspora-specific resources (PGSA/Hoffman, PolishRoots, PolishOrigins, Gesher Galicia, JewishGen) and professional research prices (willingness-to-pay benchmarks)

### Takeaway
The authoritative English-language Polish surname reference is still William F. Hoffman's *Polish Surnames: Origins and Meanings*, 3rd edition (2012), from PGSA. It sells as a $50 print set or a $30 download, with bearer counts **as of 2002**. I found no online database or licence programme for it. Paid help for the diaspora sits at a much higher price point: about $900 minimum at PolishOrigins (10 hours), $108/hour or more in 25–100 hour blocks at Legacy Tree, and about $5,000 heritage tours. Community memberships cost $50–100 a year. That leaves a large gap between a $30 book and a $900+ research engagement, which a versioned, evidence-backed surname report could occupy.

### Inferences

## 5. AI in genealogy 2024–2026: FamilySearch Full-Text Search/HTR (languages, Polish coverage), MyHeritage and Ancestry AI features, AI-first startups, LLM use for surname questions; implications for commoditisation and defensibility

### Takeaway
AI in the big platforms is aimed at reading records (HTR/full-text), storytelling and research nudges inside trees, not at surname-level reference content. FamilySearch Full-Text Search officially covered English, Spanish and Portuguese as of October 2025, with French, German, Dutch, Italian and Chinese planned. **Polish was not named as live or planned** in any primary source I found. According to a secondary source, Ancestry's AI Stories languages exclude Polish. MyHeritage Scribe AI (March 2026) is the only tool that plausibly handles Polish documents, by supporting "all languages supported on the MyHeritage website". I found no AI-first Polish surname product. LLM use for surname questions is widely taught and discussed, but I found no quantitative survey.

### Inferences
- **Commoditised or commoditising:** (a) one-paragraph "meaning" text, which is DAFN-derived, widely copied, and reproducible by any LLM or AI Overview; (b) "help me read this document", now free or cheap through MyHeritage Scribe AI and general LLMs; (c) generic research-strategy advice, which platform chatbots (FamilySearch's help chatbot, Ancestry's Get Ideas) will increasingly provide. Forebears' search-dependent traffic falling 17% in a month fits pressure on (a), but proves nothing on its own.
- **Not yet commoditised for Polish names:** (a) searchable text of Polish-territory records. FamilySearch's HTR language list did not include Polish in any primary source seen, and Latin/Russian/German-Kurrent registers from partitioned Poland are still hard. So cited historical attestations of a surname stay scarce. (b) Current official counts that are gender- and diacritic-aware, and regional distribution within Poland. (c) Evidence-graded statements where "we don't know" is an allowed answer, the opposite of how LLMs fail ("trying to please people").
- **Defensibility thesis:** Name Atlas's moat is not better prose about meaning. It is (1) lawful, dated, citable data that LLMs cannot invent, such as PESEL CC0 snapshots with m/f and regional detail; (2) claim-level provenance and confidence labels that let AI answer engines, and users, check the answer; (3) Polish-language scholarly and archival inputs that English-language incumbents do not integrate; and (4) surname-level research routing by partition, archive and record language. The main risk is being summarised by AI answers without a click, so free-tier pages need a reason to click through, such as a live official count with its snapshot date, a map, or a citation trail.
- If FamilySearch adds Polish, Latin or Russian HTR in 2026–2027, the attestation layer could open up quickly, both as an opportunity (more evidence through the partner route) and as a threat (FamilySearch surfacing attestations itself). This is worth watching.
