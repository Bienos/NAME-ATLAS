## 1. Defensibility patterns for data/reference products, and what made the analogues durable or not

### Takeaway
The analogues that lasted all hold a compounding, labour-intensive or contributor-fed dataset (Geneteka's 68M volunteer indexes, JRI-Poland's indexing agreement with the State Archives, Carfax's 100k+ reporting sources), or they were bought for their records (Filae/Géopatronyme → MyHeritage). Products built on a single snapshot with no refresh path went stale or disappeared (Geogen, 2002 phone book; verwandt.de, phone directories). Name Atlas's core free input (PESEL CC0) is non-exclusive by design, so defensibility has to come from the time series, the evidence graph and curation, institutional ties, and trust/citation brand. The raw counts give none of it.

### Cited Findings
**Frameworks (management research and VC opinion)**
- Hagiu & Wright (HBR, Jan–Feb 2020, "When Data Creates Competitive Advantage…And When It Doesn't"): the gains from data-enabled learning look like network effects but "are not as powerful or as enduring". A durable advantage needs two things: the value customer data adds must be high relative to the offering's standalone value, and the marginal value of more data must fall slowly. Most firms "grossly overestimate" the advantage data confers. [OPINION/academic analysis] — [BU Questrom summary](https://insights.bu.edu/when-data-creates-competitive-advantageand-when-it-doesnt/?amp=1); [O'Reilly listing of the HBR chapter](https://oreilly.com/library/view/hbrs-10-must/9781647820046/Text/ch07.html)
- Casado & Lauten (a16z, 9 May 2019, "The Empty Promise of Data Moats"): data scale effects are rarely a strong moat. The cost of adding *unique* data can rise while the value of each increment falls, and past a point new data only serves "long tail" cases. [OPINION] — [a16z](https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/)

**Institutional/expert curation and trust brand**

### Inferences
- **PESEL counts are table stakes, not a moat.** The Ministry, dane.gov.pl search tools and the media already republish them for free (see Q7). The durable patterns above suggest four ways to build defensibility:
  - (a) **Accumulated annual PESEL snapshots.** Each year's release only becomes a time series if someone archives it. It compounds and cannot be recreated after the fact if old versions are not kept.
  - (b) **Labour-intensive curation.** The Source→Claim→Citation evidence graph with confidence labels resembles ISNP's expert work, and Geneteka shows what years of accumulated indexing produce.
  - (c) **Institutional relationships** with IJP PAN/ISNP, PTG or the State Archives (the JRI-Poland model).
  - (d) **A trust/citation brand** among journalists and academics.
- **The autoDNA pattern maps directly onto the DiscoveryIndex:** a free, truthful inventory of *what exists* before paying.
- **ISNP is both the strongest free competitor and the most natural partner.** It is authoritative and free, but it stops at ~30k surnames, uses 2014 frequencies and shows coarse geography (see Q3). A rights-cleared collaboration or citation relationship would add trust that is hard to copy. Its licence terms were not checked here.
- **Contributor networks are durable only when contributions compound into a public good with a clear owner** (Geneteka/PTG, JRI-Poland). Hagiu & Wright and a16z warn that passive customer-data "network effects" are usually weak.
- **Exit value in this space has come from record collections** (Filae → MyHeritage). Owned or indexed data is what acquirers pay for.

### Gaps
- Terms of FamilySearch's filming/digitisation agreements with the Polish State Archives: no primary source found.
- Forebears' business model and data licensing; WorldNames' (worldnames.publicprofiler.org) current status; verwandt.de shutdown date; Genealogy Indexer's operator and permission model. None were verified.
- Whether dane.gov.pl keeps prior annual PESEL surname snapshots publicly: not researched here. This matters for how exclusive a time-series archive would be.
- Carfax's free record-check behaviour (showing record counts before purchase) was not verified.

## 2. AI-answer commoditisation: AI Overviews / AI Mode in Poland, click-through evidence, what keeps traffic, how sites get cited

### Takeaway
AI Overviews ("Przegląd od AI") launched in Poland on **26 March 2025** for Polish and English queries (logged-in users, 18+). AI Mode ("tryb AI") reached Poland in Polish around **7–8 October 2025**, and AI Overviews ran on Gemini 3 from January 2026. Measured studies agree that informational queries with an AI summary get far fewer clicks:
- Pew: 8% vs 15% of visits.
- Ahrefs: position-1 CTR about −58%.
- Seer: −61%.

Citation inside the answer correlates with better CTR but no longer tracks top-10 rankings (Ahrefs 2026: 38% vs 76%). The only measured hint that original data gets cited more comes from a small sample.

### Cited Findings
**Launch status in Poland**
- AI Overviews launched in Poland on 26 March 2025, alongside AT, BE, ES, IE, DE, PT, IT and CH. It covers Polish and English queries and requires a logged-in Google account and age 18+: "Przeglądy AI w Polsce dostępne są dla zapytań w języku polskim i angielskim"; "należy być zalogowanym na konto Google - funkcja dostępna jest jedynie dla użytkowników powyżej 18 roku życia." — [Spider's Web, 26 Mar 2025](https://spidersweb.pl/2025/03/google-przeglady-overviews-ai-w-polsce.html); [Benchmark.pl](https://www.benchmark.pl/aktualnosci/wyszukiwarka-google-z-ai-overviews.html)
- AI Mode: on 7 Oct 2025 Google announced expansion to ">35 new languages" and ">40 new countries and territories" (>200 in total). The blog post does **not** name Polish or Poland explicitly — [Google blog, 7 Oct 2025](https://blog.google/products/search/ai-mode-expands-languages-locations/). Polish media reported "tryb AI" arriving in Poland on 8 Oct 2025 and working in Polish. — [Spider's Web, Oct 2025](https://spidersweb.pl/2025/10/wyszukiwarka-google-tryb-ai-w-polsce-udostepniony.html); [Benchmark.pl](https://www.benchmark.pl/aktualnosci/google-ai-mode-w-polsce.html)
- AI Overviews were "powered by Gemini 3" as of January 2026. — [Ahrefs, 2 Mar 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/)

**Click-through studies (all measured; none Poland-specific)**
- **Pew Research Center (22 Jul 2025)** [MEASURED; US panel of 900 adults, March 2025, 68,879 searches]:
  - 18% of searches produced an AI summary.
  - Users clicked a traditional result on **8%** of visits with an AI summary vs **15%** without. They clicked a link *inside* the summary on **1%** of visits.
  - Sessions ended on 26% vs 16% of pages.
  - AI summaries appeared for **8% of 1–2-word searches**, 36% of full-sentence searches, 53% of 10+-word searches and **60% of question searches**. 88% cited ≥3 sources.
  - Wikipedia, YouTube and Reddit made up 15% of AI-summary sources vs 17% of standard-result sources. .gov sites made up 6% vs 2%.
  - [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)
- **Ahrefs update** [MEASURED; 300k keywords, aggregated Google Search Console data, desktop only, Dec 2023 vs Dec 2025; correlational]:
  - Position-1 CTR with an AI Overview was 0.016 vs a forecast of 0.037 without one, "~58%" lower. The April 2025 study had found −34.5%.
  - The effect shrinks down the page, from −58.0% at position 1 to −19.4% at position 10.
  - Position-1 CTR on informational keywords **without** an AI Overview also fell, from 0.076 to 0.039.
  - [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/)
- **Seer Interactive (Sept 2025 update)** [MEASURED; 3,119 informational queries, 42 organisations, 25.1M organic impressions, Jun 2024–Sep 2025; correlational]:
  - Organic CTR on queries with an AI Overview fell from 1.76% to **0.61% (−61%)**. Queries without one fell from 2.74% to 1.62% (−41%).
  - Paid CTR on queries with an AI Overview fell from 19.70% to 6.34% (−68%).
  - Brands *cited* in the AI Overview got **0.70% vs 0.52%** organic CTR (+35%) and 7.89% vs 4.14% paid CTR (+91%).
  - Authors' caveat: "We cannot definitively prove that citation *causes* higher CTRs."
  - [Seer](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update)
- **Similarweb (July 2025)** [MEASURED panel; SECONDARY reporting]:
  - Zero-click share for news-related searches rose from **56% (May 2024) to 69% (May 2025)**.
  - Organic visits to news sites fell from >2.3 billion (mid-2024) to <1.7 billion (May 2025).
  - ChatGPT referrals to news publishers grew 25× year on year but did not offset the loss.
  - [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/similarweb-google-zero-click-search-growth-39706.html); [Yahoo/NY Post report](https://www.yahoo.com/news/google-ai-summaries-increase-frequency-223953043.html); [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/in-graphic-detail-ai-platforms-are-driving-more-traffic-but-not-enough-to-offset-zero-click-search/)

**How sites get cited**
- **Ahrefs (2 Mar 2026)** [MEASURED; 863k SERPs, 4M AI Overview URLs]:
  - **37.9%** of cited URLs rank in the top 10, down from ~76% in its July 2025 study. 31.2% rank 11–100 and 31.0% rank beyond 100.
  - Ahrefs' explanation is a *hypothesis*: "query fan-out" with Gemini 3.
  - YouTube is the most-cited domain.
  - [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/)
- **Search Engine Land** [MEASURED, small sample; UNVERIFIED at page level, HTTP 403]: 301 AI-cited pages carried 1,075 citations across 316 prompts (Gauge data). Only 8 pages (2.7%) were primary research, yet they earned 90 citations (8.4%): 11.3 citations per page vs 3.4, i.e. 3.3×. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/why-most-original-data-never-gets-cited-481676)
- **Google Search Central** (page updated 2025-12-10) [COMPANY CLAIM]:
  - "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." A page must be indexed and snippet-eligible.
  - Site owners can use `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `max-snippet` or `noindex` to limit visibility.
  - AI-feature traffic is folded into the Search Console "Web" totals and is not reported separately.
  - Google says clicks from results pages with AI Overviews "are higher quality".
  - [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

**Content that "keeps traffic": opinion only**
- SEO teams shifted towards "interactive tools, templates, calculators, proprietary data", and Google has started bringing interactive tools into AI search itself. [OPINION/industry practice] — [Ahrefs blog (original study page)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks/)
- 2026 blog claims such as "informational −55–61% vs branded −8–13%", "AI Overviews on 67% of commercial queries" and "4.79% of branded keywords trigger AI Overviews, +18.68% CTR" have unclear methods. **UNVERIFIED; do not rely on them.** — [Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/ai-overviews-traffic-impact-2026)

### Inferences
- **Surname lookups split by query shape.** Bare 1–2-word queries ("Nowak", "nazwisko Wójcik") fall in the band where Pew saw AI summaries only 8% of the time. Question-form queries ("skąd pochodzi nazwisko…", "co oznacza nazwisko…") fall in the 60% band. The meaning/origin content Name Atlas plans is therefore the most exposed to zero-click answers. This rests on US English data; Polish trigger rates are unknown.
- **Plan acquisition on much lower CTRs.** Even informational SERPs without AI Overviews roughly halved in CTR in 2023–2025 (Ahrefs), so historical SEO benchmarks overstate what Name Atlas can expect.
- **Being the primary, citable source matters more than ranking.** With top-10 overlap at 38%, the plausible (unproven) lever is to be the source a claim comes from: per-surname numbers, year-over-year changes and rarity ranks, each with a visible method and citation. Name Atlas's claim→citation structure fits that pattern.
- **Uncertainty as a differentiator is untested.** Name Atlas's "we don't know" and no-LLM-etymology stance separates it from AI answers that may produce confident etymologies. No measured evidence was found that users reward this.
- **Dynamic, personalised and interactive outputs are less substitutable by a static AI summary.** Examples: comparisons, trend charts from successive PESEL snapshots, and the DiscoveryIndex inventory. This is opinion-grade, not measured.

### Gaps
- No Poland-specific or Polish-language CTR study was found. AI Overview trigger rates for Polish surname queries are unknown.
- Adoption of AI Mode in Poland, and whether it has login or age limits there, was not verified.
- No measured study shows that interactive tools or calculators keep clicks; only practitioner opinion exists.
- The exact publication date of the Ahrefs "58%" update was not captured (its dataset runs to Dec 2025).

## 3. Demand signals in Polish and diaspora markets (keyword volumes, trends, unmet needs)

### Takeaway
No published Polish keyword volumes were found. The strongest public demand proxy is the IJP PAN surname dictionary (ISNP): **nearly 1M users in 2022** and 570k in 2023, about a third abroad in 2022. Traffic spikes follow media coverage, and few users return (14%). Hundreds of user emails spell out the unmet needs:
- coverage of their own (often rare) surname
- origin and meaning
- where in Poland the name is common, at gmina level
- first attestation
- up-to-date counts
- noble-status claims

Diaspora forums add Americanised→Polish spelling reconstruction.

### Cited Findings
**ISNP usage statistics** [MEASURED, reported by the dictionary's authors] — [Górny 2024, Onomastica](https://onomastica.ijppan.pl/index.php/ONOM/article/view/533/730):
- **2022:** "liczba użytkowników słownika zbliżyła się do miliona" (nearly one million users); "ok. 14% osób powracało" (about 14% returned); ">4,5 mln odsłon" (over 4.5 million page views); 3–4 pages per session; about 3 minutes average search time. About **67% of users were in Poland**; the rest came mainly from the UK, US, Germany, France and Canada.
- **2023:** **570k users (505k in Poland)**; 2.7M page entries; about 2 minutes engagement; peaks in July and August. Users also came from Germany, the UK, the Netherlands, the US, Norway and Sweden.
- **Q4 2023:** 132k users, 642k views. **December 2023:** 50k users, 240k views.
- **Peak days:** 13 Nov 2022 (~86k users, >266k pages) and 27 Dec 2022 (~60k users). An Interia piece, "Skąd się wzięło twoje nazwisko? Łatwo to sprawdzisz", ran on 27 Dec 2022.
- **Baseline:** about 49k users a month, 19k a week and 5k a day.
- **Most-viewed entries in 2023:** Nowak, Abel, Kowalski, Abramowicz, Lewandowski, Wójcik. In late 2023 Abraham and Tusk also appeared.
- **Media drivers:** a Magda Mołek YouTube interview with Michał Rusinek (Feb 2022), PAP "Nauka w Polsce" (26 Dec 2022) and Radio Kraków (14 Apr 2023).

**ISNP user emails** — the same source: hundreds of emails since October 2022, from Poland, the US, Canada, Brazil, Australia and the Netherlands. Recurring requests:
- explain the origin of *my* surname, or add it ("nie ma mojego nazwiska w bazie", "my surname isn't in your database"); many writers have rare surnames
- "kiedy i gdzie po raz pierwszy pojawiło się moje nazwisko" (when and where my surname first appeared)
- "na jakich terenach Polski jest popularne" (which parts of Poland it is common in)
- "Dlaczego nie zrobicie listy najmniej popularnych nazwisk?" (why not publish a list of the rarest surnames?)
- "Czy można u Was zamówić drzewo genealogiczne mojej rodziny?" (can I order my family tree from you?)
- "Skąd pochodzi moje nazwisko i kim byli moi przodkowie" (where does my surname come from and who were my ancestors)
- requests to update frequency and geography data, with complaints about missing powiats or gminas. Some writers count their own family members: "Jest nas dziesięcioro… W danych statystycznych podana jest liczba 9. Kogo pominięto?" (There are ten of us… the statistics say 9. Who was left out?)
- arguments from "polskość", "szlacheckość" (nobility) or descent from a famous hetman for why their name should be included

### Inferences
- **Audience size.** A single free academic dictionary with incomplete coverage drew 0.5–1M users a year. That suggests a sizeable, spiky, one-off curiosity market that is mostly Polish (67–89%) with a meaningful diaspora tail (UK, US, DE, NL, NO, SE, CA).
- **The low return rate (~14%) favours one-time purchases** over subscriptions, which matches the Name Atlas model.
- **Unmet needs Name Atlas can meet from its planned data:**
  - (1) *every* surname, not just the top 30k: PESEL lists ~380k male and ~400k female forms (see Q7)
  - (2) current annual counts rather than 2014
  - (3) finer geography, subject to the privacy limits in Q6
  - (4) an explicit "we don't know yet" for unresearched surnames, since ISNP's gaps caused frustration
  - (5) gentle, evidence-based correction of noble-descent assumptions
  - (6) guidance on which archive holds records for a region, without matching records to individuals
- **High-ranking "Abel", "Abramowicz" and "Abraham" in ISNP statistics may be an alphabetical-list artefact.** This is a speculative inference, not established.
- **Users treat counts personally** ("there are ten of us"), so how precision, suppression and snapshot dates are explained will drive trust.

## 5. B2B angles: Polish name matching and normalisation (KYC/AML, identity resolution, CRM de-duplication), and buyers of surname-statistics data

## 6. Consented user-contributed data ("my family came from region X") as a data network effect: GDPR constraints

### Takeaway
The regulatory ground moved in 2025–2026:
- The EDPB adopted draft **Guidelines 02/2026 on Anonymisation** on 7 July 2026 (consultation until 30 Oct 2026). They set a relative, entity-specific test built on record isolation, linkage and inference. Anonymisation is itself processing, which needs a legal basis and, for special-category data, an Art. 9 condition.
- CJEU C-184/20 means data that *indirectly* reveal special categories, such as ethnic origin inferred by cross-referencing, get Art. 9 protection.
- CJEU C-413/23 P (SRB, 4 Sept 2025) confirms a context-dependent view of identifiability.
- UODO's DPIA list (M.P. 2019 poz. 666) generally requires a DPIA when two or more criteria apply.

No numeric aggregation threshold (a minimum cell count) was found in the sources reviewed. These are cited sources, not legal conclusions.

### Cited Findings
**EDPB draft Guidelines 02/2026 on Anonymisation**
- Adopted 7 July 2026; open for feedback 8 July to **30 October 2026**. They update WP29 Opinion 05/2014. — [EDPB consultation page](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/public-consultations/guidelines-022026-on-anonymisation_en); [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/08/eu-edpb-publishes-draft-guidelines-on-anonymisation/); [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/07/27/edpb-publishes-draft-guidelines-on-anonymisation/)
- Key positions, per Covington's summary:
  - identifiability "may vary depending on the entity assessing the data"
  - the test is means "reasonably likely to be used", and "contractual restrictions should not be treated as equivalent to legal prohibitions"
  - "if the data are personal data for the controller, they are personal data for the processor"
  - anonymising requires an Art. 6 legal basis, plus an Art. 9 derogation for special-category data where it serves a different purpose
  - three criteria: **Record Isolation, Linkage, Inference**
  - a warning that "contextual assessments may falsely conclude that a dataset is anonymous"
  - [Covington](https://www.covingtonblogs.com/2026/08/04/edpb-publishes-draft-guidelines-on-anonymisation/)

**EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 on Pseudonymisation**
- Published 16 Jan 2025; consultation to 14 Mar 2025. Pseudonymised data remain personal data. Pseudonymisation can support reliance on legitimate interests. — [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/documents/public-consultations/2025/guidelines-012025-pseudonymisation_es); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/european-data-regulators-release-updated-pseudonymisation-guidelines-for-2025/)

**CJEU case law**
- **C-413/23 P EDPS v SRB (4 Sep 2025):** pseudonymised data may fall outside "personal data" for a recipient who cannot reasonably re-identify. Transparency duties are assessed from the controller's viewpoint at collection. — [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2025/09/pseudonymized-data-after-edps-v-srb.html); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/cjeu-clarifies-concept-of-personal-data-for-a-transfer-of-pseudonymised-data-to-third-parties)
- **C-184/20 OT v Vyriausioji tarnybinės etikos komisija (1 Aug 2022):** personal data "liable to disclose indirectly" special categories "following an intellectual operation involving deduction or cross-referencing" need Art. 9's strengthened protection. In the case, a spouse's name could reveal sexual orientation. — [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/eu-data-protection/special-category-data-by-inference-cjeu-significantly-expands-the-scope-of-article-9-gdpr/); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/processing-of-personal-data-that-may-indirectly-reveal-sensitive-information-on-the-basis-of-a-legal-obligation-the-cjeu-draws-the-contours)

**UODO (Poland)**
- The DPIA list is attached to the Communication of the President of UODO of 17 June 2019 (Monitor Polski 2019 poz. 666). It has 12 categories; as a rule, meeting **≥2 criteria** requires a DPIA. — [ELI: M.P. 2019 poz. 666 (PDF)](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/MP/2019/666/text/O/M20190666.pdf); [Rzeczpospolita](https://pro.rp.pl/prawo-w-firmie/art9282251-jest-nowy-wykaz-operacji-przetwarzania-danych-ktore-wymagaja-oceny-skutkow)
- No dedicated UODO anonymisation guideline was found. Searches returned breach-notification guides instead. — [UODO bulletin](https://nowybiuletyn.uodo.gov.pl/poradnik-dotyczacy-naruszen-ochrony-danych-osobowych-stare-czy-nowe-podejscie-uodo/?pdf=1433)

**Evidence that surnames act as ethnicity proxies in practice**
- OnoMAP (language, religion, region and culture classes), used by Public Health Wales. — [Public Profiler](https://gbnames.publicprofiler.org/resources.php); [UK COVID-19 Inquiry exhibit](https://covid19.public-inquiry.uk/pl/documents/inq000224048-report-from-public-health-wales-individuals-titled-technical-report-rapid-analysis-of-ethnic-variation-in-covid-19-outcomes-in-wales-using-onomap-a-name-based-ethnicity-clas)
- Namsor markets ethnicity inference from names. — [GenderPortal](https://genderportal.eu/node/1545)

**Spontaneous user disclosure**
- ISNP correspondents volunteer family headcounts by gmina, for example "W gminie Jemielno mieszkają 3 osoby" (three people live in Jemielno gmina). — [Górny 2024](https://onomastica.ijppan.pl/index.php/ONOM/article/view/533/730)

**GDPR text** (Arts. 4(1), 6, 7, 9, 35) — [EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)

### Inferences
These are risk observations, not legal conclusions.
- **A contribution tied to an account is personal data.** "My family came from region X", linked to a user and a surname, is personal data. If the region or community correlates with an ethnic or religious group (for example borderland, Lemko, German, Jewish or Ukrainian communities), C-184/20 suggests it may count as special-category data by inference.
- **That points to explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a)), a DPIA and strict separation of domains.** The DPIA follows because the processing plausibly meets two or more UODO criteria. The separation of public surname cache and private family data is already an invariant.
- **Surname-level aggregates of contributions are the network-effect asset, and also the risk.** For rare surnames (single-digit bearers, see the ISNP emails), a "families of surname S came from region X" aggregate can single out a household. Under the EDPB draft's record-isolation, linkage and inference criteria, a fixed k-threshold alone may not suffice: combining it with PESEL gmina counts could enable linkage.
- **The draft could change.** Because the guidelines are under consultation until 30 Oct 2026, design to the draft and track the final text.
- **The data network effect itself is weak by Hagiu & Wright's test.** Each extra "region X" report adds diminishing value for common surnames and scarce data for rare ones. The durable value is more likely the *curated, evidence-linked* surname record than raw crowd claims.

### Gaps
- The EDPB 02/2026 PDF itself was not read, so any worked examples on aggregated statistics, small cells or minimum counts are not captured.
- UODO-specific anonymisation or aggregation guidance was not found.
- No source was found on whether the dane.gov.pl PESEL surname dataset applies a minimum-count suppression rule.
- National statistics disclosure-control thresholds (GUS/Eurostat) were not researched.
- EDPB consent guidelines (05/2020) were not reviewed in this pass.

  - rising and falling forms
  - rare-name counts
  - the gender-form split
  - regional shifts, as far as the dataset allows
